## 1. Features, screens and flows (with acceptance criteria)

### 1.1 Medication setup and the medication plan

- **Setup inputs per medicine.** The user enters seven things: exact product, brand and strength ("Vyvanse 50 mg capsule"); usual dose time ("On waking" or 7:45); their own experience ("Kicks in after about 45 min, good for about 7 hours"); prescribed booster, if any ("Ritalin 10, usually early afternoon"); latest-dose cut-off ("2:00 pm", set by the user or their prescriber); planned medication-free days ("Weekends", or "sometimes"); desired productive activities ("Essay drafting, case reading, tutorial prep"). AC: the user enters every value. The app never proposes a dose, dose time, booster or cut-off. [p24]
- **Product picks the day-one curve.** The exact product selects its template curve from the per-product library (PDF section 6), and that curve is the label-data starting point. AC: with no stated experience, the day-one window is the product template. With stated experience, the stated values dominate (next bullets). [p24]
- **A brand or generic change is a new product.** AC: switching brand saves a separate product with its own learned curve and reopens learning for about 1 to 2 weeks. This includes a shortage-driven switch and a move to a generic. [p21, p23, p24]
- **Usual dose time.** This is only the reminder time the user set, and a default until real logs arrive. AC: once logs exist, the plan anchors on the logged dose time. [p24]
- **Stated experience is the starting estimate.** Stated onset and duration are weighted above label data, as a prior worth about 3 days of check-ins. AC: with 0 check-ins, the focus pattern card shows the stated values ("Starting estimate from what you told us: focus about 0:45 to 7:00 after logging"). [p24, p26]
- **Latest-dose cut-off.** It is stored per medicine and shown only as a neutral note when a dose is logged after it. AC: the cut-off never generates a notification, reminder or plan change. [p24, p27]
- **Planned medication-free days.** These days plan from routine and energy. AC: they never count as missed, they are excluded from adherence and from learning, and no missed-dose flow runs on them. [p24, p28]
- **Desired productive activities.** They are tagged as deep work and reserved inside the window first. AC: on a medicated day they go into the Peak zone before other flexible work. [p24, p26]
- **Prescribed booster.** A booster is recorded as part of the prescribed plan (product, usual time). A booster is, by definition, a smaller immediate-release dose that a prescriber has authorised. AC: the plan ignores the booster until it is logged. The app never prompts or suggests a booster, and never shortens a task list to make one seem necessary. [p22, p24]
- **Direct window edit.** The user can set their window at any time in plain words ("it lasts about 6 hours for me"). AC: a manual edit is a hard override that the planner uses until the user explicitly releases it. [p24]
- **"About this medicine" card.** Each product has a card the user opens themselves, showing fixed stored label text. Examples: methylphenidate IR, last dose before 6 pm; Australian dexamfetamine leaflet, a missed dose no later than 6 hours before bedtime; Vyvanse leaflet, avoid afternoon or evening. AC: plans and notifications never read this text, and it never generates timing. [p22]
- **Non-stimulant regimen.** For atomoxetine, guanfacine and clonidine, the app logs doses, keeps adherence and supply features, and plans the day from routine and energy check-ins. AC: no focus window is learned or shown for these products. [p23]
- **Onboarding statements.** Onboarding states that the app is for medication taken as prescribed. It also shows "not medical advice; follow your prescription and check with your doctor or pharmacist". AC: the app never asks for proof of prescription. [p30, p32]
- **Single-person use only.** AC: there is no way to log for someone else, and no sharing or gifting feature. [p30]

### 1.2 "I took my medication" (the dose-log event)

- **One-tap log from everywhere.** Logging works from Apple Watch, Control Center, the Action button, a widget, Siri and the NFC tag, as well as in the app. AC: every entry point creates the same dose event and triggers the same replan. [p24]
- **Backdating.** It accepts an absolute time ("took it at 7:40") or a relative one ("about 20 minutes ago"). AC: the event stores the stated taken time separately from the time of logging. [p24]
- **"Ate first?"** An optional one-tap question on the log. AC: a yes shifts the day's expected onset for products with a known food effect (section 4). [p21, p24]
- **Duplicate guard.** If the same medicine was logged within the user's minimum interval, the app shows the earlier log with its time and requires a deliberate second confirm, in neutral words (never "safe to take"). AC: a second same-medicine log inside the interval cannot complete with a single tap. [p24, p30]
- **Taken means tapped.** AC: snooze and dismiss never mark a dose as taken. Only an explicit "taken" tap does, including from a notification. [p24, p30]
- **Apple Health import check.** If the user also logs in Apple Health's Medications, the app reads those dose events (Health is read-only for third parties). It de-duplicates them against its own log by time and product. AC: a dose recorded in both places appears once. [p25]
- **Live Activity.** It starts on log with "Logged 8:10 · usual focus 9:10 to 5:10" and mirrors to the Watch Smart Stack and the Mac menu bar. AC: one Live Activity lasts 8 hours active and 12 visible, so a second is chained near the 8-hour mark and a long-acting dose stays covered. [p25]
- **Replan.** AC: a log runs the core day-planning flow (original inventory section 3), with the learned window replacing the fixed T to T+9h. [p25]
- **Explain.** AC: after replanning, one line lists each moved item with its new time and states whether anything conflicts. Example: "Essay moved to 9:15. Lunch 1:10. Gym 5:30, after your focus window. Nothing conflicts." [p25]
- **Booster log.** AC: logging a booster adds the booster's own curve on top of the fading morning curve, which extends the window. [p22]
- **Undo, edit, delete.** AC: undo is instant after logging, any log can be edited or deleted, and every change is written to a local audit trail. [p30]

### 1.3 Check-ins and optional measures

- **One-question check-in.** "Working now? Yes / Partly / No" appears on the Watch or the Lock Screen. AC: each prompt asks one question, never a questionnaire. [p25, p26]
- **Learning phase.** For the first 5 to 7 medicated days, there are 3 to 5 prompts a day. Each is timed where the model is least sure, usually around the onset and wear-off edges. [p25]
- **Taper.** Prompts drop to about one every 2 to 3 days once the 80% interval on both edges is within ±30 minutes. [p25]
- **Relearn triggers.** Learning reopens (learning-phase cadence) on a dose, product or brand change, or when change detection fires. [p26]
- **Optional objective probe.** A 30 to 60 second arithmetic or reaction task, modelled on PERMP (the lab measure with the least placebo response), for users who want better data. AC: off by default. [p26]
- **Optional rebound check.** A user-enabled "irritable or flat right now?" at the end of the window. AC: off by default and not part of the default model. [p22]

### 1.4 Focus pattern display

- **Under 3 check-in days.** Shows "Starting estimate from what you told us: focus about 0:45 to 7:00 after logging". [p26]
- **3 to 7 days.** Shows "Early estimate (5 days): 0:50 to 6:20, ±50 min". [p26]
- **8+ days with interval under ±30 min.** Shows "Your usual focus: 0:55 to 6:30 after logging (23 check-ins, ±25 min)". [p26]
- **Stated and learned disagree.** Prompt: "You said about 7 hours; your check-ins suggest about 5.5. Use 5.5 for planning?" It has three actions: Accept, Keep mine, Ask later. [p26]
- **Framing.** Times are relative to the logged dose ("after logging"). AC: the label is "your focus pattern from your check-ins", never "when your medication wears off". [p26, p29]

### 1.5 Planning the day around the window

- **Focus demand on every flexible task.** Each one is deep, medium, light or body. AC: the solver fills window zones by demand. [p26]
- **Zone: before the dose and kicking in.** Shower, breakfast, commute, a warm-up micro-task. It is a safe on-ramp that also defuses waiting mode. [p26]
- **Zone: Peak (highest probability of "working").** The user's chosen deep work, longest blocks first. [p26]
- **Zone: Fading.** Medium tasks, then light ones: admin, email, planning tomorrow. [p26]
- **Zone: outside the window.** Gym, errands, social plans, dinner, evening routines. Lunch follows the user's own meal rule and often falls inside the window. [p27]
- **Capacity shortfall.** AC: if the window cannot hold the chosen work, the app changes tasks (split, defer, shrink to the baseline day, flag the deadline risk) and never medication. [p27]
- **Worked example as a golden acceptance test.** Learned window 1:00 to 9:00 after logging, with a personal rule of lunch no earlier than 5 h after the dose: [p27]
  - 7:30, Before: app-owned wake alarm dismissed; dose reminder. Reason shown: "Reminder time the user set, anchored to wake". [p27]
  - 8:10, Logged: the Live Activity starts and the day replans. Reason: dose event. [p27]
  - 8:10 to 9:10, Kicking in: shower, breakfast, commute. Reason: on-ramp. [p27]
  - 9:15 to 12:15, Peak: essay drafting, 2 × 80 min with a break. Reason: deep work, longest free block. [p27]
  - 13:10 to 13:50, Peak: lunch. Reason: personal rule, at least 5 h after dose. [p27]
  - 13:50 to 15:20, Peak: case reading, 90 min. Reason: next deep task. [p27]
  - 15:20 to 17:10, Fading: tutorial admin, email, tomorrow's plan. Reason: medium and light work. [p27]
  - 16:40: landing alert "wrap up, snack". Reason: 30 min before usual end. [p27]
  - 17:30 onward, Outside: gym, dinner, evening routine. Reason: body and social tasks. [p27]
- **Reasons on blocks.** Every scheduled block carries a short "reason shown" string (as in the worked example). [p27]
- **Late log.** The window simply shifts. AC: if the projected end passes the user's own cut-off, the app shows the stored note "Your prescriber or pharmacist can advise how late is appropriate" and nothing more. [p27]
- **No log.** One reminder at the time the user set. If there is still no log, the app offers "Plan today without medication?", which switches to a lower-demand baseline day. AC: no further nudge to take a dose. [p28]
- **Planned break days.** They use the same unmedicated plan and are excluded from adherence and from learning. [p28]
- **Travel and time zones.** On a time-zone change, the app asks whether reminders follow home time or local time. It suggests confirming a travel plan with the prescriber before the trip. AC: the app never picks a dose time. [p28]
- **Sleep debt.** Short sleep lowers the day's planned load, even when check-ins say "working". [p21]

### 1.6 Support, trust and consent

- **Get support.** A static, non-AI screen with deterministic crisis routing: Lifeline 13 11 14 and 000 in Australia, 988 in the US. AC: the content never depends on model output. [p30]
- **Age.** Launch is 18+ only. [p30, p32]
- **Supply tracker.** Neutral: quantity left and next script only. AC: no auto-refill flow, no "study hack" framing. [p32]
- **Export.** The user can export their log "to show your doctor if you choose". [p31]
- **Third-party data consent.** The app clearly discloses where personal data will be shared with third parties, including third-party AI, and gets explicit permission before sharing. [p32]
- **AI transparency.** Users are told when they are talking to AI. [p29]

### 1.7 AI-assisted features (bounded)

- **Screenshots and messages into tasks.** Parsed content fills fixed fields only (title, time, place). AC: every calendar write from parsed content needs a confirm, and parsed content can never create or change a medication entry or reminder. [p31, p32]
- **Medication questions.** AC: any medication question returns the fixed referral "That's one for your prescriber or pharmacist." [p31, p32]
- **Reflective follow-up.** The LLM turns a vent ("that was stupid") into a targeted question ("Was the problem research after gym, or the block being too short?"). [p37]
- **Explanations.** The LLM phrases explanations from numbers the deterministic code produced. AC: the LLM never computes a number. [p37]

### 1.8 Learning transparency and control

- **Duration estimate helper.** While the user estimates, the app shows logged past actuals, e.g. "Your last 6 essay-drafting sessions ran 1.4 to 2.3× your guess". AC: only logged actuals, never recalled ones. [p34]
- **Floating buffer block.** One floating buffer block per day, sized as the P80 day total minus the sum of the median blocks. [p34]
- **Reason code prompt.** One tap, shown only when something lapses, with 5 codes: blocked by a rule or event; chose not to or couldn't start; started then stopped; did it but didn't log; no answer. [p35]
- **"Did it but didn't log".** Counts as a success and asks for the real duration once. [p35]
- **"What the app has learned" screen.** Lists every learned value with its evidence count, range and last change, and a reset button for each. [p35]
- **Evidence labels.** "Typical for people like you: ×1.4" (fewer than 3 observations); "Early estimate (n = 5)" with a wide range (3 to 7); "Learned" (8+ and range narrower than ×1.5). [p35]
- **Proposed standing rules** (e.g. "move reading to mornings"). Shown as a suggestion with its evidence. AC: applied only after the user approves. A rejected proposal is shelved for 30 days. [p35]
- **N-of-1 experiments** (e.g. "90 vs 60-minute blocks"). They run and are adopted only with the user's yes. [p36]
- **Change notices.** Every detected change is explained and asked about, e.g. "Since Tuesday your wake time averages 9:10, up from 7:20. Update your routine?" [p36]
- **Protected flag.** The user can mark any item as protected. AC: protected items are never used for exploration. [p36]

### 1.9 Lifestyles (the Lifestyle 2 toggle) and modes

- **Lifestyle 2.** A second, separately learned version of the user's routine. The toggle changes which version the planner reads. AC: switching back is instant and nothing is forgotten. [p37]
- **Figure, "Lifestyle switching · 2 lifestyles, 1 shared layer".** The Planner reads the active lifestyle plus a shared layer. Lifestyle 1 "Term" is active (wake about 7:15, classes, study mornings) and keeps learning every day it is active. Lifestyle 2 "Holidays" is dormant: a frozen snapshot with no updates and no decay, ready the moment you switch back. One tap switches between them. Each lifestyle is the shared values plus its own routine. [p37]
- **Toggle placement.** Control Center, a widget, and the top of the Today screen. [p40]
- **Renaming.** Lifestyles can be renamed ("Term" and "Holidays" read better than "Lifestyle 1 and 2"). [p40]
- **Preview before applying.** Tapping the toggle shows a one-screen preview, e.g. "Wake anchor 9:30 instead of 7:15 · study blocks move to afternoons · 6 term-only routines paused." [p40]
- **Pause, never delete.** On a switch, term-only routines and app-created blocks are paused, never deleted, and external calendar events like classes are left alone. AC: undo is one tap. [p40]
- **First switch-on of Lifestyle 2.** Three questions, under a minute: roughly when will you wake; any fixed commitments (work shifts, a course, travel); what do you want this period for (rest, work, a project, catch-up). Each answer becomes a stated starting point. [p38]
- **Plan lightly while unsure.** Early Lifestyle 2 days lean on "anytime today" tasks and the baseline day rather than tight time blocks, and tighten as confidence grows. [p38]
- **Scheduled switch.** The user enters session dates once ("Lifestyle 2 from 28 Nov to 22 Feb"). The app switches on those dates, with a heads-up the evening before, and the toggle still works at any time. [p39]
- **Forgotten-toggle suggestion.** Example: "Last 3 days: up at 9:40 (term average 7:15), no classes. Switch to Holidays? Your settings from July are ready." AC: always a suggestion, never an automatic flip, with one tap to accept or dismiss. [p39]
- **Late switch.** "Switch since Monday" reassigns the logged days since Monday to Lifestyle 2 by replaying the raw events. [p40]
- **Switching back.** Lifestyle 1 is exactly as it was on the day of the switch. The first 3 to 5 days back carry extra uncertainty and a lighter plan. An optional "back to term" ramp moves the app's own wake alarm earlier in small steps across the last few days of the break. [p39]
- **Next holidays.** The next switch to Lifestyle 2 restores last time's snapshot, with its confidence reduced. [p39]
- **Cap and merge.** Cap named lifestyles at about 4. If two turn out statistically near-identical, the app suggests merging them. [p40]
- **Modes: Exam, Sick day, Travel, Assessment crunch.** A mode is a temporary rule overlay that changes priorities and rules. It does not learn, lasts days to a few weeks, and stacks on whichever lifestyle is active. Only one lifestyle is active at a time. Lifestyles (Term, Holidays, Placement, Night shifts) last weeks to months and learn their own anchors and timing. [p40]
- **Short breaks.** A one-week mid-semester break does not create a new lifestyle. With under 3 days of evidence, the app stays close to the prior and plans loosely. [p40]
- **Mixed periods.** Shifts during holidays are fixed events inside Lifestyle 2. The lifestyle is about the anchors, not the calendar. [p40]

## 2. Data model (entities, fields, types, retention)

- **MedicationProduct.** id (UUID); name, brand and strength (e.g. "Vyvanse 50 mg capsule"); active ingredient (methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine, dexamfetamine, amphetamine, atomoxetine, guanfacine, clonidine); formulation (IR, XR, OROS, bead; fixes ramp lengths and food effect); class (stimulant or non-stimulant; non-stimulants have no daily window); templateCurve (the day-one curve); aboutThisMedicineText (fixed label text, String). A brand or generic change creates a new record. [p21, p22, p23, p24, p25]
- **MedicationPlanItem** (the user's prescribed plan). productId; role (morning or booster); usualDoseTime (enum onWaking, or a clock time); statedOnsetMinutes (Int, e.g. 45); statedDurationHours (Double, e.g. 7); latestDoseCutoff (time, e.g. 14:00) with setBy (user or prescriber); medicationFreeDays (weekday set or "sometimes"); minimumInterval (duration, drives the duplicate guard); reminderTime (user-set only). [p22, p24]
- **DoseEvent.** id; productId; takenAt (Date, backdatable); loggedAt (Date); source (watch, controlCenter, actionButton, widget, siri, nfc, app, healthImport); ateFirst (Bool, optional); isBooster (Bool); dose amount (a logged change reopens learning); duplicateConfirmed (Bool); lifestyleId. Health-imported events are de-duplicated by time and product. [p21, p24, p25, p30]
- **DoseAuditEntry.** doseEventId; action (create, edit, delete, undo); previous and new values; timestamp. Kept locally as the local audit trail. [p30]
- **CheckIn.** id; scheduledAt; answeredAt (optional); response (yes, partly, no, or missing); surface (Watch or Lock Screen); minutes since dose (derived); products in effect; phase (learning or taper). Unanswered prompts are stored as missing. [p25, p26]
- **ReboundCheck** (optional, user-enabled). time; answer to "irritable or flat right now?". [p22]
- **ProbeResult** (optional). time; type (arithmetic or reaction); length 30 to 60 s; score. [p26]
- **Covariate inputs** (all optional). ateFirst on the dose; acidic drinks, vitamin C, antacids; caffeine; menstrual cycle phase (HealthKit); sleep (HealthKit); heart rate (Watch, low weight, onset only). No genetics or CYP2D6 field: it is not modelled. [p21, p22, p23, p25]
- **FocusWindowModel.** One per product, plus a separate one per booster product, global across lifestyles. Fields: posterior over a grid of onset 0 to 180 min × offset 2 to 16 h in 15-minute steps (about 740 cells); lapse rates ε0 and ε1 (about 0.1); fixed ramp lengths per formulation; stated prior and its weight (about 3 days); evidence (check-in days, check-in count); 80% interval half-width for each edge; status (starting, early, learned); phase (learning or tapered); manualOverride (value plus active flag). Reset on a product or dose change. [p24, p25, p26, p33]
- **Task.** id; title; focusDemand (deep, medium, light, body); category (e.g. essay drafting, reading, admin); userEstimateMinutes; deadlineCritical (Bool; booked at q = 0.8); due date (exams and assessments due within 48 h are never explored); protected (Bool); fixed or flexible; anytimeToday (Bool); lifestyle scope (e.g. term-only); paused state (switching lifestyle pauses, never deletes). [p26, p34, p36, p38, p40]
- **LedgerEvent** (the behaviour ledger: the original fields plus the new ones). lifestyleId and the active mode(s); focusWindowPosition (predicted E(t) at the time, 0 to 1); reasonCode (enum of 5); outcomeSource (tap, timer, Watch workout, inferred); decisionPropensity (probability of the chosen action, for exploratory decisions). Every event is tagged with its lifestyle. [p34, p35, p38]
- **ReasonCode enum.** 1 blockedByRuleOrEvent (excluded from learning); 2 choseNotOrCouldNotStart (counts against the slot and context); 3 startedThenStopped (censored duration); 4 didButDidNotLog (success, ask duration once); 5 noAnswer (missing). [p35]
- **DayRecord.** date; lifestyleId; day type (medicated, unmedicated, planned break); dayLoggingCoverage (share of planned items with an outcome, 0 to 1); sleep duration (for the sleep-debt load reduction). [p21, p28, p34, p35]
- **DurationModel** (per category, in a population, then user, then category hierarchy). A Normal-Inverse-Gamma posterior on log(actual ÷ estimate), with m_n, a_n, s_n; forgetting 0.99 a day, or none; a small per-lifestyle offset for interruptions. [p33, p34, p38]
- **CompletionModel.** Bayesian logistic regression coefficients; features include time of day, context and E(t); a shared base with a per-lifestyle time-of-day offset; forgetting about 0.97 a day; updated nightly. [p27, p33, p38]
- **RoutineAnchorModel** (per lifestyle and per weekday type). Wake, sleep and meal anchors as Normal-Inverse-Gamma posteriors; forgetting 0.97 to 0.98 a day. [p33, p38]
- **TransitionBufferModel** (per place or transition, e.g. "home after work to first deep task"). Normal-Inverse-Gamma; default 15 minutes; travel time from maps ETA. [p33]
- **NotificationPolicy** (per lifestyle). A Thompson-sampling posterior for each of 3 to 6 daily decision points, including a "send nothing" action; fatigue state; receptivity; forgetting 0.95 a day. [p33, p38]
- **LearnedValue** (backs "What the app has learned"). name; value; range; evidence count; last change date; status label; reset action. [p35]
- **RuleProposal.** text (e.g. "move reading to mornings"); evidence; comparable event count; probability the effect is real and worthwhile; stability duration; lifestyleId; status (proposed, approved, rejected); shelvedUntil (rejection plus 30 days); optional lifestyle tag. [p35, p38]
- **Experiment** (N-of-1). question (e.g. 90 vs 60-minute blocks); 6 to 8 pairs of study days, with the order randomised within each pair; outcomes; posterior probability of a worthwhile gain; user consent; adopted flag. [p36]
- **ChangePointState.** For each signal (wake time, sleep midpoint, first activity, focus-window reports, duration ratios): a run-length posterior and a CUSUM statistic; expectChangeUntil (a known trigger plus 14 days). [p36]
- **Lifestyle.** id; name (renamable); isActive (exactly one active). Per-lifestyle members: wake, sleep and meal anchors by weekday type; usual dose clock time (follows wake time); time-of-day completion offset; notification timing and receptivity; routine set and which items are calendar-worthy; priority ladder order (e.g. holidays: shifts > gym > study); movement policy and preferred slots; weekly template (classes vs none, shift patterns). Also: the frozen snapshot while dormant, the last-used period (to scale confidence on reuse), and the setup answers. About 4 named lifestyles at most. [p37, p38, p39, p40]
- **SharedLayer** (across lifestyles). Task-duration multipliers; focus window measured from the dose; task-category effects (reading vs admin); hard rules and approved learned rules (each can be tagged to one lifestyle); places, travel overheads, opening hours; medication list, supply and scripts; the behaviour ledger. [p38]
- **LifestyleSchedule.** lifestyleId; start and end dates (e.g. 28 Nov to 22 Feb); heads-up the evening before. [p39]
- **Mode.** type (Exam, Sick day, Travel, Assessment crunch); start; end; priority and rule overrides; stacks on the active lifestyle; no learned state. [p40]
- **SupplyRecord.** productId; quantityLeft; nextScript. [p32, p38]
- **TravelSetting.** Reminder time policy (home time or local time), chosen at a time-zone change. [p28]
- **AppWakeAlarm.** time; dismissedAt (anchors "on waking" dose reminders); optional back-to-term ramp step schedule. [p27, p39]
- **ThirdPartyAIConsent.** provider; data categories disclosed; explicit permission and its timestamp. Medication, check-in and Health data are never eligible for third-party cloud AI. [p30, p32]
- **Retention, dose-log audit trail.** Kept locally. The duration is not specified. [p30]
- **Retention, dormant lifestyles.** Snapshots are stored and reused, never deleted. Reuse restores them with reduced confidence. [p37, p39]
- **Retention, learned values.** The effective memory follows the forgetting factors: 0.97 to 0.98 is about 33 to 50 days and 0.95 is about 20 days (per the PDF). 0.99 is about 100 days (inferred, 1 ÷ (1 - λ)). Forgetting freezes during a 3+ day absence. [p33, p35]
- **Retention, rejected rule proposals.** Shelved for 30 days. [p35]
- **Retention, raw ledger events.** They must persist so that late lifestyle reassignment and offline replay of new model versions can run over them. [p36, p40]
- **Never collected.** Proof of prescription. [p32]
- **Never stored.** Personal health information in iCloud. [p32]

## 3. Backend services, APIs, jobs, integrations

- **On-device learning engine.** All learning is small Bayesian arithmetic that runs on the phone. No server-side training is described. [p33]
- **Focus-window posterior service.** Exact grid computation on the device, in milliseconds, always explainable in plain numbers. [p25]
- **Nightly job.** Updates the completion model (Bayesian logistic regression). [p33]
- **Event-driven replan.** Runs on a dose log, a booster log, a late log, and the switch to the no-medication baseline day. [p22, p25, p27, p28]
- **Check-in scheduler.** Picks prompt times where the posterior is least sure: 3 to 5 a day while learning, then about one every 2 to 3 days. [p25]
- **Change-detection job.** Bayesian online change-point detection plus a CUSUM alarm on wake time, sleep midpoint, first activity, focus-window reports and duration ratios. [p36]
- **Lifestyle detector.** A daily two-state sticky filter over wake time, sleep midpoint, first activity, class hours and (if permitted) campus visits. [p39]
- **Lifestyle scheduler.** Switches on the entered session dates and sends a heads-up the evening before. [p39]
- **Raw-event replay.** Reassigns logged days to another lifestyle ("Switch since Monday"). [p40]
- **Live Activity manager.** Starts on log and chains a second activity near the 8-hour mark. [p25]
- **HealthKit reads.** Medications dose events (read-only for third parties), sleep, heart rate, menstrual cycle phase, workouts. [p21, p23, p25, p33, p34]
- **Health de-duplication.** Imported Health dose events are matched to app logs by time and product. [p25]
- **Calendar integration.** Reads for class hours and for new timetables (a known change trigger). Writes from parsed content need a user confirm. External events like classes are never changed by a lifestyle switch. [p32, p36, p39, p40]
- **Maps ETA.** Seeds travel buffers. [p33]
- **Location (optional).** Campus visits, only if permitted, feed the lifestyle detector. [p39]
- **AI generation.** Output is generated into fixed structures: Apple Foundation Models `@Generable` types, or JSON schemas for other models. No structure has a field for a medication action. [p31]
- **AI routing.** Medication, check-in and Health data are processed on the device or through Apple's Private Cloud Compute, and never sent to third-party cloud AI. [p30]
- **Bring-your-own AI provider constraints.** Provider terms ban medical advice anyway. Google's Gemini API terms ban medical advice and any app "likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18". OpenAI bars tailored medical advice without a licensed professional. Anthropic requires professional review for high-risk healthcare uses, and age verification plus safeguards for products serving minors. [p32]
- **Deterministic output filter.** Runs on every AI output and blocks dose, timing and booster language. [p31]
- **Untrusted-input pipeline.** Screenshots, emails and web pages are parsed as data only, into title, time and place. [p32]
- **Sync constraint.** App Review 5.1.3(ii) (no personal health information in iCloud) rules out plain CloudKit sync of dose logs [confirm with Apple]. The sync design is in PDF section 15. [p32]
- **Model release pipeline.** Every exploratory decision logs its probability, so a new model version can be replayed over the user's history before it ships. [p36]
- **Export.** User-initiated export of the dose log. [p31]
- **Crisis routing.** Deterministic, static, by country: Lifeline 13 11 14 and 000 in Australia, 988 in the US. [p30]
- **Content libraries.** Per-product template curves, fixed ramp lengths per formulation, and fixed label text for "About this medicine". [p22, p24, p25]

## 4. Algorithms and parameters (every number)

### 4.1 Focus-window model

- **Template curve per product.** Effect rises from 0 at onset to full over a short ramp, holds, then falls to 0 at offset. Only onset and offset are learned per person. Ramp lengths are fixed per formulation. [p25]
- **Check-in likelihood.** `P(yes at t) = ε0 + (1 - ε0 - ε1) × E(t - t_dose)`. Each check-in is a noisy yes, partly or no. [p25]
- **Lapse rates.** ε0 and ε1 are about 0.1 each. They absorb a "yes" while flat and a "no" while fine. [p25]
- **Grid posterior.** Computed exactly with onset 0 to 180 min and offset 2 to 16 h, both in 15-minute steps, about 740 cells (13 × 57 = 741, inferred). Runs in milliseconds on the phone. [p25]
- **Boosters.** Curves are added, each with its own learned parameters: `E_total(t) = min(1, Σ_k E_k(t - t_k))`. [p25]
- **Bateman curve.** A pharmacokinetic (Bateman) curve is used only as a shape prior, because its absorption parameters can't be pinned down from sparse yes/no data. [p25]
- **Heart rate.** A low-weight extra signal for onset only. It never triggers a prompt, never sets the end of the window, and never confirms that a dose was taken. [p23, p25]
- **Stated prior.** The user's own experience is weighted above label data and is worth about 3 days of check-ins. [p24]
- **Manual override.** A manual window edit is a hard override until the user releases it. [p24]
- **Check-in cadence.** 3 to 5 prompts a day in the first 5 to 7 medicated days (about a week), each placed where the model is least sure. [p23, p25]
- **Taper criterion.** Once the 80% interval on both the onset and offset edges is within ±30 minutes, drop to about one prompt every 2 to 3 days. [p25]
- **Missing data.** Plan for 1 in 5 prompts missing (average compliance 79% across 477 studies). Prompt count per day matters less than questionnaire length, so keep one question. [p26]
- **Display thresholds.** Under 3 check-in days: starting estimate. 3 to 7 days: early estimate with ±. 8+ days and interval under ±30 min: "Your usual focus". [p26]
- **Relearn triggers.** A dose change, product change or brand change, or a change-detection event. A brand or generic switch reopens learning for about 1 to 2 weeks. [p21, p26]
- **Focus window scope.** One curve per product, shared across lifestyles, measured from the dose. It resets on a product or dose change. [p33, p38]

### 4.2 Modifiers (what shifts the window for one person)

- **Food at dose time.** Lisdexamfetamine is about 1 h later to peak; Adderall XR about 2.5 h later; bead products are variable; Concerta has no food effect. The one-tap "ate before?" shifts the day's expected onset for that formulation. [p21]
- **Acidic drinks, vitamin C, antacids (amphetamines).** Exposure varies about 3.7-fold between acidic and alkaline urine. This is an optional covariate: the effect size at dose time is unquantified, so it is learned, not assumed. [p21]
- **Menstrual cycle (mainly amphetamines).** The effect was felt stronger in the follicular phase, tracking oestradiol (r up to +.78). HealthKit cycle phase is an optional covariate. The app shows patterns and never adjusts doses. [p21]
- **Acute tolerance (all stimulants).** The felt effect fades before blood levels fall, and a closely spaced second dose gives a smaller gain. Half-life is a poor guide to wear-off, so boosters get their own learned curve. [p21, p22]
- **Sleep debt (all stimulants).** Short sleep lowers the day's planned load even when check-ins say "working". [p21]
- **CYP2D6 (atomoxetine).** Poor metabolisers (about 7% of Caucasians) get about 10× exposure. Not modelled, because there is no daily window. [p21]
- **Brand or generic switch (especially OROS).** Two US generics released more slowly at 7 to 12 h. Each brand is a separate product, and a switch reopens learning for 1 to 2 weeks. [p21]
- **Dose change.** Exposure scales with dose (lisdexamfetamine is linear from 50 to 250 mg). A logged dose change reopens learning. [p21]
- **Caffeine, time of day, chronic tolerance.** No usable quantitative data. Optional logs only. The app learns whether they matter for this person. [p22]
- **Booster day-one shapes.** Methylphenidate IR starts in 20 to 60 minutes and lasts 2 to 4 hours. Dexamfetamine IR lasts 3 to 6 hours. A booster may feel weaker than the same tablet taken first thing, so booster curves are learned separately from morning ones. [p22]
- **Label limits are display text only.** Methylphenidate IR: last dose before 6 pm. Australian dexamfetamine: a missed dose no later than 6 h before bedtime. Vyvanse: avoid afternoon or evening. These are never used by the planner or notifications. [p22]
- **Rebound.** Usually irritability for about an hour. In a children's trial it was rarer on the drug (3.4%) than on placebo (9.7%), and no adult prevalence data exist. So rebound is not in the default model. [p22]
- **Heart rate evidence.** Medication raises average HR by about 5.4 bpm, and Watch HR error is about 2%. After lisdexamfetamine or dexamfetamine, HR stays raised for up to 24 h, so HR is weak evidence for onset and of no use for wear-off. [p23]

### 4.3 Filling the window

- **Zones and fill order.** Before and kicking in: on-ramp tasks. Peak: deep work, longest blocks first. Fading: medium, then light. Outside: body and social tasks. [p26, p27]
- **E(t) in the completion model.** The predicted effect E(t) is one feature among several, so the solver learns whether this person really finishes reading better at 10 am than at 3 pm. [p27]
- **Shortfall order.** Split, defer, shrink to the baseline day, flag the deadline risk. Never medication. [p27]
- **Worked-example constants.** Window 1:00 to 9:00 after logging (dose at 8:10, so focus 9:10 to 17:10); lunch at least 5 h after the dose (13:10); deep blocks of 2 × 80 min with a break, then 90 min; landing alert 30 min before the usual end (16:40). [p27]
- **Live Activity limits.** 8 hours active, 12 visible. Chain the next one near the 8-hour mark. [p25]

### 4.4 Learning system

- **Shrinkage.** The personal data share of an estimate is `n ÷ (n + κ)`, where κ is the default's weight in pseudo-observations. With κ = 4: 50% at 4 observations, 71% at 10, 86% at 25. Default κ is 4 ("half the weight after about 4 observations"). [p33, p34]
- **Durations model.** Log of actual ÷ estimate, Normal-Inverse-Gamma, in a population, then user, then category hierarchy. The prior multiplier is about 1.4 to 1.6× (students took 1.64× their estimate). Memory is very slow (a trait): forgetting 0.99 a day, or none. [p33]
- **Block length.** `block = E × exp(m_n + t⁻¹_{2a_n}(q) × s_n)`, where E is the user's estimate and t⁻¹ is the Student-t quantile with 2a_n degrees of freedom. [p34]
- **Quantile choice.** q = 0.5 (median) for normal tasks and q = 0.8 for deadline-critical work (reference: +15% at median, +32% at 80% in large projects). [p34]
- **Day buffer.** Buffers come from means, not medians (median overrun 1×, mean 1.81×). Simulate the day's total and hold the gap between the P80 total and the sum of medians as one floating buffer block. [p34]
- **Past-actuals display.** Show the range of the last N logged sessions (e.g. the last 6). In the cited study, linking estimates to past experience raised on-time completion from 29.3% to 60%. [p34]
- **Learn the spread, not only the bias.** ADHD time estimates are more variable, not just shorter. [p34]
- **New categories.** Start at the user's overall multiplier, not the population's. [p34]
- **Completion model.** Bayesian logistic regression, updated nightly. The prior is the user's stated preferred times, weighted as 10 to 15 observations. Per lifestyle, with forgetting about 0.97 a day. [p33]
- **Routine anchors (wake, sleep, meals).** Normal-Inverse-Gamma per weekday type. The prior is Health sleep data plus the stated routine. Per lifestyle, with forgetting 0.97 to 0.98 (33 to 50 days). [p33]
- **Transition and travel buffers.** Normal-Inverse-Gamma per place or transition. The prior is 15 minutes, with maps ETA for travel. Per lifestyle. [p33]
- **Notification timing.** Thompson sampling with a "send nothing" option. Send probability is clipped to 10% to 80%, with a fatigue term. 3 to 6 decision points a day, gated by Focus, class, driving and sleep. Per lifestyle, with forgetting 0.95 (about 20 days). [p33]
- **Ledger hygiene.** Never default "actual = planned" for learning, because it would pull every multiplier toward 1. Days where under 30% of planned items have an outcome are excluded from completion models. A gap of 3+ days away freezes forgetting. [p35]
- **Reason-code semantics.** Code 1 is excluded from learning. Code 2 counts against that slot and context. Code 3 is censored: the duration is at least the elapsed time, and it is never treated as done. Code 4 is a success and asks for the duration once. Code 5 is missing. [p35]
- **Passive evidence.** It is weighted below explicit logs (outcome source: tap, timer, Watch workout, inferred). [p34]
- **Confidence tiers.** Fewer than 3 observations: a soft default. 3 to 7: a soft cost with a wide range. 8+ with a range narrower than ×1.5: "Learned", still a soft cost. [p35]
- **Standing-rule threshold.** 10+ comparable events in the current lifestyle, 90% probability that the effect is real and worthwhile, and 2 weeks of stability, then the user's approval. A rejection shelves the proposal for 30 days. [p35]
- **Stated preferences.** They are strong priors, and behaviour can only propose changes to them. [p35]
- **N-of-1 experiments.** Blocked pairs of study days, one of each condition in random order, 6 to 8 pairs, analysed with a Bayesian model. Adopt only if P(worthwhile gain) > 80% and the user says yes. [p36]
- **Everyday exploration.** Only flexible, low-stakes items (errands, habits, optional study), at most 1 to 2 exploratory moves a day, with posterior scaling to damp "weird suggestions". [p36]
- **Never explored.** Exams and assessments due within 48 hours, medication reminders, fixed commitments, and anything marked protected. [p36]
- **Change detection.** A shift is real only if all four hold: (1) it is probably under 3 days old (probability over 0.8); (2) it moves the mean by more than 30 minutes or one normal day-to-day spread, whichever is larger; (3) at least two independent signals agree (e.g. wake time and first Watch activity); (4) it has lasted 3+ days. [p36]
- **Known triggers.** A logged dose or product change, or a new timetable in the calendar, makes the model expect change for 14 days, so it adapts fast. [p36]
- **Division of labour.** The LLM never computes a number. The LLM interprets, the rules engine constrains, the solver solves. [p37]

### 4.5 Lifestyles

- **Personalisation curve for a new Lifestyle 2.** About 50% after 3 days, 70% after 7, 82% after 14. This matches `n ÷ (n + 3)` with a 3-day prior (inferred). [p37, p39]
- **Starting anchors.** They come from the user's measured term-time weekend timing. With no weekend data, the default is weekday wake plus 50 to 90 minutes (students released from fixed schedules went to bed about 50 minutes later). [p38]
- **Weak prior.** The borrowed pattern is worth about 3 days of evidence, with 1.5× the usual uncertainty. [p38]
- **Fast forgetting.** For the first 14 days older days are discounted quickly, then forgetting slows (holiday sleep shifts abruptly, drifts through week 1, settles in week 2). [p38]
- **Dormant lifestyle.** No updates and no decay. Switching back is a pointer swap, not a relearn. [p37]
- **Switching back.** Extra uncertainty and a lighter plan for 3 to 5 days (irregular sleepers' melatonin onset was 2 h 36 min later). [p39]
- **Next use of a snapshot.** Confidence is reduced in proportion to how long ago it was, capped at about a week of evidence. The new period's data dominates within about a week. [p39]
- **Forgotten-toggle filter.** Two-state and sticky, with a 97% chance of staying put. It asks when the other lifestyle has been over 90% likely for 2 days running. If Lifestyle 2 has never been used, change-point detection runs on the routine anchors, more sensitive around entered session dates. [p39]
- **Limits.** About 4 named lifestyles. Suggest a merge when two are statistically near-identical. With under 3 days of evidence, stay near the prior. [p40]

## 5. Notifications and nudges

- **Dose reminder.** Only at the time the user set, or anchored to wake if the user chose "on waking" (e.g. when the app-owned wake alarm is dismissed). If an inferred schedule is ever used, the user must confirm it. [p24, p27, p29]

- **Rebound check.** An optional, user-enabled "irritable or flat right now?" at the end of the window. [p22]
- **Landing alert.** 30 minutes before the usual end of the focus pattern: "wrap up, snack". [p27]
- **Live Activity.** "Logged 8:10 · usual focus 9:10 to 5:10", chained near 8 h and mirrored to the Watch Smart Stack and the Mac menu bar. [p25]
- **Timing policy.** Learned per lifestyle with Thompson sampling. There is a "send nothing" option, send probability is clipped to 10% to 80%, and there is a fatigue term. 3 to 6 decision points a day, gated by Focus, class, driving and sleep. [p33]
- **Medication reminders are never explored.** They are excluded from exploration and experiments. [p36]

- **Lifestyle suggestion.** "Last 3 days: up at 9:40 (term average 7:15), no classes. Switch to Holidays? Your settings from July are ready." One tap to accept or dismiss, never an automatic flip. [p39]
- **Scheduled lifestyle switch.** Heads-up the evening before. [p39]
- **Reason code prompt.** Shown only when something lapses, one tap. [p35]
- **Duration follow-up.** For "did it but didn't log", ask for the real duration once. [p35]
- **Standing-rule suggestion.** Shown with its evidence. A rejection means no re-ask for 30 days. [p35]
- **Back-to-term ramp.** The app's own wake alarm moves earlier in small steps over the last few days of a break (optional). [p39]

## 7. Privacy, security, safety, compliance (build-relevant)

- **Rule 1.** Never suggest, calculate or prompt taking, moving, adding, skipping or changing a dose or booster. This includes "take your booster now to cover your 3 pm lecture". [p29]
- **Rule 2.** Every medication reminder is set by the user at a time they choose. Any schedule the app infers must be confirmed by the user. [p29]
- **Rule 3.** Dose time is a fixed input to the scheduler. When capacity is short, change tasks, never medication. [p29]
- **Rule 4.** Call the learned window "your focus pattern from your check-ins", never onset, wear-off, effectiveness or a pharmacokinetic curve. [p29]
- **Rule 5.** Never claim to treat, manage or reduce ADHD symptoms in the App Store listing, marketing, onboarding or notifications. [p29]
- **Rule 6.** Never alert on heart rate or sleep in connection with medication. [p30]
- **Rule 7.** Before a second same-medicine log within the user's interval, show the earlier log and require a deliberate confirm, in neutral words (never "safe to take"). [p30]
- **Rule 8.** Instant undo, plus edit and delete of any log, with a local audit trail. [p30]
- **Rule 9.** Never mark a dose taken from a notification without an explicit "taken" tap. Snooze and dismiss are not taken. [p30]
- **Rule 10.** Never create booster, catch-up or late-afternoon medication prompts. A late user-set reminder gets at most the stored "ask your prescriber or pharmacist" note. [p30]
- **Rule 11.** No logging for someone else, no sharing or gifting features, no marketing to undiagnosed people. [p30]
- **Rule 12.** AI outputs structurally cannot contain medication advice. [p30, p31]
- **Rule 13.** Medication, check-in and Health data are processed on the device or through Apple's Private Cloud Compute, and never sent to third-party cloud AI. [p30]
- **Rule 14.** Show "not medical advice; follow your prescription and check with your doctor or pharmacist" in onboarding and on medication screens (App Store 1.4.1). [p30]
- **Rule 15.** A static, non-AI "Get support" screen and deterministic crisis routing: Lifeline 13 11 14 and 000 in Australia, 988 in the US. [p30]
- **Rule 16.** Launch 18+ only. [p30, p32]
- **The line not to cross.** The single feature that would make the app a regulated device is any suggestion about when or how much to take, including boosters. Logging, a self-reported focus pattern, and arranging tasks around it must stay on the productivity side through wording, data handling and AI outputs. [p28]
- **No treatment purpose.** The app must not rely on any regulator's wellness or digital-mental-health exclusion, so it must have no treatment purpose at all. [p29]
- **AI structure.** Every AI output goes into a fixed structure (`@Generable` or a JSON schema) with no field for a medication action. A deterministic filter blocks dose, timing and booster language. [p31]
- **Medication questions to AI.** They return the fixed referral "That's one for your prescriber or pharmacist." [p31, p32]
- **Prompt injection.** Screenshots, emails and web pages can carry hidden instructions. Parsed content is data only and fills fixed fields. Every calendar write from parsed content needs a confirm. Parsed content can never create or change a medication entry or reminder. [p32]
- **AI transparency.** Tell users they are talking to AI. [p29]
- **Third-party sharing.** Clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including third-party AI, and get explicit permission first (5.1.2(i)). [p32]
- **Bring-your-own AI.** Provider terms ban medical advice (Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic), and Gemini's terms bar apps likely used by under-18s, which is one reason for 18+. Keeping medication, check-in and Health data out of third-party AI is mandatory. [p30, p32]
- **Health data in iCloud.** No personal health information in iCloud (5.1.3(ii)), so no plain CloudKit sync of dose logs [confirm with Apple]. [p32]
- **Accurate privacy claims.** Say "Health details are processed on your device where possible". Never say "Your data never leaves your phone" if any cloud call exists (FTC v GoodRx, deceptive privacy promise). [p31]
- **Data minimisation.** Do not ask for proof of prescription, because it would only collect more sensitive data. [p32]
- **Misuse and diversion.** Never make non-medical use easier. The supply tracker stays neutral (quantity left, next script). No "study hack" framing, no auto-refill style flows, no advertising that persuades people without ADHD that they have it. Onboarding says the app is for medication taken as prescribed. [p32]
- **Heart rate.** Never used for wear-off, never used to confirm a dose, never used to prompt. [p23, p25]
- **Menstrual cycle data.** Optional. It only shows patterns and never adjusts doses. [p21]
- **Label limits.** Stored product label limits are user-opened text only and are never used in plans or notifications. [p22]
- **Travel.** The app never picks a dose time. [p28]
- **Age.** 18+ at launch. The Children's Online Privacy Code must be registered by 10 December 2026, so revisit a 16 to 17 tier after the Code is final. The OAIC presumes people 15 and over can consent, but Gemini's terms bar under-18 apps outright, and Anthropic requires age verification and safeguards for products serving minors. The under-16 social media ban does not apply to a health-support tool. [p32]
- **Publisher.** A Pty Ltd, because healthcare and sensitive-data apps "should be submitted by a legal entity" (5.1.1(ix)). [p32]
- **Dosage calculators.** Never compute dose or timing (1.4.2: only approved entities may offer dosage calculators). [p32]
- **Legal review gate.** Items marked [confirm] need an Australian health-regulatory and privacy lawyer before launch. [p28]
- **Full rule set.** There are 32 regulatory rules in separate research notes; the 16 above are the ones that shape the product. [p30]

- **Misuse framing.** No "study hack" framing. Onboarding says the app is for medication taken as prescribed. [p32]
- **LLM tone for venting.** Turn a vent into one targeted, specific question, not generic reassurance. [p37]

## 9. Analytics, metrics, experiments (privacy-preserving)

- **Where evaluation runs.** All learning and evaluation run on the device. These pages define per-user quality metrics and no raw-data telemetry. [p33, p36]
- **Duration calibration.** Share of tasks finishing within the P50 prediction (target 50%) and the P80 prediction (target 80%) over a rolling 30 tasks, compared with the raw-estimate baseline (about 30% finish within their best guess). [p36]
- **Completion prediction quality.** Brier score and a 5-bin reliability check. [p36]
- **Plan-level metrics.** Blocks done within 30 minutes of plan; reschedules a day; end-of-day overrun minutes; suggestion acceptance. [p36]
- **Offline replay.** Every exploratory decision logs its probability (decision propensity), so a new model version can be replayed over the user's history before it ships. [p35, p36]
- **Check-in compliance.** Expect about 79%, so design for 1 in 5 missing. Response rate per prompt is the health signal for check-in burden (inferred). [p26]
- **Day logging coverage.** Recorded per day. Days under 30% coverage are excluded from completion models, to stop good-days-only logging from biasing them. [p34, p35]
- **N-of-1 experiments.** 6 to 8 randomised pairs, Bayesian analysis, adopt at over 80% probability of a worthwhile gain, and only with the user's yes. [p36]
- **Everyday exploration.** At most 1 to 2 moves a day, on low-stakes items only, with posterior scaling. [p36]
- **Notification bandit.** Thompson sampling with a "send nothing" arm, send probability clipped to 10% to 80%, and a fatigue term. [p33]
- **Standing-rule adoption.** 10+ events, 90% probability, 2 weeks stable, plus approval. [p35]
- **Transparency as a metric surface.** Every learned value shows its evidence count, range and last change. [p35]
- **Data boundary.** Medication, check-in and Health data never go to third-party cloud AI. Any analytics claim must match the actual data flow (no "never leaves your phone" if any cloud call exists). [p30, p31]

## 10. Testing and quality requirements

- **Performance.** The focus-window posterior (about 740 cells) updates in milliseconds on the phone. [p25]
- **Explainability.** Every learned number can be shown in plain numbers with its evidence count and range. [p25, p35]
- **Golden test.** The worked example (window 1:00 to 9:00, dose logged 8:10, lunch rule 5 h) reproduces the exact timeline and reason strings on page 27. [p27]
- **Duplicate guard.** A second same-medicine log within the minimum interval shows the earlier log and requires a second confirm. The copy contains no "safe". [p24, p30]
- **Notifications.** Snooze and dismiss never create a taken dose. Only an explicit "taken" tap does. [p24, p30]
- **No-prompt tests.** No booster, catch-up or late-afternoon medication prompt is ever generated. Neither the cut-off nor the label text ever produces a notification or a plan change. [p22, p24, p30]
- **Missed dose.** Exactly one reminder, then only the "Plan today without medication?" offer. [p28]
- **Break days.** Planned break days and medication-free days never register as missed and never enter learning. [p24, p28]
- **AI schema.** `@Generable` types and JSON schemas contain no medication-action field. [p31]
- **AI filter.** Dose, timing and booster language is blocked. Every medication question returns the fixed referral string. [p31, p32]
- **Prompt injection.** Hidden instructions in screenshots, emails and web pages cannot create or change a medication entry or reminder, and every calendar write from parsed content waits for a confirm. [p32]
- **Data flow.** Medication, check-in and Health data never reach a third-party cloud AI endpoint. Only on-device or Private Cloud Compute is allowed. [p30]
- **Copy lint.** Banned terms are absent from the App Store listing, marketing, onboarding, notifications and medication screens. The disclaimer is present on onboarding and on every medication screen. [p29, p30, p31]
- **Heart rate and sleep.** No alert ties heart rate or sleep to medication. Heart rate never sets the window end and never triggers a prompt. [p25, p30]
- **Health de-duplication.** A dose logged in both Health and the app counts once (matched by time and product). [p25]
- **Live Activity continuity.** A second Live Activity is chained near 8 h, so an 8+ hour window stays visible. [p25]
- **Check-in cadence.** 3 to 5 prompts a day in the learning phase. Taper only when both 80% edges are within ±30 min. Learning reopens on a dose, product or brand change. [p25, p26]
- **Learning hygiene.** Rule-blocked lapses are excluded. Started-then-stopped items are censored, not done. Never "actual = planned". Days under 30% coverage are excluded. A 3+ day absence freezes forgetting. [p35]
- **Exploration exclusions.** Exams and assessments within 48 h, medication reminders, fixed commitments and protected items are never explored. [p36]
- **Change detection.** A shift fires only when all four criteria hold. A known trigger raises change expectation for 14 days. [p36]
- **Lifestyles.** The dormant lifestyle receives no updates and no decay. Switching back restores it exactly. Undo is one tap. Paused routines are never deleted. External events are untouched. [p37, p40]
- **Sticky filter.** A single late night never triggers a lifestyle suggestion. A suggestion appears only after the other lifestyle has been over 90% likely for 2 days running. [p39]
- **Late switch.** "Switch since Monday" produces the same models as if the switch had happened on Monday (raw event replay). [p40]
- **Time zones.** A time-zone change asks home vs local and never changes a dose time on its own. [p28]
- **Model release gate.** Each new model version is replayed over the user's history before it ships. [p36]
- **Crisis routing.** Deterministic and static: 13 11 14 and 000 for Australia, 988 for the US. [p30]
- **Age gate.** Under-18 users cannot proceed at launch. [p32]
- **Launch gates.** Lawyer review of the [confirm] items, and Apple confirmation on health data and CloudKit, before launch. [p28, p32]

## 11. Roadmap and phasing

- **Pre-launch gates.** An Australian health-regulatory and privacy lawyer reviews the [confirm] items. Apple confirms the 5.1.3(ii) iCloud and CloudKit position. A Pty Ltd is set up as the publisher. [p28, p32]
- **Launch audience.** 18+ only. [p30, p32]
- **Post-launch.** Revisit a 16 to 17 tier after the Children's Online Privacy Code is final (it must be registered by 10 December 2026). [p32]
- **Off by default at launch.** The objective probe and the rebound check are off. The covariates (acidic drinks, caffeine, cycle phase) are optional. [p21, p22, p26]
- **Core differentiator.** The PDF names learned personal duration multipliers and learned medication-anchored focus windows as the core differentiator, because no surveyed scheduler documents either. [p33]
- **Regulatory backlog.** Bring the full 32-rule regulatory set (in separate research notes) into the spec. The 16 product-shaping rules are listed in section 7. [p30]
- **Per-user phasing, focus window.** Starting estimate (under 3 days), then early estimate (3 to 7 days), then learned (8+ days and ±30 min), then taper. Relearn on triggers. [p25, p26]
- **Per-user phasing, Lifestyle 2.** About 50% personalised at 3 days, 70% at 7, 82% at 14. Plan lightly until confident. [p37, p38, p39]
- **Model updates.** Ship only after offline replay over user history passes. [p36]
- **Market context (Australia).** NSW specialists have held a class authority since 13 November 2023. Trained GPs can continue stable prescriptions from 1 September 2025. The first GPs trained to diagnose and start treatment finished in March 2026, and funded assessment services start in early 2027. By November 2025 there were 560 GP authorities and 2,953 patients. SafeScript NSW is mandatory for the new GP prescribers. [p23]
- **Supply context.** Methylphenidate products were short in 2023 to 2025. The TGA judged the shortage "largely resolved" in June 2026, but specific strengths are patchy. Emergency-import approvals mostly lapsed on 31 January 2026. So the brand-switch relearn flow must be solid at launch. [p23]

## 12. Edge cases, failure modes, mitigations

- **Brand or generic switch** (including shortage-driven). Treat as a new product and reopen learning for 1 to 2 weeks. [p21, p23]
- **Dose change logged.** Reopen learning and reset the product's window. [p21, p33]
- **Food at dose time.** "Ate first?" shifts the expected onset for that formulation. Concerta is unaffected. [p21]
- **Short sleep.** Lower the planned load even if check-ins say "working", because stimulants can mask sleep loss. [p21]
- **Booster feels weaker than a morning dose.** Learn booster curves separately. Combine curves with `min(1, Σ)`. [p21, p22, p25]
- **Rebound confused with wear-off.** Rebound is optional and user-enabled. It is not built into the default curve. [p22]
- **Heart rate stays high for up to 24 h after some stimulants.** Never use heart rate for wear-off or for dose confirmation, and never alert on it. [p23]
- **Non-stimulant only.** No window. Plan from routine and energy check-ins, and keep adherence and supply features. [p23]
- **Double logging.** The duplicate guard shows the earlier log and requires a deliberate second confirm. [p24]
- **Dose also logged in Apple Health.** De-duplicate by time and product. [p25]
- **Long-acting dose outlives one Live Activity.** Chain a second near 8 h. [p25]
- **Noisy check-ins** ("yes" while flat, "no" while fine). The lapse rates of about 0.1 absorb them. [p25]
- **Missing check-ins.** Expect 1 in 5 missing. Keep prompts to a single question. [p26]
- **Stated and learned disagree.** Ask: Accept, Keep mine, or Ask later. A manual edit stays a hard override until released. [p24, p26]
- **Window cannot hold the chosen work.** Split, defer, shrink to the baseline day, flag the deadline risk. Never touch medication. [p27]
- **Late log past the cut-off.** Show the stored neutral note only. [p27]
- **No log.** One reminder, then offer the unmedicated baseline day. No dose nudging. [p28]
- **Planned break days.** Unmedicated plan, excluded from adherence and learning. [p28]
- **Travel across time zones.** Ask home vs local, suggest talking to the prescriber, never pick a dose time. [p28]
- **Prompt injection via screenshots, emails or web pages.** Parsed content is data only, calendar writes need a confirm, and it has no path to medication data. [p32]
- **Non-medical use and diversion.** Neutral supply tracker, no study-hack framing, no logging for others, no sharing or gifting. [p30, p32]
- **Good-days-only logging.** Track day logging coverage and exclude days under 30%. [p34, p35]
- **Recall bias.** Use logged actuals only, never recalled durations. [p34]
- **Started then stopped.** Treat the duration as censored at the elapsed time, never as done. [p35]
- **User absent for 3+ days.** Freeze forgetting so models don't decay toward defaults. [p35]
- **"Actual = planned" shortcut.** Never used for learning, because it would pull every multiplier toward 1. [p35]
- **Brand-new task category.** Start at the user's overall multiplier. [p34]
- **Occasional blow-out tasks.** Size day buffers from the P80 total, not medians. [p34]
- **Weird suggestions from exploration.** Cap at 1 to 2 low-stakes moves a day with posterior scaling. Never explore critical items. [p36]
- **Superstitious rule changes.** Standing rules need 10+ events, 90% probability and 2 weeks of stability, plus approval. A rejection shelves the rule for 30 days. [p35]
- **Life changes silently.** Change-point detection with four criteria and an explanation prompt. Known triggers speed adaptation for 14 days. [p36]
- **One late night.** The sticky filter (97% stay) ignores it. [p39]
- **Forgotten lifestyle toggle.** Suggest after 2 days over 90%. Never auto-flip. [p39]
- **Toggle flipped late.** "Switch since Monday" replays raw events into Lifestyle 2. [p40]
- **Back from holidays.** 3 to 5 days of extra uncertainty and a lighter plan, with an optional back-to-term alarm ramp. [p39]
- **Stale last-year snapshot.** Reduce confidence in proportion to its age, capped at about a week of evidence. [p39]
- **A one-week mid-semester break.** Too little data. Don't create a new lifestyle. Under 3 days of evidence, stay near the prior and plan loosely. [p40]
- **Holidays with some shifts.** Model the shifts as fixed events inside Lifestyle 2. [p40]
- **Lifestyle sprawl.** Cap at about 4 and suggest merging near-identical ones. [p40]
- **Lifestyle switch and calendar data.** Pause term-only routines and app-created blocks. Never delete them, and never touch external calendar events. [p40]
- **Rule-driven skip vs a real miss.** Reason code 1 is excluded from learning. Code 2 counts against the slot. [p35]

## 13. Open questions

- **"Partly" in the model.** The likelihood only defines P(yes). The formula for a "Partly" response (for example half credit, or an ordinal model) is not specified. [p25]
- **Template curves and ramps.** The day-one template curves and the fixed ramp lengths per formulation live in PDF section 6 (before page 21). They are needed for the product library. [p24, p25]
- **Core planning flow.** The "original inventory's section 3 flow" and the fixed "T to T+9h" window are defined outside these pages. [p25]
- **Minimum interval.** The per-medicine minimum interval for the duplicate guard has no stated default, and there is no setup field for it in the 7.1 table. [p24, p30]
- **Missed-dose timing.** How long after the single reminder the app offers "Plan today without medication?" is not stated. [p28]
- **Prior units.** Is the "worth about 3 days of check-ins" prior counted in days or in check-ins (3 to 5 a day)? The κ for the focus window is therefore ambiguous. [p24]
- **Zone boundaries.** The E(t) thresholds that divide kicking in, Peak and Fading are not given. [p26, p27]
- **Covariate models.** How food, acidity, cycle phase, caffeine and sleep-debt covariates enter the model is not specified (e.g. an onset shift vs a planned-load factor). The size of the sleep-debt load reduction and what counts as "short sleep" are also unspecified. [p21, p22]
- **Food effect.** The food effect is quoted as a shift "to peak". How it maps onto the onset of a template curve is unclear. [p21]
- **Heart-rate weight.** "Low-weight" is not quantified. [p25]
- **Change detection criteria.** Criterion 1 ("probably under 3 days old", probability over 0.8) and criterion 4 ("lasted 3+ days") read as contradictory. The intended run-length rule needs clarifying. [p36]
- **Unquantified learning terms.** The fatigue term in the notification bandit, the posterior-scaling factor for exploration, and the "worthwhile" effect-size threshold used for experiments and standing rules are not quantified. [p33, p35, p36]

- **Travel overheads.** "Transition and travel buffers" are per lifestyle in 9.1, but "places, travel overheads, opening hours" are shared in 10.2. Which is it? [p33, p38]
- **Merge rule.** The statistical test for "near-identical" lifestyles that triggers a merge suggestion is not given. [p40]
- **Heads-up time.** The time of the evening-before heads-up for a scheduled switch is not specified. [p39]
- **Campus detection.** How "campus visits" is defined and which location permission it needs are not specified. [p39]
- **"Focus window" in copy.** The explanation copy says "after your focus window", while rule 4 mandates "your focus pattern from your check-ins". Confirm whether "focus window" is allowed in user-facing text. [p25, p29]
