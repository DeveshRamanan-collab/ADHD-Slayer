## 1. User needs and pain points → the feature or behaviour that answers each

- Time blindness: deficits across every timing paradigm (meta-analysis of 55 studies; time discrimination and reproduction show medium deficits, estimation small-to-medium, production small) → make time external and ambient: countdown rings, "leave by" times, Live Activities, Dynamic Island, Watch complications and haptics [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #12]
- Reminders fire once at a clock time, get dismissed, and show no elapsed or remaining time → persistent countdown surfaces showing time remaining to the next commitment instead of one-shot alerts [§1 Executive function: Inferences]
- Chronic misestimation of task duration and plans built around rare hyperfocus episodes → estimate-vs-actual calibration that learns a personal multiplier (example copy "you usually take 1.6× your guess") and applies it to future plans [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings; Impl #14]
- Task initiation paralysis ("big start-up difficulty") and procrastination (r=0.42, n=490) → "first tiny step" starter: micro-step breakdown by AI or template, a 2-minute "just start" timer, optional body-double session [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings; Impl #16]
- Planning is the largest prospective-memory deficit (d=1.60) while recall of a made plan is intact (about 86.5% after 40 min in both groups) → spend the product effort on a 2 to 3 minute guided planning ritual and the first step, not on repeatedly reminding the user of a plan [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #17]
- To-do apps assume the user can plan and sequence → guided sequencing inside the planning ritual (pick at most 3 items, set ideal and baseline, order them) [§1 Executive function: Inferences; Impl #17]
- Event-based self-initiation is weaker (40% vs 56%, not significant) → context-triggered reminders on location, CarPlay and Focus events so the cue comes from the world, not memory [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings; Impl #20]
- Task-switching deficit (d=0.94) → staged transition cues at 10, 5 and 1 minutes before a switch plus a "park your thought" quick capture [§1 Executive function: Inferences; Impl #18]
- Verbal and visuospatial working-memory deficits persist into adulthood → three-second universal capture into one inbox, triaged later [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings; Impl #21]
- Hyperfocus (more total and frequent hyperfocus with higher symptoms; pilot n=251, replication n=372; includes screen time), where the problem is not stopping → hyperfocus guardrail: interval taps with elapsed time, escalating to "stand, drink, eat" [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #19]
- "Waiting mode": an upcoming appointment consumes the whole block before it, which gets filled with scrolling → waiting-mode protocol: a hard "stop and go" alarm with buffer so the gap feels safe to use, plus "side quests" sized to fit [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #15]
- "Deadline addiction" (crisis-driven, unsustainable productivity) → assignment and exam backward planner with dated milestones and buffer [§8 Study and work: Cited Findings; Impl #33]
- Emotional dysregulation is large and common (pooled g=1.17; emotional lability g=1.20; about 70% of adult patients report it; correlates r=0.54 with severity) → one-tap "brain weather" capacity check-in that collapses the day to a baseline plan and reduces notifications, plus a mood-adaptive interface [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings; Impl #24]
- Pervasive self-blame, shame spirals and rejection sensitivity (77% of n=43 young adults in the largest qualitative study) → never-scolding tone, no public comparison, compassionate microcopy [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- A broken streak triggers all-or-nothing abandonment of both app and habit → forgiving progress model: optional streaks, flexible frequency goals, streak insurance or grace periods, percentage-based tracking, weekly percentages, pause days, progress kept across missed days [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings; Impl #22]
- Overwhelm and paralysis (70% rated overwhelm "life-altering" in a self-selected survey) and piles of overdue tasks → overdue amnesty / reset day: one tap reschedules or archives everything overdue and hides red counts [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings; Impl #23]
- Planners punish lapses with red overdue counts, broken streaks and stacks of missed notifications → no red counts, no destructive streaks, no pile-up of missed notifications after a lapse [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Inferences]
- Boredom proneness (r=0.40 across 18 studies, over 22,000 participants) and novelty wearing off by around week 2 → optional rotating novelty: themes, widget faces, reward variety [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #25]
- App abandonment base rates (median 4% of 30-day installers open on a given day; day-15 retention 3.9%; day-30 3.3%) and "struggle with consistently adopting any system" → designed-for-lapse re-engagement: expect breaks of 1 to 3 weeks and a "welcome back" flow that gets the user running in under 30 seconds without backlog guilt [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #25]
- Unsustainable productivity and burnout risk (masking, deadline-driven patterns) → "ideal vs baseline" day plans and "Emotionally Aware Pause Days" [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings; §14 Life transitions: Cited Findings]
- Delayed sleep/wake cycle in 73 to 78%, DLMO about 90 min later in adults, insomnia in up to 80% → dose reminder anchored to detected wake rather than a fixed clock time [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #2]
- Stimulant insomnia (lisdexamfetamine 11 to 19% vs 4 to 5% placebo) with efficacy up to 14 h post-dose, so a late morning dose reaches bedtime → user- or clinician-set latest-dose cut-off banner shown when a dose would be late [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #3]
- Revenge bedtime procrastination driven by "reclaiming my time" → protected guilt-free "me-time" block earlier in the evening, wind-down worked back from the required wake time, Screen Time shield at the cut-off [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #26]
- Morning wake difficulty and start-up difficulty with hygiene ("I obviously want to brush my teeth ... but I stay in bed") → morning launch sequence with timed sub-steps (dose, water, breakfast, hygiene, leave-by) and haptic progression [§3 Sleep: Inferences; §10 Health and self-care: Cited Findings; Impl #27]
- Wake, dose timing and bedtime form one coupled system, but sleep apps and planners do not talk to each other → model wake, dose, meals and bedtime together on one timeline [§3 Sleep: Takeaway, Inferences]
- Caffeine-use-disorder symptoms rise with ADHD symptoms (β=0.35) although amount consumed does not → caffeine log with a personal cut-off time and a view of caffeine alongside sleep [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings; Impl #28]
- Decreased appetite (26.5 to 33% vs 2 to 6% placebo) and reduced interoceptive accuracy (heartbeat accuracy 0.60 vs 0.70; self-report IAS 78.2 vs 88.4), so "eat when hungry" fails → meal prompts tied to the medication timeline (eat before the dose, lunch "even if not hungry", pre-wear-off protein snack, evening meal preparation) and 1-tap "ate something?" logging [§4 Eating: Takeaway, Cited Findings; Impl #6]
- Dry mouth (26 to 32% vs 1 to 8%) → hydration reminders on by default, optional HealthKit water logging [§4 Eating: Inferences]
- Appetite returns at evening wear-off (rebound) → evening meal preparation prompt ahead of rebound [§4 Eating: Inferences; §4 Eating: Cited Findings]
- Food, water and medication live in separate apps with separate reminders, none of which knows lunch falls in peak appetite suppression → one timeline for meals, water and doses [§4 Eating: Inferences]
- Forgetting is the most common unintentional reason for nonadherence (nonadherence 13.2 to 64%; fewer than half of adults "consistently medicated") → one-tap dose log from a Watch complication, lock-screen widget, Action button or NFC sticker on the pill bottle [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #1]
- Not knowing whether a dose was taken (double-dose fear) → lock-screen and wrist glance "Taken 08:02 · 3 h ago" answerable in under 2 seconds; a second tap warns "already logged today" [§5 Medication: Inferences, Gaps; Impl #1]
- Inconvenience of daily dosing → logging without opening the app [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #1]
- Wear-off rebound (about 60 minutes, typically mid-to-late afternoon; irritability, tearfulness, anger; almost all short-acting and some long-acting stimulants) → pre-wear-off "landing" alert (example 30 min before) with wrap-up, protein snack, low-stakes tasks and a warning before scheduling hard conversations [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #5]
- Peak hours wasted because nothing plans around coverage → coverage curve (onset, peak, wear-off per formulation) drawn behind calendar events; deep-work blocks placed inside peak coverage [§5 Medication: Inferences; §8 Study and work: Inferences; Impl #4]
- Refills require sustained mental effort → supply countdown and refill planner (auto-decrement, run-out forecast, repeats, script expiry, prescriber appointment, eScript tokens) [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #7]
- Shortages (US: 38% had trouble filling in 13 months, calling pharmacies 3 to 16+ times a month, driving 3 to 50 miles; UK: 97% affected, 70% rationed or skipped, 68% called multiple pharmacies; Australia: methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine and dexamfetamine shortages from 2023 into late 2025) → shortage mode: pharmacy call list, stock-check log, call-back reminders, TGA shortage database link, prompt to contact the prescriber [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #8]
- Suboptimal dosing, short appointments, unsupportive clinicians and poor prescriber communication → clinician-ready PDF report with adherence, timing, side effects, 1 to 10 effectiveness ratings and cycle overlay [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #11]
- Poor psychoeducation drives nonadherence; women are diagnosed about 5 years later (28.96 vs 24.13) → 60-second in-context micro-psychoeducation cards and onboarding psychoeducation for late-diagnosed adults [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; §12 Sex and gender: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #41]
- Stigma and embarrassment are reasons for nonadherence, and the medicine is Schedule 8 → discreet mode: neutral notification text, alternate icon, local-first data, Face ID lock [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #39]
- Drug holidays and as-needed use are real patterns → planned medication days off are not logged as "missed" [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; §14 Life transitions: Cited Findings; Impl #34]
- Tolerance worry, waning effectiveness (anecdote) and "loss of authentic self" → 1 to 10 effectiveness ratings and side-effect logs over time, surfaced in the clinician report for discussion, with no in-app interpretation (extractor note) [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #11]
- Symptoms vary with the menstrual cycle while doses stay fixed (worst in menstruation, mildest mid-follicular; n=30 over 35 days) → optional cycle-phase overlay with daily 1-tap focus and mood ratings for prescriber discussion [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; §12 Sex and gender: Cited Findings; Impl #9]
- Perimenopause reported as peak impairment (94% report worse symptoms, 83% some symptoms for the first time, self-selected sample) → perimenopause tracking mode (brain fog, overwhelm, sleep, mood) with clinician report [§12 Sex and gender: Cited Findings; Impl #10]
- Cost and access barriers (PBS co-payment $25.00 general and $7.70 concession from 1 Jan 2026; Safety Net resets every January; one witness cited an $830 initial consultation) → Australian admin pack: Safety Net tracking, co-payment awareness, state rule notes, "new script needed" prompt [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; §16 Australian context: Inferences; Impl #42]
- Scripts from one state or territory cannot always be filled in another (border communities); NSW paper Schedule 8 scripts historically had to be filled at the same pharmacy → state rule notes in the refill planner and in travel mode [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #7]
- Care moving from psychiatrist to GP continuation prescriber (NSW Stage 1 from 1 Sept 2025; Stage 2 in early 2026) → prescriber records can change without losing history [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; §16 Australian context: Inferences]
- Missed due dates and forgotten subscription renewals (57% miss loan payments, secondary figure; "you forgot to cancel it"), while banking apps only notify after the charge → bills and subscriptions on the timeline, alerts before free trials convert, autopay check [§6 Money: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #29]
- Impulsive shopping (62%, secondary figure) with nothing adding friction at the moment of purchase → impulse-buy cool-off: share to the app, hold on a wishlist, ask again in 48 to 72 h [§6 Money: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #30]
- Self-blame for the "ADHD tax" → optional non-judgemental ADHD-tax log that makes costs visible without shame [§6 Money: Inferences; §2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings]
- Clutter and doom piles (clinically significant hoarding 20% vs 2% of controls, tracks inattention; not raised spontaneously but acknowledged when asked) → 10 to 15 minute doom-pile sprints with body-doubling, offered proactively (extractor note on "offered proactively") [§7 Household and admin: Cited Findings; Impl #31]
- "Out of sight, out of mind": a digital calendar "basically ceases to exist for my brain once closed"; 61% prefer paper because it stays in view → persistent glanceable surfaces (widgets, complications, Live Activities) that stand in for the sticky note [§7 Household and admin: Cited Findings, Inferences; §15 Accessibility and UX: Cited Findings; Impl #37]
- Admin combines low interest, many steps and time sensitivity → paperwork templates split into steps and appointment booking prompts [§7 Household and admin: Takeaway, Inferences; Impl #31]
- Recurring chores turn into guilt-inducing overdue items → chores modelled as rhythms, not deadlines [§7 Household and admin: Inferences]
- Losing items → departure-triggered "keys, wallet, meds?" prompt on leaving home with a Find My hand-off [§7 Household and admin: Inferences; Impl #20]
- Long assignments get a due date but no dated milestones sized to the user's pace → backward planner using the calibration multiplier [§8 Study and work: Inferences; Impl #33]
- Meetings → meeting prep and follow-up capture [§8 Study and work: Inferences]
- Semester, exam and break periods need different routines → Semester, Exam and Break templates [§8 Study and work: Inferences; Impl #34]
- Shift work, where "the day" does not start at midnight (no ADHD-specific evidence exists) → days anchored to wake time in a Shift mode [§8 Study and work: Inferences, Gaps; Impl #34]
- Medication gaps damage grades and jobs (91% worried about their job, 44% feared losing it; "three months without medication ... devastating effect on his college grades") → supply, refill and shortage features are core, not optional extras (extractor note) [§8 Study and work: Cited Findings]
- Texting back fails because a read message vanishes from attention → reply-later inbox fed from the share sheet or Shortcuts, with gentle batch reminders [§9 Relationships: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #32]
- Birthday reminders fire on the day, too late to buy a gift → birthday lead times from Contacts: 7 days (gift) and on the day (message) [§9 Relationships: Inferences; Impl #32]
- Friendships drift without contact → per-person check-in cadence and scheduled recurring check-ins [§9 Relationships: Cited Findings; Impl #32]
- Lateness driven by time-perception deficits → leave-by planner (arrival time minus travel time minus learned lateness buffer) and leave-by alerts for social plans [§9 Relationships: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #13]
- High relational stakes (75% worried about relationships and 28% worried one might end during the UK shortage) → social scaffolding plus supply continuity [§9 Relationships: Cited Findings]
- Peer support is valued ("Only in the ADHD group do I feel that I can tell the truth.") → optional accountability buddy or AI companion framed as companionship [§9 Relationships: Cited Findings; Impl #44]
- Self-care fails at the start-up point → morning and evening launch sequences with timed sub-steps, and self-care paired with existing anchors such as the dose [§10 Health and self-care: Takeaway, Inferences]
- Daily streaks punish missed showers or workouts → flexible-frequency goals ("3× a week", "every 2 to 3 days") [§10 Health and self-care: Inferences; Impl #43]
- Exercise is valued (rated effective by 37% vs 40% for stimulants in a self-selected survey) but adherence evidence is missing → HealthKit workout detection auto-credits exercise goals [§10 Health and self-care: Cited Findings; Impl #43]
- Dental harm (children with ADHD symptoms: more than twice the caries incidence, 60% more extractions, 75% more restorations) and doctor appointments → dentist and GP recall reminders and appointment booking prompts [§10 Health and self-care: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #43]
- Social-media disorder is about 5 times more likely (15.0% vs 3.3%, OR 5.25), driven by escape; ADHD users over-represented at both ≤1 h and ≥6 h a day → focus shields tied to plan blocks via the Screen Time API and an optional "what are you escaping from?" check-in on long scroll sessions [§11 Digital distraction: Cited Findings; Impl #35]
- Opening the phone to check the plan invites a rabbit hole ("Digital requires me to turn on a device, and down the rabbit hole I go.") → Watch-first and widget-first plan checking without unlocking the phone [§11 Digital distraction: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- Planner apps add to notification overload → notification budget: small daily maximum, bundling, escalation only for dose and leave-by alerts [§11 Digital distraction: Inferences; Impl #36]
- Comorbid anxiety (about 50%) and autism (27% of autistic adults without intellectual disability have ADHD) require predictability and sensory control → Calm vs Spark presentation modes, no surprise layout changes, customisable haptics and sounds [§13 Comorbidities: Cited Findings, Inferences; Impl #38]
- Imperative "Do X now!" copy can trigger demand avoidance → invitational language everywhere [§13 Comorbidities: Inferences; Impl #38]
- Dyslexia and learning disabilities (estimated 50 to 60%, unsourced clinician estimate) and reading fatigue → icon- and colour-led UI, voice input and output, dyslexia-friendly font option, low reading load [§13 Comorbidities: Cited Findings, Inferences; §15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences; Impl #37]
- Depression (18.6 to 53.3%), higher suicide rates, and 38% reporting new or increased suicidal thoughts during the UK medication shortage → mood safety net: persistently low check-ins trigger Australian crisis and support signposting and a prompt to contact a GP [§13 Comorbidities: Cited Findings; §10 Health and self-care: Cited Findings; Impl #40]
- Substance use disorders are 2× more likely; caffeine-use-disorder symptoms rise with ADHD symptoms → discreet handling of substance and caffeine logging [§13 Comorbidities: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- Routines anchored to external structure collapse when that structure disappears (holidays, weekends, travel) → context modes with separate templates switched automatically from calendar data, plus weekend wake-anchor protection [§14 Life transitions: Takeaway, Inferences; Impl #34]
- Reminders scheduled in the home time zone silently follow device time or drift, unexplained → travel mode asks "home time or local time?" for dose reminders, explains the behaviour, and prompts a prescriber check before the trip [§14 Life transitions: Inferences; Impl #34]
- Safety Net and new scripts reset in January → January reminders about the Safety Net and new scripts [§14 Life transitions: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- Feature-rich planners push setting up the system (itself an executive-function task) onto the user → opinionated defaults instead of configuration screens [§15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences]
- Choice overload → at most 3 focus items visible [§15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences; Impl #37]
- Input must be low friction → capture in under 3 seconds by voice, Action button or Watch [§15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences; Impl #21]
- Literature leans on deficit framing and a therapeutic perspective while digital interventions show only small symptom effects (g=-0.32 overall, -0.25 inattention, hyperactivity not significant) → position the app as practical support for daily function and communication with the prescriber, not treatment [§15 Accessibility and UX: Takeaway, Cited Findings; Impl positioning note]

## 2. Features and behaviours (with acceptance criteria)

- **F1 One-tap dose log.** Surfaces: Watch complication, lock-screen widget, Action button, NFC sticker on the pill bottle, plus in-app. AC: (a) one tap or one NFC scan saves a dose with a timestamp without opening the app; (b) afterwards the lock screen shows the format "Taken 08:02 · 3 h ago" (dose time plus elapsed time); (c) "did I take it?" is answerable from the wrist in under 2 seconds with no navigation; (d) a second log of the same dose on the same day shows "already logged today" and is saved only after explicit confirmation. [Impl #1; §5 Medication: Inferences]
- **F2 Wake-anchored dose reminder.** AC: (a) reminder time equals detected wake (Watch sleep data or first unlock) plus the user's offset, never a fixed clock time; (b) test: with wake detected at 10:30, nothing fires at the old fixed time (e.g. 08:00) and the reminder fires after 10:30; (c) an unacknowledged reminder escalates haptics; (d) dose reminders may escalate even when the notification budget is spent. [Impl #2; Impl #36; §3 Sleep: Inferences]
- **F3 Latest-dose cut-off banner.** AC: (a) the user or clinician enters a cut-off time and the app records who set it; (b) when a dose is logged, or would be logged, after the cut-off, the banner reads "this is later than your usual cut-off; consider checking with your prescriber"; (c) logging is never blocked and the app suggests no alternative time, amount or skip. [Impl #3; §3 Sleep: Inferences]
- **F4 Coverage curve on the day timeline.** AC: (a) onset, peak and wear-off are user-entered per formulation; (b) the curve is drawn behind calendar events and re-anchors to the actual logged dose time; (c) demanding tasks and deep-work blocks can be placed inside coverage; (d) editing any value redraws the curve immediately. [Impl #4; §5 Medication: Inferences; §8 Study and work: Inferences]
- **F5 Pre-wear-off "landing" alert.** AC: (a) fires a set lead time before wear-off (source example 30 min); (b) content covers wrapping up, a protein snack and switching to low-stakes tasks; (c) creating or keeping a hard conversation inside the landing or rebound window shows a warning (extractor note: this is the hook for the product's "move this social plan" suggestion). [Impl #5; §5 Medication: Cited Findings]
- **F6 Meal and water prompts on the medication timeline.** AC: (a) prompts exist for eat before the dose, a scheduled lunch "even if not hungry", a pre-wear-off protein snack and evening meal preparation; (b) prompt times move when the dose time moves; (c) "ate something?" logs with one tap; (d) hydration reminders are on by default; (e) HealthKit water logging is optional and needs permission. [Impl #6; §4 Eating: Inferences]
- **F7 Supply countdown and refill planner.** AC: (a) each dose log decrements supply by the units per dose; (b) a forecast run-out date is displayed; (c) a refill reminder fires at run-out minus the refill lead time; (d) repeats remaining and script expiry are tracked and a "new script needed" prompt asks the user to book the prescriber; (e) eScript tokens can be stored and reopened; (f) for a NSW paper Schedule 8 script the same-pharmacy rule is shown, noting temporary transfers are now permitted. [Impl #7; §5 Medication: Cited Findings, Inferences; §16 Australian context: Inferences]
- **F8 Shortage mode.** AC: (a) the user can switch it on and off; (b) it keeps a pharmacy call list with a stock-check log (pharmacy, date and time, result) and call-back reminders; (c) it links to the TGA Medicine Shortage Reports Database; (d) it prompts the user to contact the prescriber about alternatives; (e) the app never names an alternative medicine or dose. [Impl #8; §5 Medication: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- **F9 Cycle-phase overlay (optional).** AC: (a) off by default; HealthKit cycle import only after permission; (b) daily 1-tap focus rating and 1-tap mood rating; (c) chart of ratings by phase; (d) when menstruation and late-luteal weeks score worse, it shows "menstruation/late-luteal weeks look harder" as a point to discuss with the prescriber; (e) no dose suggestion appears anywhere. [Impl #9; §12 Sex and gender: Inferences]
- **F10 Perimenopause tracking mode.** AC: optional; logs brain fog, overwhelm, sleep and mood; appears as its own section of the clinician report. [Impl #10; §12 Sex and gender: Inferences]
- **F11 Clinician-ready report (PDF).** AC: (a) the user picks a date range and generates a PDF with adherence, dose timing, side effects (appetite, sleep, rebound), 1 to 10 effectiveness ratings and the cycle overlay if enabled; (b) it exports through the share sheet; (c) it contains data and patterns only, never recommendations. [Impl #11; §5 Medication: Cited Findings]
- **F12 Visible countdowns.** AC: time until the next commitment and the "leave by" time render as a shrinking ring in a Live Activity, the Dynamic Island and a Watch complication, readable without opening the app. [Impl #12; §1 Executive function: Inferences]
- **F13 Leave-by planner.** AC: (a) leave-by = arrival time minus travel time minus personal lateness buffer; (b) the buffer is learned from the user's history; (c) social plans get leave-by alerts too. [Impl #13; §9 Relationships: Inferences]
- **F14 Estimate-vs-actual calibration.** AC: (a) the user can enter a guessed duration and the app times the task; (b) the app reports the learned multiplier in copy such as "you usually take 1.6× your guess"; (c) new plan durations and backward-planner milestones apply the multiplier. [Impl #14; §1 Executive function: Inferences]
- **F15 Waiting-mode protocol.** AC: (a) any appointment can get a hard "stop and go" alarm with buffer; (b) the gap before it offers "side quests" that fit the remaining time; (c) multiple alarms per appointment are supported; (d) when an appointment is created, an early-in-day slot and backward time estimation can be suggested. [Impl #15; §1 Executive function: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- **F16 "First tiny step" starter.** AC: (a) any task can be split into micro-steps by AI or by a template; (b) a 2-minute "just start" timer launches from the first step; (c) an optional body-double or social-presence session can be started. [Impl #16]
- **F17 Guided planning ritual.** AC: (a) completes in 2 to 3 minutes and is offered morning or evening; (b) the user picks at most 3 items; (c) the user sets an "ideal" and a "baseline" version of the day; (d) the user sequences the items. [Impl #17; §1 Executive function: Inferences]
- **F18 Staged transition cues.** AC: haptic cues at 10, 5 and 1 minutes before a switch; a "park your thought" quick capture is reachable directly from the cue. [Impl #18]
- **F19 Hyperfocus guardrail.** AC: (a) during a focus block the Watch taps at set intervals showing elapsed time; (b) it escalates to "stand, drink, eat"; (c) long scroll sessions are detected. [Impl #19]
- **F20 Context-triggered reminders.** AC: (a) triggers include arriving at or leaving a location, connecting to CarPlay and a Focus mode change; (b) leaving home shows "keys, wallet, meds?"; (c) that prompt offers a Find My hand-off. [Impl #20; §7 Household and admin: Inferences]
- **F21 Three-second universal capture.** AC: capture via Siri, the Action button, Watch dictation or the share sheet lands in one inbox in under 3 seconds; items are triaged later. [Impl #21; §15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences]
- **F22 Forgiving progress model.** AC: no destructive streaks; progress shown as weekly percentages; pause days; streak insurance; a missed day never resets accumulated progress. [Impl #22; §2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings]
- **F23 Overdue amnesty / reset day.** AC: one tap reschedules or archives every overdue item, red counts are hidden, and the confirmation uses kind microcopy. [Impl #23; §2 Emotional dysregulation: Inferences]
- **F24 "Brain weather" capacity check-in.** AC: one tap records capacity; a low setting collapses the day to the baseline plan and reduces notifications. [Impl #24]
- **F25 Designed-for-lapse re-engagement.** AC: (a) after a break of 1 to 3 weeks, a "welcome back" flow reaches a running plan in under 30 seconds; (b) no backlog or missed count is shown; (c) optional novelty rotation of themes, widget faces and reward variety. [Impl #25; §2 Emotional dysregulation: Inferences]
- **F26 Wind-down and protected me-time block.** AC: (a) wind-down is worked back from the required wake time; (b) an earlier guilt-free leisure block is scheduled; (c) a Screen Time shield starts at the cut-off. [Impl #26; §3 Sleep: Inferences]
- **F27 Morning launch sequence.** AC: timed sub-steps (dose, water, breakfast, hygiene, leave-by) advance with haptic progression. [Impl #27; §3 Sleep: Inferences; §10 Health and self-care: Inferences]
- **F28 Caffeine log.** AC: caffeine entries with a personal cut-off time, a nudge at the cut-off, and a view of caffeine alongside sleep. [Impl #28; §3 Sleep: Inferences]

## 6. Algorithms, parameters, defaults and thresholds

- Dose reminder trigger = detected wake time plus user offset; test case: waking at 10:30 must not produce an 08:00 reminder. [§3 Sleep: Inferences; Impl #2]
- Wake source order: Watch sleep data, then first unlock where detectable, then manual (extractor note on ordering). [Impl #2]
- Late-dose check: if the dose time is after the latest-dose cut-off, show the cut-off banner; evidence anchor: lisdexamfetamine efficacy up to 14 h post-dose, insomnia 11 to 19% vs 4 to 5% placebo. [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings; Impl #3]
- Coverage curve: per-formulation onset, peak and wear-off entered by the user and positioned from the actual dose time; the only reference values in the source are lisdexamfetamine onset about 1.5 h and efficacy up to 14 h. [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings; Impl #4]
- Landing alert lead: 30 min before wear-off (source example). [Impl #5]
- Rebound window: about 60 minutes from wear-off, typically mid-to-late afternoon; possible with long-acting formulations; used to warn about hard conversations and social plans. [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #5]
- Meal anchors: before the dose; lunch inside coverage "even if not hungry"; protein snack before wear-off; evening meal preparation before rebound. [Impl #6; §4 Eating: Inferences]
- Hydration reminders default ON (dry mouth 26 to 32% vs 1 to 8%); frequency not specified. [§4 Eating: Inferences]
- Wind-down: start worked backwards from the required wake time; me-time block placed earlier in the evening; Screen Time shield at the cut-off; durations not specified. [Impl #26]
- Caffeine: personal cut-off time, nudge at the cut-off, plotted against sleep. [Impl #28; §3 Sleep: Inferences]
- Estimation multiplier: learned ratio of actual to estimated duration (example 1.6×), applied to plan durations and milestone buffers; minimum sample size and aggregation method not specified. [Impl #14; Impl #33]
- Leave-by = arrival time minus travel time minus a personal lateness buffer learned from history. [Impl #13]
- Waiting mode: hard stop-and-go alarm at leave-by with buffer; side quests offered only if they fit the remaining gap. [Impl #15]
- Transition cues at T-10, T-5 and T-1 minutes. [Impl #18]
- Hyperfocus guardrail: taps at set intervals with elapsed time, then escalation to "stand, drink, eat"; interval and escalation point not specified. [Impl #19]
- "Just start" timer: 2 minutes. [Impl #16]
- Planning ritual: 2 to 3 minutes; maximum 3 items. [Impl #17]
- Maximum visible focus items: 3. [§15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences; Impl #37]
- Capture latency budget: under 3 seconds end to end. [§15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences; Impl #21]
- "Did I take it?" latency budget: under 2 seconds from the wrist. [§5 Medication: Inferences]
- Re-engagement: expected lapse 1 to 3 weeks; welcome-back to a running plan in under 30 seconds. [Impl #25; §2 Emotional dysregulation: Inferences]
- Novelty wears off by around week 2, so rotation must be available before then; cadence not specified. [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Inferences]
- Capacity: a low check-in switches to the baseline plan and reduces notifications. [Impl #24]
- Notification budget: small daily maximum (number not specified), bundling, escalation only for dose and leave-by, haptic-first on the Watch; low capacity reduces it further. [Impl #36; Impl #24]

## 7. Content and copy rules (tone, words to avoid, must-say / never-say)

- Never recommend a dose change; anything touching dose must record, visualise and prompt a conversation with the prescriber. [Impl intro; §5 Medication: Inferences]
- Features are product ideas, not medical advice; no copy may read as medical advice. [Impl intro]
- Position the app as practical support for daily function and for communication with the prescriber; never call it a treatment and make no symptom-reduction claims (digital interventions show only g=-0.32). [Impl positioning note; §15 Accessibility and UX: Takeaway]
- Use a support framing, not a deficit framing. [§15 Accessibility and UX: Takeaway, Cited Findings]
- Mood features are signposting only; no therapy claims. [§13 Comorbidities: Inferences; Impl #40]
- Rejection sensitivity may inform tone but must never be framed as a diagnosis (it is not in DSM-5-TR and has no validated measure). [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- Never scold; no public comparison; compassionate, kind microcopy; returning users see "welcome back" with no backlog guilt. [§2 Emotional dysregulation: Inferences; Impl #23; Impl #25]
- Invitational, not commanding: avoid imperative "Do X now!" phrasing. [§13 Comorbidities: Inferences; Impl #38]
- Must-say (late dose): "this is later than your usual cut-off; consider checking with your prescriber". [Impl #3]
- Must-say (duplicate log): "already logged today". [Impl #1]
- Glance format: "Taken 08:02 · 3 h ago". [Impl #1; §5 Medication: Inferences]
- Calibration example: "you usually take 1.6× your guess". [Impl #14]
- Discreet notification example: "Morning ✓" instead of the drug name. [Impl #39]
- Travel prompt: "home time or local time?" for dose reminders. [Impl #34]
- Departure prompt: "keys, wallet, meds?". [Impl #20]
- Eating prompts: "ate something?" and a lunch "even if not hungry". [Impl #6]
- Hyperfocus escalation: "stand, drink, eat". [Impl #19]
- Scroll check-in (optional): "escaping something?" or "what are you escaping from?". [§11 Digital distraction: Inferences; Impl #35]
- Buddy ping: "I'm starting now". [Impl #44]
- Cycle flag: "menstruation/late-luteal weeks look harder", always framed as something to discuss with the prescriber. [Impl #9]
- Never surface the premenstrual 30 to 50% dose-increase case series, or any cycle-based dosing, as a suggestion; surface the pattern and prompt a prescriber conversation. [§12 Sex and gender: Cited Findings, Inferences]
- Never suggest adding or timing a booster; the source describes boosters as clinician-managed, so the app may only log a booster the prescriber set (extractor note). [§5 Medication: Cited Findings]
- Never recommend melatonin doses or light therapy (e.g. the 0.5 mg melatonin finding) even though the research mentions them; route sleep questions to the clinician (extractor note). [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings]
- Shortage mode prompts contacting the prescriber about alternatives and never suggests alternatives itself. [Impl #8]
- Travel mode prompts the user to confirm a plan with their prescriber before a trip. [§14 Life transitions: Inferences]
- Planned medication breaks must never be labelled "missed". [Impl #34; §14 Life transitions: Inferences]
- The ADHD-tax log is non-judgemental; tone follows "stop blaming ourselves for paying it". [§6 Money: Inferences; §2 Emotional dysregulation: Cited Findings]
- Relational features say "companionship rather than surveillance" in spirit and wording. [§15 Accessibility and UX: Cited Findings; Impl #44]
- Do not present self-selected survey percentages as population prevalence, and label or avoid secondary statistics (e.g. "57% miss loan payments") in any in-app content. [Scope note; §6 Money: Cited Findings]
- Make no quantified claims about caffeine and stimulant interaction (no source quantified it), and do not shame caffeine amount (amount is not associated with ADHD symptoms). [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; §3 Sleep: Cited Findings]
- "Waiting mode" is not a clinical term and must not be presented as a diagnosis or clinical fact (extractor note). [§1 Executive function: Cited Findings]
- Keep reading load low: short copy, icon- and colour-led, with voice output available. [§15 Accessibility and UX: Inferences; §13 Comorbidities: Inferences]
- Psychoeducation cards: about 60 seconds each, on onset, wear-off, rebound, sleep and appetite, in context, supporting informed talks with the prescriber. [Impl #41]

## 10. Edge cases, failure modes and their mitigations

- Late wake (e.g. 10:30) against a fixed 08:00 reminder → wake-anchored reminder. [§3 Sleep: Inferences; Impl #2]
- No Watch or no sleep data → fall back to first unlock or a manual "I'm up" (extractor note on the manual step). [Impl #2]
- Dose taken late carries up to 14 h into bedtime → cut-off banner, never blocking. [§3 Sleep: Cited Findings; Impl #3]
- User unsure whether they took the dose → glance within 2 seconds; the second tap warns. [§5 Medication: Inferences; Impl #1]
- Multi-dose days (e.g. booster, long-acting, booster) → "already logged today" must apply per scheduled dose slot, not per medication per day (extractor note). [Impl #1]
- Shift workers dosing after midnight → "today" follows the wake-anchored day, not the calendar date (extractor note). [§8 Study and work: Inferences; Impl #1]
- Rebound during a social plan or hard conversation → landing alert and scheduling warning. [Impl #5; §5 Medication: Cited Findings]
- Lunch skipped during appetite suppression → scheduled lunch "even if not hungry". [Impl #6]
- Evening rebound hunger → evening meal preparation prompt. [§4 Eating: Inferences]
- Hyperfocus through meals and water → guardrail escalation "stand, drink, eat". [Impl #19]
- Waiting mode before an appointment → stop-and-go alarm with buffer plus side quests. [Impl #15]
- Lapse of 1 to 3 weeks → welcome back in under 30 seconds with no backlog. [Impl #25]
- Pile of overdue items → one-tap amnesty. [Impl #23]
- Broken streak → no destructive streaks; weekly percentages. [Impl #22]
- Low-capacity day → baseline plan and fewer notifications. [Impl #24]
- Notification overload and habituation → daily cap, bundling, escalation only for dose and leave-by. [Impl #36]
- Checking the plan on the phone leads to a rabbit hole → Watch-first and widget-first checks. [§11 Digital distraction: Inferences]
- Long scroll session → optional escape check-in; shields in planned blocks. [Impl #35]
- Weekend or holiday wake drift → weekend wake-anchor protection. [§14 Life transitions: Inferences]
- Semester vs break routines → automatic mode switching from calendar data. [Impl #34]
- Wrong automatic mode switch → manual override (extractor note). [Impl #34]
- Crossing time zones → ask "home time or local time?", explain the behaviour, prompt a prescriber check. [Impl #34; §14 Life transitions: Inferences]
- Planned drug holiday → not logged as missed and excluded from adherence. [Impl #34]
- Supply runs out or a shortage hits → shortage mode, pharmacy log, TGA link, prescriber prompt. [Impl #8]
- Rationing or skipping doses because of supply (70% in the UK) → allow "skipped" with a neutral supply reason and no shame (extractor note). [§5 Medication: Cited Findings]
- Expired medication used during shortage (10% in the UK) → the source tracks script expiry only; pack expiry is not covered (extractor note). [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #7]
- Script cannot be filled in another state or territory → state rule notes. [§5 Medication: Cited Findings]
- NSW paper Schedule 8 script tied to one pharmacy → same-pharmacy note, with temporary transfers now permitted. [§5 Medication: Cited Findings; Impl #7]
- Repeats exhausted or script expiring → "new script needed" appointment prompt. [Impl #7; Impl #42]
- Safety Net resets in January → January reminder. [§14 Life transitions: Inferences]
- Prescriber changes (psychiatrist to GP continuation prescriber) → history kept. [§16 Australian context: Inferences]
- Distress during medication gaps → mood safety net. [Impl #40]

- Leaving home without keys or meds → departure prompt. [Impl #20]
- Anxious or autistic users overwhelmed by stimulation → Calm mode. [Impl #38]
