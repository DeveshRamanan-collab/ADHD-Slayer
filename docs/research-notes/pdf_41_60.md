## 1. Features, screens and flows (with acceptance criteria)

### 1.1 Onboarding and permissions
- **Onboarding under 3 minutes.** It asks for exactly three things: calendar permission, the user's medication with their own estimate of its focus window, and three priorities. Everything else is inferred or preset. AC: a new user reaches a planned Today view in under 3 minutes, and nothing else is mandatory. [p59]
- **Progressive, in-context permissions.** Each permission gets its own screen and is asked in context, never all at once during onboarding. Calendar comes first, Health at the first check-in, location when the first errand is added. AC: onboarding shows only the calendar request; the Health request first appears at the first check-in; the location request first appears when the first errand is created. [p57]
- **Per-medication Health grant.** HealthKit grants medication read access one medication at a time, so import must work when the user shares only some medications. AC: with a partial grant, import works for the shared medications and never errors or claims the others do not exist. [p41]

### 1.2 Today view and planning behaviour
- **Capacity cap.** The planner fills at most about 60% of free focus time, and always includes transitions between blocks and a floating buffer. AC: in any generated plan, planned flexible minutes divided by free focus-window minutes is at most about 0.6; every pair of adjacent blocks has a transition; a floating buffer exists. [p59]
- **Separate, hideable app calendar.** Every block the app creates goes to one dedicated app calendar that the user can hide. AC: no app block is written to any other calendar; hiding that calendar in Apple Calendar hides all app blocks and nothing else. [p42, p59]
- **Baseline day one tap away.** The baseline day (defined elsewhere in the doc) is always one tap from Today. AC: one tap from Today shows the baseline day. [p59]
- **A reason on every placement.** Every block the app places shows why it is there, taken from the solver record (rule, cost terms, evidence). AC: opening any app-placed block shows a non-empty reason that matches the stored change record. [p55, p60]
- **Suggest-then-confirm for the first 2 weeks.** For the first 14 days after install the planner proposes changes and applies them only after the user confirms. AC: during days 1 to 14 no move is written without an explicit confirm. [p60]
- **Pinned and user-created items never move.** The planner never moves an item the user pinned or created. AC: no automatic change set changes the time of a pinned or user-created item. [p60]
- **Daily cap on automatic moves.** The planner makes only a limited number of automatic moves per day. The cap's value is not given on these pages. AC: automatic moves in a day never exceed the configured cap. [p60]
- **Daily "what moved" digest.** A digest lists what the app moved that day. AC: every automatic move made that day appears in the digest. [p60]
- **One-tap undo.** Every change set, including Fix my day, can be undone with one tap. AC: one tap restores the state before the last change set. [p48, p60]
- **"Plan with me" mode.** A mode where the user and the app build the day together instead of the app planning it alone, so the user builds the skill rather than depending on the app. [p60]
- **Routine graduation.** A routine can "graduate" as part of building skill rather than dependence. The mechanics are not given on these pages. [p60]
- **Lapse-proof re-entry, amnesty and fresh start.** Coming back after a lapse never confronts the user with a backlog of failures; amnesty and a fresh start are offered. AC: opening the app after several unused days offers a fresh start and shows no red counts or broken-streak messages. [p59, p60]
- **Value without opening the app.** The plan reaches the user through the Live Activity, the Watch complication and calendar blocks. AC: Now, Next and the focus window are visible on the Lock Screen and the Watch without launching the app. [p59]
- **Micro-sessions.** Core interactions are designed to take under 3 minutes. AC: the key flows (log, check-in, fix my day, review) each finish in under 3 minutes in usability tests. [p59]
- **Optional themes and rotating wording.** Optional visual themes, plus copy that rotates, to counter boredom and prompt fatigue. [p59]
- **Stable navigation contract.** The primary navigation stays the same across releases, and new features are opt-in. AC: an update never moves or renames an existing primary destination; a new feature stays off until the user turns it on. [p60]

### 1.3 Input: commands, references, voice, sharing, search
- **Command and chat input (inv. 2).** A fixed grammar runs first and handles short forms such as "gym tomorrow 3", "took meds" and "skip gym" with no model at all. Anything else goes to the on-device model, which maps the text to fixed command types. Long inputs go to Private Cloud Compute. AC: the three example phrases work with Apple Intelligence unavailable and with no network. [p47, p56]
- **Interpretation preview.** Before any medium- or high-risk change, the app shows how it understood the request. AC: no medium- or high-risk command is committed without first showing the interpretation and getting a confirm. [p47]
- **References (inv. 2).** Code, not the model, resolves "move that" and "same as last Tuesday", using a stack of recently mentioned items and a lookup of dated history. When two candidates are close, the app asks and shows both. AC: an ambiguous "that" produces a two-option question, never a silent guess. [p47]
- **Voice and Siri (inv. 2, 66).** Every core intent has a fixed-phrase App Shortcut that works on every device, on the Watch and through AirPods. Siri AI through iOS 27 app schemas is a bonus and is never required. AC: every core intent can be triggered by its fixed phrase on an iPhone without Apple Intelligence. [p47]
- **Dose shortcuts.** "Log my dose" and "Log my booster" App Shortcuts work on every device, from Siri, Shortcuts and the Action button. [p42]
- **Email and booking ingestion (inv. 2).** The user shares from Mail or Gmail into the app. At launch, parsing happens on-device only, and every extracted booking needs the user's confirmation. AC: a shared email never creates an event or task without an explicit confirm. [p48]
- **Screenshots and photos (inv. 2, 10).** Vision table OCR runs on-device and parses into fixed fields, using the roster flow (1.10). [p47]
- **Search and questions (inv. 62).** The on-device model turns a question into a structured query, and code runs that query over the behaviour ledger. The interpreted query (for example "Gym sessions, last 30 days") is shown above the answer. AC: every answer is headed by its interpreted query. [p57]

### 1.4 Fix my day, running late, conflicts, what-if
- **Fix my day (inv. 17).** The pipeline is: interpret, apply rules, update estimates, solve, preview, execute, verify, explain. The 20 most common phrasings map to deterministic intents. A stability penalty means only the affected blocks move. One-tap undo. AC: a preview always comes before execution; blocks the request does not touch keep their times; an explanation follows execution. [p48]
- **Running late (inv. 67).** "Running 30 late" shifts downstream flexible blocks and leave-by times and leaves fixed items alone. When more than half of the flexible work no longer fits, the app offers the baseline day. AC: fixed items never move; the baseline-day offer appears once more than 50% of the flexible work no longer fits. [p58]
- **Conflict resolution (inv. 5).** Uses a priority ladder plus explicit exceptions, and never silently double-books. Questions are batched into one card with a recommended answer. An unanswered question keeps both items visible and never times out. Clashes caused by outside changes (such as a new meeting invite) are flagged, not auto-fixed. AC: the app never creates a double booking itself; a pending question persists until answered; an external clash shows a flag and nothing is changed. [p50]
- **Scenarios and what-if (inv. 32, 63).** The app clones state in memory, solves, and shows the diff. Nothing is written until the user approves. Only changed items and the net effect are shown, for example "gym moves to Sat, PIL 1 h short". AC: cancelling a scenario leaves the store, calendars and notifications unchanged. [p51]

### 1.5 Medication and dose logging
- **"I took my medication" (inv. 3).** Doses go into the app's own log, which is the source of truth. It supports backdating, a double-dose guard and de-duplication, including duplicates from Health import. The app never adjusts the dose time the user entered. AC: the stored time is exactly what the user entered or backdated to; an import that duplicates an existing log creates no second DoseEvent. [p41, p49]
- **Learned focus window.** The medication window is learned per person, not a fixed rule of T to T+9h (the model is in doc §7.2). AC: the window shown follows the user's check-in data rather than always spanning 9 h from the dose. [p49]
- **Optional Health dose import.** An anchored import of HealthKit medication dose events, de-duplicated by Health's own IDs, that copes with Health backdating, deleting and re-saving events. AC: importing the same Health events again, or after Health re-saves them, never creates duplicates. [p41]
- **Dose log starts the Live Activity.** The dose-log App Intent starts the focus-window Live Activity. AC: logging a dose from the app, a Shortcut or the Watch starts or updates the Live Activity. [p43]
- **Optional "hard" dose reminder.** The user can set a "hard" dose reminder that uses AlarmKit, which breaks through Silent and Focus. AC: it is off by default and exists only if the user creates it. [p43]
- **No medication actions.** Neither the app nor its AI ever takes or suggests a medication action. [p57]

### 1.6 Check-ins, energy and wake
- **Energy feedback (inv. 26).** Low, Normal or High, entered on the Watch, saved in the app and optionally written to Health as State of Mind. During the learning phase it is folded into the focus check-in; afterwards it is optional. AC: the learning-phase check-in includes energy; after the learning phase energy can be switched off. [p54]
- **Wake detection.** No API exposes the Sleep Schedule or wake alarm. Wake is detected from the app's own AlarmKit wake alarm, a Shortcuts "Wake Up" automation that calls an App Intent, the first Watch interaction, or HealthKit sleep stages when the Watch is worn at night. AC: with no Watch and no sleep data, wake is still detected from the app alarm or the Shortcut. [p42, p54]
- **Wake-anchored timing.** The daily briefing and wake-anchored reminders fire after the detected wake, not at a fixed clock time. [p46, p48]

### 1.7 Tasks, study and deadlines
- **Quick task entry (inv. 4).** The form asks only for title, rough duration and deadline. Category, energy, minimum and maximum block, split and location are inferred from the title and history, and all are editable later. AC: the create form shows only those three inputs, and the inferred fields are visible and editable afterwards. [p49]
- **Partial completion (inv. 18).** "Done for now" records the time spent and computes the remaining minutes (formula in section 4). The record is stored as censored data for learning. AC: after "Done for now" the task shows the computed remaining minutes and stays schedulable. [p51]
- **Uni study system (inv. 8).** Assessments have weight, workload, dependencies and focus demand. They are imported from the unit guide PDF (Vision plus the on-device model) and from the LMS calendar feed where one exists, and each item is confirmed. AC: no imported assessment is scheduled before the user confirms it. [p50]
- **Deadline and exam engine (inv. 9).** Plans backwards from the deadline in blocks sized with the user's P80 multiplier, with Exam mode as an overlay. An early forecast (for example "2 hours short by Thursday") always comes with a proposed fix. Milestones appear on the Lock Screen widget. AC: no shortfall forecast is ever shown without a fix. [p50]
- **Assessment escalation (inv. 34, 35).** Reminders for an assessment escalate at 7 days, 3 days and 1 day before, then the morning of. [p53]
- **Weekly capacity forecast (inv. 30, 31).** Capacity is computed as in section 4. Any shortfall is shown with a concrete fix, in neutral wording. [p51]

### 1.8 Movement, meals and routines
- **Movement policy (inv. 6).** Covers candidate slots and skip conditions; skips the rules cause are excluded from learning. A weekly movement floor with short fallbacks (for example a 20-minute walk before study) stops exam weeks from removing all exercise. Watch workout detection credits sessions automatically, and a one-tap "did it" covers sessions without a Watch. AC: in Exam mode the weekly movement floor is still met through fallbacks. [p50, p54]
- **Meals (inv. 7).** Meal anchors sit on the medication timeline. Lunch is a protected block, there is a snack before the window ends, and a one-tap "ate". AC: flexible work never displaces the protected lunch block. [p50]
- **Routines (inv. 7).** Each routine is tagged either calendar-worthy or checklist. Checklist habits live in a widget, not the calendar. AC: checklist routines never create calendar events. [p50]

### 1.9 Errands, travel and places
- **Travel time (inv. 11).** MapKit ETA (car, walk, transit) plus a learned overhead for each place (parking, lifts, walking in). If maps fail, the last buffer is kept. AC: with MapKit unavailable, leave-by times still use the last known buffer. [p52, p56]
- **Opening hours (inv. 12).** Hours come from Google Places details, shown on a Google map with attribution and fetched fresh before scheduling. Hours the user enters override them. On a conflict the app shows "conflicting hours, not scheduled yet". AC: an errand with conflicting hours is not scheduled, and that message is shown. [p44, p52]
- **Errand clustering (inv. 13).** A small route optimisation over pending errands near an existing trip, limited by free time and opening hours. [p52]
- **Location surfacing (inv. 36).** A geofence fires on arrival near an errand cluster, only when the place is open and the next hour is free, at most 1 to 2 location prompts a day. AC: never more than 2 location prompts in a day; none while the place is closed or the next hour is busy. [p53]
- **Leave reminders.** Reminders name the action ("Leave at 2:20"), and leave-now can use AlarmKit if the user opts in. [p43, p53]

### 1.10 Work shifts (roster screenshot import)
- **Roster ingestion (inv. 10).** Flow: screenshot, then Vision table OCR, find the user's row, extract shifts, diff against the Work calendar, confirm, write. The user taps their row once and the layout is remembered for that source. The idempotency key is employer + date + start. Existing matching events are adopted rather than duplicated, removed shifts are asked about, and shifts over 12 h need a confirm. It runs as a user-started long task with a system progress indicator. AC: importing the same screenshot twice creates no duplicates; a shift already added another way (for example by iOS 27 Visual Intelligence) is adopted; a shift over 12 h asks for confirmation. [p44, p45, p51, p56]

### 1.11 Briefing and reviews
- **Daily briefing (inv. 71).** Fires after the detected wake, at most 5 lines, with the day's 1 to 3 priorities first. AC: never more than 5 lines; priorities come first. [p48]
- **Weekly planning session (inv. 30).** A guided session of about 5 minutes that is optional and resumable. AC: leaving midway and coming back resumes at the same step. [p48]
- **End-of-day review (inv. 72).** Asks only about ambiguous items, with at most 3 one-tap questions. Skipping is fine and records the reason code "no answer". AC: never more than 3 questions; a skipped review writes "no answer". [p49]
- **Weekly review (inv. 54 to 56).** Opens with what worked, gives at most 3 findings, and pairs each finding with one proposed change. It teaches the pattern, not just the result. AC: at most 3 findings, each with exactly one proposed change. [p55, p60]

### 1.12 Learning transparency and control
- **Approval before rule changes (inv. 20 to 22).** Evidence thresholds and shrinkage toward defaults stop the app over-reading two examples, and any rule change needs the user's approval. AC: no learned rule changes without an approval event. [p54]
- **Provenance (inv. 50 to 53).** Every learned item shows where it came from. Stated preferences are strong priors, behaviour proposes changes, and a correction becomes a one-off, a preference or a hard rule. AC: every learned item shows its provenance. [p55]
- **Health-based adjustment (inv. 27).** Health signals adjust only discretionary load, and each adjustment is offered as "Accept adjustment?". AC: no health-based change is applied without acceptance. [p55]
- **Explainability (inv. 40, 74).** Every change stores the rule, cost terms and evidence that produced it. Explanations take their numbers and reasons from the solver record; the model only phrases them. [p55]
- **Missed items (inv. 23).** When the planner itself skipped an item, "blocked by rule" is inferred automatically. The user is asked only about real misses, with one-tap reason codes when something lapses. [p54]
- **Category split suggestion (inv. 28, 29).** When a duration category's spread is very wide, the app suggests splitting it. [p55]

### 1.13 Undo, audit and review lists
- **Undo and audit log (inv. 41, 73).** A before-and-after snapshot is kept for every change set, which enables "restore this morning's plan". A restore re-diffs against the current state and asks about anything edited since. AC: a restore after an outside edit asks about that edit before overwriting it. [p56]
- **"Needs review" list (inv. 14).** Outside items that cannot be matched go to a "needs review" list and are never silently deleted. [p52]
- **Outside edit summary (inv. 2, 15).** Edits the user makes in Calendar or Reminders are detected on the next launch or change notification, and their consequences are shown as one summary, not a stream. [p48]
- **Deletion undo.** Deletions keep a 30-day tombstone so they can be undone. [p52]
- **Honest write state (inv. 43, 60).** A task is never shown as rescheduled if the write failed. Written items show as "pending" until verified. [p56]

### 1.14 Settings: diagnostics, connectors, export, AI
- **Diagnostics page (inv. 76).** Shows the last successful read and write for each source. For Health types it says "no data seen since [time]" rather than "connected". AC: a Health type is never labelled "connected". [p56]
- **Connectors page (inv. 45, 46).** A status page for connectors, which are added one at a time. At launch the only connector is EventKit. [p53]
- **Export (inv. 75).** Exports everything as JSON and CSV. The app warns before exporting, leaves out Health-sourced data by default, and keeps the file on the device until the user shares it. AC: the default export contains no Health-sourced records, and the warning appears first. [p57]
- **Cloud AI limit explainer (inv. 57, 58).** When a user wants a cloud AI feature that would need medication, check-in or Health data, the app explains the limit and sends only derived summaries with no health detail. [p57]
- **Opt-in third-party AI (inv. 77, 78).** Third-party models (such as Claude or Gemini) are opt-in and only ever receive text with no health content. [p44, p58]

### 1.15 Lifestyles and modes
- **Daily toggling becomes a suggestion.** If the user switches lifestyle back and forth daily, the app suggests a mode or a weekly template (weekday vs weekend) instead. [p41]
- **Exam mode.** Exam mode is an overlay on the plan. [p50]
- **Study Focus.** A Study Focus filter starts the planned block and quiets nudges. [p43]

### 1.16 Surfaces and devices
- **Focus-window Live Activity.** Shown on the Lock Screen, Dynamic Island, Watch Smart Stack and Mac menu bar. It starts from the dose-log intent, a second activity is chained near 8 h, countdowns use dynamic date text, and state stays at 4 KB or less. [p43]
- **Block timers (inv. 68, 69).** Timers run in a Live Activity. Blocks start automatically at their planned time with a one-tap "not started", starting the next block stops the previous one, and the Watch taps on overrun. AC: no manual start or stop is needed for a timed block. [p58]
- **Widgets and complications (inv. 2, 64).** Show Now, Next and the focus window, with countdowns in dynamic date text. There is a checklist-habits widget and a Lock Screen widget with assessment milestones. [p48, p50]
- **Logging while the phone is locked.** Widget buttons do nothing while the phone is locked, so locked logging goes through Watch and AlarmKit actions. [p48]
- **iPhone is the hub.** It holds model state and does the planning, Live Activities, notifications and Health reads; the other devices depend on it. [p45]
- **Apple Watch app.** One-tap logs, check-ins, transition haptics, a complication, double-tap to log, and a distinct haptic for each event type. [p45, p60]
- **Control Center and Action button.** Core actions are exposed through ControlWidget and App Intents. [p42]
- **Mac app.** A planning console with week view, unscheduled queue, forecasts, rule editor, roster import and history, plus a menu-bar extra showing "now / next / focus window". The Mac has no Health data and no Screen Time, and AlarmKit only through Catalyst. [p45, p46]
- **iPad app.** The same as the Mac, plus Health and Screen Time. [p46]
- **Simple mode (low load).** Just Now, Next and Log, built on the AssistiveAccess scene and supporting Reduce Motion and Dynamic Type. [p45]
- **Distraction shields (v1.x).** Screen Time shields around planned blocks, using stateless extensions and registering only the next few blocks. [p43]
- **Reminders mirror.** Simple items and location alarms are mirrored into Apple Reminders; rich tasks stay in the app. [p42]
- **Tasks as system entities.** Tasks are exposed as Reminders and Calendar entities through iOS 27 app schemas. [p42]

## 2. Data model (entities, fields, types, retention)

- **Canonical object set.** The original inventory's objects stay, plus Medication, Product, DoseEvent, CheckIn, FocusWindowModel, Lifestyle, Mode, ReasonCode and ConsentRecord. Full schemas are in doc §16; Product and ConsentRecord fields are not defined on these pages. [p53]
- **Stable app identity.** Every app item has an app UUID that never changes. [p52]
- **Calendar identity map (local).** The app UUID is written into each linked event's URL field. A local map stores, for each linked item: the app UUID (UUID), the external ID (String), and the occurrence or start date (Date), because recurring occurrences share one external ID. eventIdentifier is treated as volatile because it changes when an event moves calendar. [p42, p52]
- **DoseEvent.** Fields: timestamp exactly as the user entered it (backdating allowed; the app never adjusts it), source (the app's own log or a Health import), the Health object ID for de-duplication, and kind (dose or booster). The app's own log is the source of truth. [p41, p42, p49]
- **Health import state.** Stores the HealthKit anchor for anchored queries and upserts by Health ID, because Health may backdate, delete and re-save events. [p41]
- **Health-derived values are nullable.** A missing value means unknown and is stored as null, never zero, because a denied permission looks the same as no data. [p41]
- **Energy value.** An enum of Low, Normal or High, stored in the app and optionally written to HealthKit as State of Mind. [p54]
- **Behaviour ledger (local event log).** Each event is tagged with its lifestyle, mode and reason code(s). Passive sources come first (timers, Watch workouts, calendar attendance), plus one-tap reason codes when something lapses. [p41, p54]
- **Lifestyle.** Every ledger event carries its lifestyle. Parameters are either specific to one lifestyle or shared, and only shared ones learn across lifestyles. Confidence decays with time since the lifestyle was last used. [p41]
- **Mode and weekly template.** Modes are temporary overlays (Exam mode is one). A weekly template (weekday vs weekend) is the alternative to toggling lifestyles daily. [p41, p50]
- **ReasonCode.** Includes "no answer" (a skipped end-of-day review) and "blocked by rule" (inferred when the planner itself skipped). Skips the rules cause are flagged so they are left out of learning. [p49, p50, p54]
- **Task.** The inventory defines 22 fields. Only three are asked: title (String), rough duration, deadline (Date). These are inferred and editable: category, energy, min block, max block, split (splittable) and location. Flags: pinned and user-created, both meaning "never auto-move". [p49, p60]
- **Task progress.** Time spent (minutes), remaining minutes (formula in section 4), and a censored-observation flag for learning. [p51]
- **Assessment.** Weight, workload, dependencies, focus demand, deadline, milestones and escalation state. Imported items stay unconfirmed until the user confirms them. [p50, p53]
- **Duration category.** About 10 at the start. Each keeps a learned multiplier (including P80) and spread statistics, which drive split suggestions. [p50, p51, p55]
- **Scheduled block.** Planned start and end; fixed or flexible; protected (for example lunch); auto-start state; a "not started" flag; actual start and stop from timers; placement reason; leave-by time where travel applies. [p50, p58, p60]
- **Routine.** Type is calendar-worthy or checklist; checklist routines are rendered in a widget, not the calendar. [p50]
- **Movement policy.** Candidate slots, skip conditions, a weekly movement floor, fallback options (for example a 20-minute walk before study), and credit from a Watch workout or a one-tap "did it". [p50, p54]
- **Meal anchors.** Anchors on the medication timeline, a protected lunch block, a snack before the window ends, and an "ate" log event. [p50]
- **Place.** A learned overhead (parking, lifts, walking in), user-entered opening hours (these override Google), a Google place ID (the only Google Places data that may be cached), and a relevance rank for the 20-slot geofence rotation. [p44, p52]
- **Errand.** A pending errand linked to a Place, scheduled within free time and opening hours. [p52]
- **Shift (from a roster).** Employer, date, start and end. The idempotency key is employer + date + start, built from normalised fields. Shifts over 12 h are flagged as implausible. [p51, p56]
- **Roster source layout.** The layout remembered for each source, including which row is the user's (set by one tap). [p51]
- **Import record.** Every import carries a deterministic key: source + external key, built from normalised fields. [p56]
- **Change set (audit log).** A before-and-after snapshot for each change set. Each change stores the rule, cost terms and evidence that produced it. Used for undo, "restore this morning's plan" and compensating rollback. [p55, p56]
- **Write verification state.** A written item stays "pending" until a re-read has seen it twice; a mismatch marks it for repair. [p56]
- **Tombstone.** A deleted item keeps a tombstone for 30 days so the deletion can be undone. Retention: 30 days, then purge. [p52]
- **Needs-review queue.** Outside items that could not be matched, kept until the user resolves them and never auto-deleted. [p52]
- **Learned item.** Provenance (stated preference, behaviour or correction), correction type (one-off, preference or hard rule), and an approval state required before any rule changes. [p54, p55]
- **Notification record.** Class (silent fix, inform after, ask before, urgent), whether it is exempt from the budget (leave-now, transition, user-set medication reminder), budget accounting, and whether the user ignored or acted on it (feeds the budget that learns). [p53, p58, p60]
- **Connector status (per source).** Last successful read time and last successful write time; for Health types, the last time data was seen. [p56]
- **Question card.** Batched conflict questions, each with a recommended answer and no expiry. [p50]
- **Scenario.** An in-memory clone of state; nothing is persisted unless the user approves it. [p51]
- **Live Activity content state.** Must fit in 4 KB and carry no network-dependent data (countdowns use dynamic date text). [p43]
- **Export.** JSON and CSV covering all data, with Health-sourced records left out by default. [p57]
- **Storage split.** General data syncs through SwiftData + CloudKit with encrypted fields. Personal health information is never stored in iCloud; it stays local and syncs only through the app's own end-to-end encrypted sync (doc §15). [p45]
- **Privacy classification.** Medication, check-in and Health data are locked to on-device or Private Cloud Compute processing whatever the user's settings. [p57]

## 3. Backend services, APIs, jobs, integrations

- **Architecture split.** "The LLM interprets, the rules engine constrains, the solver solves, the connectors execute, the behaviour engine learns": the model interprets and deterministic code decides. [p46, p57]
- **Event-driven upkeep, no periodic runtime.** The app re-plans on every foreground launch; inside App Intents (logging a dose, marking done, "fix my day"); in HealthKit observer callbacks (sleep ended, workout saved); on calendar change notifications after launch; in background refresh when iOS grants it; and from server silent pushes as a backstop. [p45, p53]
- **Silent-push backstop service.** A server sends at most about 3 silent pushes per hour per device, because iOS throttles above 3 an hour. Pushes are a backstop for upkeep, not the main path. [p43, p45, p53]
- **User-started long tasks.** The weekly re-plan and roster import run as BGContinuedProcessingTask, started by the user, with a system progress indicator. [p43, p45]
- **Opportunistic refresh.** BGAppRefreshTask gets about 30 s when the system chooses, so it must fit in that time and be safe to skip. [p43]
- **Health compute timing.** The Health store cannot be read while the phone is locked, so Health-based computation runs on unlock or in the foreground. [p41]
- **Calendar diff job.** Change notifications carry no diff, so each wake runs a full diff of the planning horizon against the app's identity map; linked tasks and events move together. [p42, p48, p52]
- **Notification scheduler job.** Keeps a rolling 48-hour window of local notifications (the capability map allows 24 to 48 h), refilled on every launch and event, and never above iOS's 64 pending notifications. [p43, p53, p60]
- **Transactional calendar writer.** Plan, validate, batch the saves with EventKit's deferred commit, commit, re-read. On failure it rolls back from the audit log with compensating writes; if the rollback fails, it lists the exact unresolved changes. [p56]
- **Post-write verifier.** Re-reads every written item right after commit and again on the next wake. [p56]
- **Model routing.** Grammar first, then the on-device Foundation Models, then Private Cloud Compute (long inputs, heavy parsing of health-adjacent content), then an opt-in third-party model. [p47, p58]
- **Private Cloud Compute (iOS 27).** Needs an entitlement. Free under 2M downloads in the Small Business Program. Per-user daily quotas. 32K context. [p44]
- **Third-party models.** Claude and Gemini packages behind the same API. Opt-in, text with no health content only, estimated at US$0.35 to $1.05 per active user per month. [p44, p58]
- **Google Places details API (opening hours).** Fetched fresh before scheduling, shown on a Google map with attribution, and only place IDs are cached. [p44, p52]
- **MapKit calculateETA.** Car, walking and transit ETAs, plus the learned overhead for each place. [p44, p52]
- **Vision document pipeline.** Vision RecognizeDocumentsRequest (table extraction), then a deterministic parse, on-device model clean-up, user confirmation, and a calendar write. Used for roster screenshots and unit guide PDFs. Visual Intelligence hands the app only a pixel buffer, which then enters the same pipeline. [p44, p50, p51]
- **LMS calendar feed import.** Assessments are imported from the LMS calendar feed where one exists, and each is confirmed. [p50]
- **Launch connectors: EventKit only.** Apple Calendar and Reminders through EventKit, which already sees Google and Exchange accounts added in iOS Settings. [p53]
- **Later connectors.** Gmail or Outlook connection, Notion, Drive and LMS feeds, added one at a time with a status page. Direct Google APIs need OAuth verification; Gmail's restricted scopes may need an independent security assessment. [p48, p53]
- **Reminders mirror.** Simple items and location alarms are written to Apple Reminders; subtasks, flags, sections and tags stay in the app, because the API cannot hold them. [p42, p52]
- **Sync services.** General data uses SwiftData + CloudKit with encrypted fields. Health data uses the app's own end-to-end encrypted sync (doc §15). [p45]
- **Offline queue.** Local-only mode queues changes and replays them when writes are possible again. [p56]
- **Search executor.** The model outputs a structured query; code runs it over the local ledger. [p57]
- **Diagnostics collector.** Records the last successful read and write for each source (for example calendar, reminders and each Health type). [p56]

## 4. Algorithms and parameters (every number)

- **Platform baseline.** Minimum iOS 26. iOS 27 (released 14 September 2026) is an upgrade that adds the Private Cloud Compute model for third-party apps, 8K on-device context and Siri app schemas. [p41]
- **Lifestyle learning.** Every ledger event carries its lifestyle, and only shared parameters learn across lifestyles, so holiday data does not leak into term estimates. [p41]
- **Lifestyle confidence decay.** Confidence decays with time since last use, so that after a year's gap this year's data dominates within a week. The decay function is not specified. [p41]
- **Toggle detection.** Switching lifestyle back and forth daily triggers a suggestion of a mode or a weekly template (weekday vs weekend). The threshold is not specified. [p41]
- **HealthKit background delivery.** Hourly on iPhone; 4 times an hour on the Watch when a complication is active. [p41]
- **Watch complication.** Also buys 4 background wakes an hour. [p45]
- **Live Activity limits.** 8 h active plus 4 h on the Lock Screen; 4 KB state; no network; buttons need an unlock. A second activity is chained near 8 h. [p43]
- **Local notification cap.** iOS keeps only the 64 soonest local notifications. The app schedules a rolling 24 to 48 h window, and 48 h is used in the reminder and risk sections. [p43, p53, p60]
- **Background runtime.** About 30 s refresh at the system's discretion; silent pushes throttled above 3 an hour; a server backstop of at most about 3 an hour. [p43, p45, p53]
- **Screen Time extensions.** 6 MB memory each; a known threshold bug on iOS 26.2; register only the next few blocks. [p43]
- **Geofences.** CLMonitor allows 20 conditions per app, so the app rotates the 20 most relevant places or hands location alarms to Reminders. [p44, p52]
- **On-device model context.** 4,096 tokens on iOS 26 and 8,192 on iOS 27 (newer devices); Private Cloud Compute has 32K. The on-device model is rate-limited in the background (TN3193). [p44, p47]
- **Private Cloud Compute economics.** Free under 2M downloads in the Small Business Program, with per-user daily quotas. [p44]
- **Device reach.** iOS 27 runs on iPhone 11 and later. Apple Intelligence and Siri AI need iPhone 15 Pro or later; Siri AI is English only at launch and not in the EU or China. [p42, p46]
- **Command parsing order.** Grammar first (no model), then the on-device model into fixed command types, then Private Cloud Compute for long inputs. Medium- and high-risk changes are shown before they apply. [p47]
- **Reference resolver.** A stack of recently mentioned items plus a lookup of dated history. If two candidates are close, the app asks with both shown. The closeness threshold is not specified. [p47]
- **Fix my day.** Deterministic intents for the 20 most common phrases; a stability penalty so only affected blocks move. [p48]
- **Widget budget.** 40 to 70 widget reloads a day, so countdowns use dynamic date text rather than reloads. [p48]
- **Briefing and reviews.** Briefing at most 5 lines with 1 to 3 priorities; weekly planning about 5 minutes; end-of-day review at most 3 one-tap questions; weekly review at most 3 findings, each with 1 proposed change. [p48, p49, p55]
- **Medication window.** A fixed window of T to T+9h is wrong for most people; the window is learned per person (doc §7.2). [p49]
- **Task inference.** Of the 22 inventory fields, 3 are asked (title, rough duration, deadline). Category, energy, min and max block, split and location are inferred from the title and history. [p49]
- **Solver.** A small on-device constraint solver (for example Google OR-Tools CP-SAT compiled for iOS, or a custom search). Hard constraints come first, then weighted soft costs, plus a "minimal change" cost. The next 2 hours are frozen and as few blocks as possible move. The choice is validated in a spike. [p49]
- **Constraints named on these pages.** Stated as "never": pinned and user-created items never move, fixed items are untouched when running late, and nothing is silently double-booked. Named without saying hard or soft: a protected lunch block, mandatory transitions, a floating buffer, and at most about 60% fill of free focus time. [p50, p58, p59, p60]
- **Conflict policy.** A priority ladder plus explicit exceptions. Questions are batched with a recommended answer. Unanswered questions have no timeout. Outside clashes are flagged, not fixed. [p50]
- **Movement.** A weekly movement floor with short fallbacks (a 20-minute walk before study). Skips the rules cause are excluded from learning, and Watch workouts credit automatically. The rationale is the acute effect of exercise on focus (g = 0.55). [p50, p54]
- **Deadline block sizing.** Blocks are sized with the user's P80 duration multiplier, planning backwards from the deadline. [p50]
- **Remaining time.** Remaining = original estimate x learned multiplier - time spent, and the observation is treated as censored for learning. [p51]
- **Capacity.** Capacity = free hours inside predicted focus windows x completion probability. [p51]
- **Capacity cap.** Fill at most about 60% of free focus time, plus mandatory transitions and a floating buffer. Buffer and transition lengths are not given on these pages. [p59]
- **Running late.** Downstream flexible blocks and leave-by times shift by the stated delay (for example 30 min). If more than half of the flexible work no longer fits, the app offers the baseline day. [p58]
- **Roster idempotency.** Key = employer + date + start; existing matching events are adopted; shifts over 12 h need a confirm. [p51, p56]
- **Import keys.** Source + external key, built from normalised fields so that an OCR misread such as "1-4" read as "1-14" is caught. [p56]
- **Identity matching.** Match by the UUID in the event's URL first, then by external ID + start date. Anything unmatched goes to "needs review" and is never deleted. [p52]
- **Sync collisions.** On low-risk items the latest edit wins; high-risk items ask the user. Deletions keep a 30-day tombstone. [p52]
- **Post-write verification.** An item is "pending" until seen twice; a mismatch triggers a repair, not a duplicate. [p56]
- **Assessment escalation.** 7 days, then 3 days, then 1 day, then the morning of. Escalations count against the daily budget, except leave-now and medication reminders. [p53]
- **Location prompts.** At most 1 to 2 a day, only when the place is open and the next hour is free. [p53]
- **Travel.** MapKit ETA plus a learned overhead for each place; if maps fail, the last buffer is kept. [p52, p56]
- **Duration categories.** Start with about 10. Suggest a split when a category's spread is very wide; the threshold is not specified. [p55]
- **Pattern learning.** Evidence thresholds, shrinkage toward defaults, and user approval before any rule changes; never learn a rule from 2 examples. [p54]
- **Reason inference.** "Blocked by rule" is set automatically when the planner itself skipped an item. [p54]
- **Stated preferences vs behaviour.** Stated preferences are strong priors, behaviour proposes changes, and corrections become a one-off, a preference or a hard rule. [p55]
- **Notification budget.** Decision-point alerts only, within a daily budget (the value is in doc §5.7). Leave-now, transition and user-set medication reminders are exempt; everything else batches. The budget learns which prompts get ignored. [p58, p60]
- **Onboarding and sessions.** Onboarding under 3 minutes with 3 priorities; micro-sessions under 3 minutes. [p59]
- **Trust ramp.** Suggest-then-confirm for the first 2 weeks, then automatic moves up to a daily cap (value not given). [p60]
- **Third-party AI cost.** Roughly US$0.35 to $1.05 per active user per month; on-device and Private Cloud Compute cost the developer nothing within programme limits. [p58]
- **Design-rationale statistics (not tunable).** Mental-health apps keep 3.9% of users active at day 15 and 3.3% at day 30. Prompt effects fade to zero by day 28. Boredom correlates with ADHD at r = 0.40. Emotional dysregulation effect size g = 1.17. Broken streaks cut continuation from 65.8% to 48.0%. [p59, p60]

## 7. Privacy, security, safety, compliance (build-relevant)

- **No personal health information in iCloud (App Review 5.1.3(ii)).** Health data stays on the device and syncs only through the app's own end-to-end encrypted sync (doc §15). [p45]
- **Medication, check-in and Health data are locked to on-device or Private Cloud Compute processing**, whatever the user's settings. [p57]
- **Cloud AI gets derived summaries only**, with no health detail: "a light day", never "slept 5 h, HRV low". [p57]
- **Third-party models only for text with no health content**, and only after the user opts in. Private Cloud Compute handles heavy health-adjacent parsing. [p44, p58]
- **Calendars are not end-to-end encrypted (TN3153)**, so every calendar event the app writes has a neutral title. [p42]
- **No drug names on the Lock Screen.** [p43]
- **No condition or medication name in any health-based notification** (wellness rules bar condition-linked alerts). [p55]
- **Top approval class: medication actions are never taken or suggested.** [p57]
- **No medication-action field in any model output type** (doc §8.4), so model output cannot carry medication advice. [p57]
- **The app never adjusts a logged dose time.** [p49]
- **Health signals adjust only discretionary load**, always through "Accept adjustment?". [p55]
- **Never infer cognition from sleep.** [p54]
- **Missing Health data is treated as unknown, never zero**, and the UI never claims a Health type is "connected" (a denied permission looks the same as no data). [p41, p56]
- **Untrusted email content.** Emails can carry hidden instructions (prompt injection), so parse on-device and never act on email content without the user confirming each extracted booking. [p48]
- **Explanations come from records.** Numbers and reasons come from the solver's stored record; the model only phrases them, because model-written explanations can be wrong. [p55]
- **Permission transparency.** One screen per permission, asked in context, never everything at once. [p57]
- **Export safety.** Warn before exporting, leave out Health-sourced data by default, and keep the file on the device until the user shares it. [p57]
- **Google Places terms.** Opening hours are shown only on a Google map with attribution, and nothing but place IDs is cached. [p44, p52]
- **Gmail connector compliance.** Restricted scopes need Google app verification, possibly including an independent security assessment, before a Gmail connector ships. [p48]
- **Entitlements to obtain.** HealthKit medication read (possibly entitlement-gated), Screen Time (FamilyControls) for the app and each extension, and Private Cloud Compute. [p43, p44, p46]
- **The behaviour ledger and search run locally.** Queries run on-device over the local ledger. [p54, p57]
- **Trust safeguards.** Pinned and user-created items never move; suggest-then-confirm for the first 2 weeks; a reason on every placement; one-tap undo. [p60]

- **Launch voice.** Fixed-phrase App Shortcuts for all core intents; Siri AI through app schemas is an optional bonus on supported devices. [p42, p47]
- **After launch.** Gmail or Outlook connection, Notion, Drive and LMS feeds, one connector at a time, each with a status page. [p48, p53]
- **v1.x.** Distraction shields (FamilyControls, ManagedSettings, DeviceActivity), not in v1.0. [p43]
- **Per-user phase: first 2 weeks.** Suggest-then-confirm only; after that, automatic moves up to a daily cap. [p60]
- **Per-user phase: learning phase.** The energy check-in is folded into the focus check-in, and becomes optional afterwards. [p54]
- **Release process.** A TestFlight beta group for changes; new features opt-in; a stable navigation contract. [p60]

## 12. Edge cases, failure modes, mitigations

- **User switches lifestyles back and forth daily.** Mitigation: treat the pattern as a mode or a weekly template (weekday vs weekend), and have the app suggest one. [p41]
- **Holiday data leaks into term estimates.** Mitigation: lifestyle-tagged ledger events; only shared parameters learn across lifestyles. [p41]
- **Stale lifestyle after a year.** Mitigation: confidence decays with time since last use, so current data dominates within a week. [p41]
- **Health dose writes are impossible** (third parties cannot write doses). Mitigation: the app's own log is the source of truth, with an optional import. [p41, p46]
- **Health backdates, deletes or re-saves events.** Mitigation: anchored import de-duplicated by Health IDs. [p41]
- **Health store unreadable while locked.** Mitigation: compute on unlock or in the foreground. [p41]
- **Denied permission looks like no data.** Mitigation: treat missing as unknown; show "no data seen since". [p41, p56]
- **No API for wake time.** Mitigation: the app's AlarmKit wake alarm, the Shortcuts "Wake Up" automation, the first Watch interaction, or Health sleep when the Watch is worn at night. [p42, p54]
- **Calendar change notifications carry no diff, and iOS may not wake the app.** Mitigation: a full diff of the planning horizon on every wake. [p42, p48]
- **eventIdentifier changes when an event moves calendar; recurring events share one external ID; Exchange IDs differ by device.** Mitigation: UUID in the URL field, a local map with occurrence dates, and "needs review" for anything unmatched. [p42, p52]
- **Reminders cannot hold subtasks, flags, sections or tags.** Mitigation: rich tasks stay in the app; only simple items and location alarms are mirrored. [p42, p52]
- **No health or medication Siri schema.** Mitigation: fixed-phrase "Log my dose / booster" shortcuts. [p42, p47]
- **Siri AI limited to iPhone 15 Pro+, English, and not the EU or China.** Mitigation: fixed phrases for every core intent. [p42, p47]
- **Live Activity limits (8 h, 4 KB, no network, buttons need an unlock).** Mitigation: chain a second activity near 8 h; dynamic date countdowns; Watch and AlarmKit for locked actions. [p43, p48]
- **Only the 64 soonest local notifications are kept.** Mitigation: a rolling 48 h window refilled on every launch and event. [p43, p53]
- **Critical alerts are unavailable.** Mitigation: AlarmKit, opt-in, for chosen critical moments only. [p43]
- **Screen Time constraints (entitlement per extension, 6 MB, iOS 26.2 threshold bug, not on the Mac).** Mitigation: defer to v1.x; stateless extensions; register only the next few blocks. [p43]
- **No reliable background time.** Mitigation: event-driven upkeep plus a silent push backstop at most about 3 an hour. [p43, p45]
- **Only 20 geofences.** Mitigation: rotate the 20 most relevant, or hand location alarms to Reminders. [p44, p52]
- **MapKit has no opening hours, and Google data cannot be cached.** Mitigation: Google Places shown on a compliant map, fetched fresh; user hours override. [p44, p52]
- **Public holiday hours are often wrong.** Mitigation: fetch fresh before scheduling; user override; "conflicting hours, not scheduled yet". [p52]
- **Maps fail.** Mitigation: keep the last buffer, never leave travel unbuffered. [p52, p56]
- **ETA cannot run inside a Live Activity** (no network). Mitigation, inferred and not stated in the doc: compute the ETA in the app during upkeep, and have the Live Activity show only the precomputed leave-by time with dynamic date text. [p43, p52]
- **On-device model is small (4K on iOS 26), rate-limited in the background, and missing on older iPhones.** Mitigation: grammar first, Private Cloud Compute for long input, a non-AI path for everything. [p44, p47]
- **Ambiguous phrasing or the wrong "that".** Mitigation: resolve in code and ask with both candidates when they are close. [p47]
- **Model latency and big reshuffles erode trust.** Mitigation: deterministic intents for the top 20 phrases; stability penalty; one-tap undo. [p48]
- **Re-solving reshuffles the whole week.** Mitigation: a minimal-change cost, the next 2 hours frozen, as few moves as possible. [p49]
- **Outside changes double-book (for example a new meeting invite).** Mitigation: flag, never auto-fix. [p50]
- **Too many "Ask" prompts cause decision fatigue.** Mitigation: batch into one card with a recommended answer; no timeout. [p50]
- **Rule-based skips remove all exercise in exam weeks.** Mitigation: a weekly movement floor with short fallbacks. [p50]
- **Appetite suppression means lunch reminders get ignored.** Mitigation: a protected lunch block, a snack before the window ends, a one-tap "ate". [p50]
- **Entering assessments by hand is heavy.** Mitigation: unit guide PDF and LMS feed import with confirmation. [p50]
- **Estimates start wrong, and long assessments get left late.** Mitigation: an early forecast with a fix; milestones on the Lock Screen. [p50]
- **The remaining-time estimate is itself a guess.** Mitigation: treat it as censored data. [p51]
- **A shortfall number triggers anxiety.** Mitigation: always pair it with a fix, in neutral wording. [p51]
- **Big diffs are hard to read.** Mitigation: show only changed items and the net effect. [p51]
- **Finding the user's row in a roster; every employer's layout differs.** Mitigation: one tap to pick the row, and the layout is remembered per source. [p51]
- **iOS 27 Visual Intelligence may already have added the same shifts.** Mitigation: adopt existing matching events. [p51]
- **OCR misreads "1-4" as "1-14".** Mitigation: normalised keys; shifts over 12 h need a confirm. [p56]
- **Offline edits on two devices collide.** Mitigation: latest edit wins for low-risk items; high-risk items ask; 30-day tombstones. [p52]
- **EventKit has no multi-item transaction across accounts, and a sync server may reject later.** Mitigation: compensating rollback from the audit log; list unresolved changes. [p56]
- **iCloud and Exchange sync lag.** Mitigation: "pending" until seen twice; repair rather than duplicate. [p56]
- **External apps changed data before a restore.** Mitigation: re-diff and ask about the in-between edits. [p56]
- **The write failed.** Mitigation: never mark the item rescheduled; queue it in local-only mode. [p56]
- **Too many location pings drain the battery.** Mitigation: at most 1 to 2 prompts a day, only when open and free. [p53]
- **Escalation adds notification load.** Mitigation: escalations count against the daily budget, except leave-now and medication. [p53]
- **The budget hides something important.** Mitigation: exempt leave-now, transition and user-set medication reminders. [p58]
- **Late cascades empty the afternoon.** Mitigation: offer the baseline day when more than half the flexible work no longer fits. [p58]
- **Users forget to start and stop timers.** Mitigation: auto-start with one-tap "not started"; the next block's start stops the last. [p58]
- **Logging burden drives people to abandon tracking.** Mitigation: passive capture first; reason codes only on lapses. [p54]
- **Users will not classify every miss.** Mitigation: infer "blocked by rule"; ask only about real misses. [p54]
- **Gym sessions without a Watch are invisible.** Mitigation: one-tap "did it". [p54]
- **Check-in fatigue.** Mitigation: energy folded into the focus check-in, optional after the learning phase. [p54]
- **Over-reading 2 examples.** Mitigation: evidence thresholds, shrinkage toward defaults, approval before rule changes. [p54]
- **Categories too coarse or too fine.** Mitigation: about 10 to start, with split suggestions. [p55]
- **A weekly list of shortcomings reads like a report card.** Mitigation: lead with what worked; at most 3 findings. [p55]
- **Model-written explanations are wrong.** Mitigation: numbers and reasons come from the solver record. [p55]
- **Model output smuggles in medication advice.** Mitigation: no medication-action field in any output type. [p57]
- **Users want cloud AI that needs health data.** Mitigation: explain the limit; send derived summaries only. [p57]
- **Asking for every permission up front triggers refusals.** Mitigation: progressive permissions. [p57]
- **The model misreads a search question.** Mitigation: show the interpreted query. [p57]
- **An export file holding medication logs is sensitive.** Mitigation: warn, leave out Health data by default, keep it on-device until shared. [p57]
- **Emails carry hidden instructions.** Mitigation: on-device parsing; confirm every booking. [p48]
- **Walls of text get skipped; nightly questions become a chore.** Mitigation: a 5-line briefing; at most 3 review questions; skipping is fine. [p48, p49]
- **Users drift off after 2 to 3 weeks.** Mitigation: value without opening the app, lapse-proof re-entry, rotating wording and themes, micro-sessions, cohort tracking. [p59]
- **Setup too heavy before any value.** Mitigation: onboarding under 3 minutes; infer or preset everything else. [p59]
- **The scheduler over-packs the day.** Mitigation: at most about 60% fill; transitions and buffer; a hideable calendar; the baseline day one tap away. [p59]
- **One visibly bad move kills trust** (people lose confidence in an algorithm faster than in a human after the same mistake). Mitigation: suggest-then-confirm for 2 weeks, protected items, reasons, undo, a move cap, the "what moved" digest. [p60]
- **The app causes shame.** Mitigation: no red counts or streaks, neutral copy, amnesty and fresh start, forecasts paired with fixes. [p60]
- **Notifications are too weak or too many.** Mitigation: distinct Watch haptics, AlarmKit for critical moments only, a learning budget, the 48 h window. [p60]
- **Constant UI change disorients users.** Mitigation: a stable navigation contract, opt-in features, a TestFlight beta group. [p60]
- **Dependence instead of skill.** Mitigation: "plan with me" mode, routine graduation, a weekly review that teaches the pattern. [p60]
- **Most likely overall failure** is trust and adoption rather than missing features: drifting off after two weeks, an over-packed day, one bad move, heavy setup. Prioritise these mitigations. [p58]
- **Four blind spots of the original inventory to design around:** it assumed continuous background runtime (iOS gives none); it assumed write access Apple does not grant (Health doses, rich Reminders); it asked an ADHD user for too much configuration; and it treated the medication window as a fixed rule instead of a learned, regulation-sensitive estimate. [p46]
