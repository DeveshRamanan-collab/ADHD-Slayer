# Interventions evidence: build requirements

Source read in full: interventions_evidence.md (693 lines). Citations give the source question number and a shortened heading. Competitor names and competitor analysis are left out. Anything marked "(proposal)", "(product mapping)" or "(platform note)" is added for buildability and is not a research finding.

## 1. User needs and pain points → the feature or behaviour that answers each

- Skills fail at the point of performance, not for lack of knowledge → deliver every core skill where the action happens (Apple Watch, Home and Lock Screen widgets, Live Activities, lock-screen capture), not only inside the app. [Q0 Overall grading; Q2 Point of performance and time]
- Time blindness: medium deficits in time discrimination (25 studies, n = 1,633) and time reproduction (26 studies, n = 2,364), small to medium extra error in time estimation, small deficit in time production → time is always visible: a depleting ring for the current block, a "time until next commitment" countdown, a "leave in X min" countdown and travel/transition buffers. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- Poor feel for how long tasks take → estimated and actual durations are logged for timed work and shown back so users can calibrate their own estimates. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- Transitions between activities get missed → automatic transition warnings at 10, 5 and 1 minutes before calendar events. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- Delay aversion: small immediate rewards are chosen over larger delayed ones more often (37 comparisons, 3,763 participants) and real rewards nearly double the odds of the immediate choice versus hypothetical ones → immediate completion feedback (haptic plus visual) and user-defined real rewards straight after a block, not distant badges or abstract points. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q6 Reward and motivation]
- Starting is the main barrier → a first physical action under 10 minutes is required when scheduling, a "start tiny" 5-minute timer runs on that step, and an if-then plan ties the start to a cue. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q3 Implementation intentions; Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- Distant goals do not motivate → "a step a day toward that eventual goal" with immediate feedback and incentives: long-term goals break into weekly objectives and one small daily step. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q2 Point of performance and time]
- Distractions derail work → a one-tap distraction parking lot on Watch and lock screen: write it down, defer it, return to the task. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- Reflexive opening of distracting apps; 73% of participants in one body-doubling study wanted attention monitoring and distraction detection → optional "pause before" friction on user-chosen apps during scheduled focus blocks. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability; Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- Environment shapes behaviour (NICE 1.5.15 offers adults medication after environmental modifications have been implemented and reviewed) → environment tools (app friction, Focus filters, task-locked bundles) are core product features, not extras. [Q0 Overall grading; Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- Avoidance and procrastination thoughts; adults with ADHD use more suppression and less reappraisal → a thought-reframe card when a task is repeatedly deferred, plus self-forgiveness framing (students who forgave themselves for procrastinating procrastinated less before the next exam). [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q10 Emotional regulation]
- Emotion dysregulation is large and near-core (g = 1.17 vs controls; 34% to 70% of adults; r = 0.54 with symptom severity) and a missed plan is the highest-risk emotional moment → a "missed it? re-plan" flow with neutral wording, an auto-offered new slot, and no red overdue counts or shame colours. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- Low self-compassion (lowest "common humanity", highest "overidentification"; self-compassion explained 32.3% of quality-of-life variance vs 28.2% for symptoms) → misses are normalised, de-personalised and followed straight away by one small next step; an optional brief self-compassion or breathing exercise is offered. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- Broken streaks cut continuation beyond the lost behaviour (65.84% continue with an intact streak vs 47.98% broken), worst when self-attributed (28.87% vs 42.00% for external causes) → no daily streaks by default, "X of the last 7 days" or weekly targets, automatic grace days, misses shown as "paused", one-tap repair (85.20% engagement with repair vs 68.66% without). [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- Guilt and shame after abandoning tracking (16.2% of lapsed activity trackers, 8.9% of lapsed finance trackers) → a shame-free "welcome back" flow that archives stale tasks, offers a 3-task restart and never mentions missed days. [Q7 Habits and streaks; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- Collection burden is the most common reason for abandoning self-tracking → zero mandatory logging, completion captured implicitly on check-off, passive data (HealthKit sleep and steps, calendar adherence) and 1-tap capture from Watch, Siri, widget and share sheet. [Q14 Self-monitoring; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- Discomfort with what the data reveal → trends shown weekly, never daily, and never leading with deficits. [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- "Learned enough" and changed life circumstances also end tracking → "graduation" messages that retire reminders for stable routines (design for "happy abandonment") and tailored re-engagement after life changes. [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- Prompt fatigue and habituation (a prompt's effect falls about 2% a day to zero by about day 28; users found suggestions "boring after 2 to 4 weeks") → a capped daily notification budget, 2 to 3 digests for non-urgent nudges, rotating message templates, and "send nothing" as a standard option. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- Fear of missing messages and feeling less connected when notifications are off (no notifications raised anxiety, effect size 0.56, via fear of missing out) → batch, do not silence: 3 digests a day improved attention, productivity, stress and mood, while hourly batching made little difference. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- Prompts sent when a person is not receptive may harm engagement → delivery gated on receptivity signals (phone unlocked, not in a social or communication app, walking, Focus mode, calendar free, time of day) plus adaptive learning of each user's response windows. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- Choice overload and reading load → a "Now" screen with a single task and its first step, "Now / Next" by default with the backlog hidden, one primary action per screen, plain language at Year 7 to 8 reading level. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- Configuration overload → onboarding under 2 minutes with presets ("minimal", "gentle nudges", "high structure") and defaults that work with no configuration. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- Need for presence and accountability (body-doubling users report accountability or social pressure, companionship, reduced overwhelm and help starting) → optional "work alongside" mode and share-a-commitment check-ins. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- Social-anxiety cost of 1:1 video co-working → asynchronous, low-mutuality presence ("others are working now") and ad-hoc sessions as the lowest-pressure option. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- Human support raises adherence more than automated reminders, and digital interventions under-use social relationships → optional accountability partner or clinician share with process-based check-ins ("did the first step?"), never outcome scores. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability; Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- Guideline care is CBT for adolescents and adults (AADPA) plus regular follow-up (NICE 1.5.18) → the app is an adjunct to care and offers an optional summary the user can take to a coach or clinician. [Q0 Overall grading; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- Adults on medication can still have impairing symptoms that CBT skills help (Safren 2010 enrolled medication-treated adults; NICE 1.5.17 supports combining) → planning skills are designed to sit alongside the medication focus window (product mapping), with no claim of an added effect. [Q0 Overall grading; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q3 Implementation intentions]
- Some users choose not to take medication, struggle to adhere, or find it ineffective or intolerable (NICE 1.5.16) → every planning and skills feature works with medication features switched off; AADPA lists adherence support as a non-pharmacological category, which fits dose logging without advice (product mapping). [Q0 Overall grading]
- Sleep problems are common (insomnia up to 80%, delayed sleep-wake timing 73% to 78%, melatonin onset about 90 minutes later than controls) → optional wind-down at a consistent time, a "tomorrow's first task" brain dump, a screen-off cue, and next-day plans sized from HealthKit sleep. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- Energy varies day to day → an optional 1-tap mood/energy check-in that shrinks the day's plan on low-energy days. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- Short-lived executive-function boost from exercise (acute exercise g = 0.55 on inhibitory control in adults with ADHD) → an optional 10 to 20 minute "movement primer" suggested immediately before study or deep-work blocks. [Q12 Acute exercise]
- Easy tasks give momentum but habitually picking them erodes long-term performance, more so under heavy load (76% of a high-workload group chose the easy task vs 64% of a low-workload group) → "warm-up micro-task, then most important task" ordering and a flag when only low-priority or easy tasks were done for several days. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- Unrealistic habit expectations (median 66 days to automaticity, range 18 to 254; exercise 91 days; a single miss does not disrupt progress) → honest expectation copy and an if-then habit-stack wizard anchored to existing routines. [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- Real engagement is brief and intermittent (about 3.4 opens a week and 3.4-minute sessions in an ADHD CBT app study; typical mental-health apps keep 3.3% of users at 30 days) → every interaction is designed to take 1 to 3 minutes, several times a week. [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement]
- Users will lapse and return; lapse-tolerant tools recover quickly on return (26.9% of friction-tool users took breaks averaging 9.15 days) → pausing any feature has no penalty and resuming restores prior settings. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- The population is adolescents 16+ and adults, including college students (the ACCESS programme enrolled 18 to 30 year olds) → examples, day types and copy fit students as well as working adults. [Title: population 16+; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- ADHD tech research is deficit-focused (built to "mitigate" ADHD) and rarely co-designed → strengths-based framing and a co-design/beta panel of ADHD users. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- Engagement with just-in-time support depends on autonomy, relatedness and competence → every nudge is overridable, social features are optional, and the plan's challenge is sized to the day. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- Focus-window check-ins (product feature) add self-report burden → each check-in is 1 tap, passive signals are preferred where possible, and check-ins count inside the notification budget (product mapping). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q14 Self-monitoring]

## 2. Features and behaviours (with acceptance criteria)

- **Skill spine**: the default product is the CBT loop plan → decompose → schedule → capture distractions → review/reframe. AC: a fresh install on default settings shows only this loop; gamification, co-working, friction, reward menu, habits, movement primer and wind-down are optional layers, each with its own switch. Evidence: STRONG as CBT/MCT package components. [Q0 Overall grading; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Single inbox, calendar-anchored list**: one inbox feeds one task list anchored to the calendar. AC: exactly one inbox exists; items captured on Watch, Siri, widget, lock screen and share sheet all land in it; any task can be given a calendar time slot. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **A/B/C priority**: every task carries priority A, B or C. AC: set in one tap from inbox and task page; the daily plan and the easy-drift check read it. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Break it down (required first action)**: scheduling requires a first physical action under 10 minutes. AC: a task cannot be scheduled without first-action text; an estimate of 10 minutes or more prompts "make it smaller"; the first action shows on the Now screen, Live Activity and friction pause. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Break it down (on-device AI, optional)**: the on-device model suggests steps each under 15 minutes. AC: each suggested step carries a minutes estimate under 15 (first step under 10); nothing is added until the user accepts; steps can be edited, reordered or deleted; the manual flow works when the model is unavailable. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Self-instruction cue**: a short user-written cue can be attached to a task (MCT uses "self-instruction cues"). AC: the cue shows with the first step on the Now screen, Live Activity and Watch (derived from MCT content, not in the source's feature list). [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Distraction parking lot**: one tap from Watch or lock screen opens capture by voice or text; saving returns the user to the task. AC: capturing never stops the running timer; each entry records the interrupted block; entries are deferred, never lost, and resurface later (when: open question). [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Now screen**: the default launch screen shows one current task and its first step, "Next" is visible, the backlog is hidden. AC: one primary action on screen; the full list opens only through an explicit action (progressive disclosure). [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Goals with a daily step**: long-term goals → weekly objectives → one small step a day with immediate feedback. AC: a goal can produce a daily step; completing it gives immediate haptic plus visual feedback. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Guided weekly review**: auto-derived look-back (planned vs done, on-time rate), a "how did this week go?" slider, "what got in the way?", then goals → obstacles → next week's 3 commitments. AC: exactly 3 commitment slots; each step fits the 1 to 3 minute design unit; skipping carries no penalty; sharing with a coach or clinician happens only by explicit user action. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Thought-reframe card**: offered when a task is repeatedly deferred; guides a quick reframe of the avoidance thought and ends with one small next step. AC: appears only after the deferral threshold; never blocks the task; dismissible in one tap. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **"What got in the way?" and acceptance prompts (optional)**: short functional-analysis prompts and optional mindfulness/acceptance prompts. AC: optional; worded as supportive; never labelled as emotion-regulation treatment. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Skill ladder**: introduce features from basic planner mechanics (inbox, list, first action, calendar) up to higher-order planning (weekly review, if-then plans, habits), as MCT does. AC: the first-day experience shows only the basics; advanced features are offered progressively and can be turned on at any time. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Visible time**: a depleting timer for the current block, a "leave in X min" countdown and "time until next commitment" on Live Activity, Dynamic Island and Watch complication. AC: readable without opening the app; keeps counting while the app is closed. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Transition warnings**: automatic alerts 10, 5 and 1 minutes before calendar events. AC: scheduled for events in connected calendars with no setup; each offset can be switched off; delivered as time-critical alerts (Watch haptic where available). [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Travel and transition buffers**: "leave in X min" subtracts travel time and a buffer from the event start. AC: a default buffer and a per-event buffer are editable; the leave-by time updates when the event moves. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Estimated vs actual**: estimated and actual durations are logged for timed work. AC: actual time is recorded with no extra input; a weekly view shows estimate accuracy; no claim that it improves estimation. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Completion feedback**: every completion gives immediate haptic plus visual feedback; optional rotating micro-celebrations. AC: both channels fire in the same interaction; celebrations vary and can be switched off. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **If-then wizard**: when scheduling any task, ask "When/where will you start? If [cue], then I will [first action]." AC: interactive step by step, not a static tip; saving creates a time or location trigger that surfaces the stated first action at the cue; skippable. Evidence: MODERATE. [Q3 Implementation intentions]
- **Obstacle plan (optional)**: "If I feel like checking my phone, then I will…". AC: stored with the if-then plan and shown when the friction pause or the trigger fires. [Q3 Implementation intentions]
- **Work alongside (optional)**: (a) ambient presence counter or anonymous live co-working room, (b) scheduled sessions with a chosen partner, (c) a hand-off link to an external co-working service. AC: each mode is opt-in; the live room reveals no identity by default; copy makes no efficacy claim. Evidence: POPULAR-BUT-UNPROVEN. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- **Share-a-commitment**: sends process check-ins ("did the first step?") to a trusted person (friend, family member, coach) or clinician. AC: payload is the commitment plus a process status only, never outcome scores; cadence and wording are set with the user; setup suggests someone the user sees as trustworthy and supportive; framing is benevolent. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Focus timer**: presets 12/3 and 25/5 plus custom, with "suggest a break" nudges the user can override. AC: one tap to switch preset; a break suggestion never stops the timer by itself; custom lengths let users match blocks to their attention span. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Start tiny**: one tap starts a 5-minute timer on the first step. AC: available from the Now screen and Live Activity; presented as a tool, not a proven method. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Easy-drift flag**: when only low-priority or easy tasks were completed for several days, the daily plan flags it and suggests "warm-up micro-task, then most important task". AC: non-judgemental wording; dismissible; threshold defined (open question). [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Reward menu**: a user-built menu in four groups: appetisers (quick pleasures), mains, sides (paired with dull tasks) and desserts (limited). AC: add, edit, remove items; starter suggestions favour healthy mains (exercise, socialising, music); schedule a real reward after a block ("after this block: 15 min of game X"); pair a task with a side that unlocks only during the task via Shortcuts or Focus filters; options refreshed periodically. [Q6 Reward and motivation; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Teach bundling**: a short explainer of temptation bundling the first time a bundle is paired (bundling taught as a strategy kept effects up to 17 weeks). AC: shown once, skippable, re-openable from help. [Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Gamification off by default**: light and optional; no points economy by default. AC: default presets have no points, levels or leaderboards; a toggle enables light gamification; celebrations rotate. [Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Habits**: no daily streaks by default; consistency shown as "X of the last 7 days" or progress to a weekly target; automatic grace days; misses shown as "paused"; one-tap "fresh start" or repair; habit-stack wizard "After I [existing routine], I will [new tiny habit]". AC: no broken-streak notification is ever sent; one missed day never resets displayed progress; expectation copy shown when a habit is created. [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Notification budget**: a user-set daily cap (example 5). AC: non-time-critical notifications never exceed the cap; the cap is visible and editable. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Digests**: non-urgent nudges are grouped into 2 to 3 digests a day. AC: default times 09:00, 15:00, 21:00 (the tested schedule); times editable; nothing non-urgent is delivered outside a digest. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Real time only when time-critical**: only point-of-performance cues (leave now, transition warnings) are delivered in real time, as Watch haptics where available. AC: every other message type is routed to a digest. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Suppression**: respect Focus modes; no prompts during calendar events; no prompts while a social or communication app is in the foreground where detectable. AC: a test calendar event suppresses non-urgent prompts for its whole duration. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Adaptive timing**: a Thompson-sampling bandit learns each user's response windows and always keeps a no-send arm. AC: no-send is available at every decision point; the model updates from observed responses. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Template rotation**: prompts come from a content bank that varies form, presentation and timing. AC: the same template is never shown twice in a row; every message is brief and clear. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Pause-before friction (off by default)**: on user-chosen apps, only during scheduled focus blocks, a 5 to 10 second breathing pause shows the current task and its first step with the buttons "back to [task]" and "open anyway (1 min)". AC: off by default; active only inside scheduled focus blocks; a real delay is enforced (an "are you sure?" message without delay fails the spec); both buttons always present; choosing "open anyway" is never penalised. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Movement primer (optional)**: auto-suggested before scheduled study or deep-work blocks: 10 to 20 minutes of moderate aerobic activity immediately before. AC: the suggestion appears ahead of the block; a HealthKit-detected workout marks it done; copy says "may help focus for the next session". [Q12 Acute exercise]
- **Wind-down (optional)**: a Watch reminder at a consistent time, a "tomorrow's first task" brain dump and a screen-off cue. AC: one user-set daily time; the brain-dump item becomes tomorrow's first Now task; no medical sleep claims. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Sleep-sized plans**: sleep duration comes from HealthKit, never manual logging, and sizes the next day's plan. AC: after short sleep the proposed plan has fewer tasks; no sleep entry is ever required. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Passive sleep pattern view**: a weekly sleep-pattern summary from HealthKit stands in for a sleep diary (NICE 1.8.17: monitor changes in sleep pattern, for example with a sleep diary) and can be shared with a clinician. AC: read-only; nothing about medication or doses is inferred from it. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Missed it? re-plan**: when a planned block ends without completion, the app offers a new slot and an optional 20-second reframe ("Lots of people with ADHD hit this; what's one small next step?"). AC: one tap accepts the new slot; no red overdue counts; wording is neutral. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Mood/energy check-in (optional)**: one tap; low energy reduces the number of tasks planned that day. AC: one tap to complete; skipping changes nothing. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Self-compassion or breathing exercise (optional)**: brief, reachable from the re-plan flow. AC: optional; can be exited at any moment. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Minimum viable tracking**: zero mandatory logging; completion captured by check-off; optional weekly "how did this week go?" slider; auto-derived planned-vs-done and on-time rate. AC: every feature works with no logging beyond check-offs. [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Graduation**: when a routine is stable, suggest "you may not need reminders for X any more". AC: accepting turns that reminder off; declining keeps it; no penalty either way. [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Presets and personalisation**: onboarding offers "minimal", "gentle nudges" and "high structure"; later, notification style, colour and sound, reward types and gamification can be personalised. AC: presets switchable at any time; no personalisation is required to use the app. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Onboarding**: under 2 minutes, with defaults that work with no configuration. AC: median time from first launch to first planned task is under 2 minutes in usability tests; every setting is skippable. [Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Welcome back**: after a lapse, archive stale tasks, offer a 3-task restart and make no reference to missed days. AC: archived tasks are restorable; the restart shows exactly 3 tasks. [Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Capture everywhere**: 1-tap capture from Watch, Siri, widget, share sheet and lock screen. AC: each surface adds to the single inbox without opening the full app where the platform allows. [Q16 Abandonment and retention; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Undo and no timeouts**: every action is undoable; no dialog auto-dismisses or shows a countdown. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Clinician or coach share**: optional export of the weekly review, commitments and functional stats for follow-up appointments. AC: produced only on user request; the user previews exactly what is shared. [Q0 Overall grading; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Outcome measures**: planned vs done, on-time rate and weekly self-rated functioning are the success measures; engagement is a means, not the goal. AC: all three are computed and shown weekly. [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Daily top-5 (product mapping)**: the on-device model's daily priorities use A/B/C priority, give each item a first physical action, use plain language, open with a warm-up micro-task then the most important task (an untested inference in the source), and list fewer items on low-energy or short-sleep days; the screen still leads with Now/Next. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard; Q10 Emotional regulation; Q13 Sleep and wind-down; Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Social plans (product mapping)**: social calendar events get transition warnings and leave-now countdowns like any event, prompts are suppressed during them, and socialising can be a "main" in the reward menu. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q6 Reward and motivation; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Haptics (product mapping)**: every haptic has a visual equivalent; time-critical cues use a distinct pattern; haptic and sound styles are personalisable. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Full-page paging (product mapping)**: each page carries one primary action and pages keep a fixed, predictable order. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]

## 3. Data the app must store (entities, fields, retention)

- **Task**: id, title, notes, priority (A/B/C), status (inbox, planned, in progress, done, paused, archived), first physical action text and estimate in minutes (under 10), steps (text, estimate under 15 minutes, source user or on-device AI, done flag), self-instruction cue, estimated duration, actual duration, scheduled start and end, linked calendar event id, deferral count and deferral timestamps, if-then plan id, reward or bundle id, effort tag (easy/hard) for easy-drift detection, most-important flag, created, completed and archived timestamps. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q2 Point of performance and time; Q3 Implementation intentions; Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Inbox item**: id, text (typed or dictated), capture surface (Watch, Siri, widget, lock screen control, share sheet, in-app), shared payload (URL or text from the share sheet), captured at, triage state, resulting task id. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Distraction entry**: id, text, captured at, interrupted task or block id, disposition (deferred, turned into task, dismissed), resurfaced at. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **If-then plan**: id, owner (task or habit), cue type (time, location, routine), cue text (e.g. "I sit at my desk after lunch"), trigger config (time, or latitude, longitude and radius), then-action text, optional obstacle cue and response ("If I feel like checking my phone, then I will…"), created-by-wizard flag, fire history. [Q3 Implementation intentions; Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Goal**: id, long-term goal text, weekly objectives, daily step suggestions and their completions. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Focus session**: id, task id, preset (12/3, 25/5 or custom work and break minutes), start-tiny flag (5 minutes), started at, ended at, breaks suggested, taken and overridden, distractions captured, completed flag. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching]
- **Calendar link**: EventKit event identifier, start and end, busy flag, social-event flag (product mapping), travel buffer minutes, leave-by time, scheduled warning offsets (10/5/1) and their notification ids; event details are read from EventKit rather than copied (proposal). [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Estimate calibration**: pairs of estimated and actual minutes per task (optionally per category) and the derived personal calibration factor. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Reward menu item**: id, title, group (appetiser, main, side, dessert), kind (after-block reward or during-task bundle), duration minutes (e.g. 15), linked shortcut, app or media, dessert limit, active flag, last offered at, last used at, use count. [Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Habit**: id, tiny habit text, anchor routine text, weekly target (days per week), activity types that count toward the target, grace days allowed and used, completion dates, status (active, paused), fresh-start and repair dates, reminder on/off, graduated flag. [Q7 Habits and streaks; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Check-in**: id, timestamp, energy value, mood value, used-for-plan-sizing flag; every check-in is optional. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Weekly review**: id, week start date, "how did this week go?" value, functioning self-rating, "what got in the way?" text, goals, obstacles, the 3 commitments, auto-stats snapshot (planned, done, on-time rate), share records. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Daily outcome record**: date, planned count, done count, on-time count, planned-vs-done rate, on-time rate, count of A-priority completions (feeds the easy-drift check). [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement; Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Notification decision log**: decision time, context features (unlocked, walking, Focus on, calendar busy, hour bucket, weekday or weekend, day type), arm chosen (no-send or template id), channel (real time or digest), delivered at, acted on within the outcome window (yes/no); plus bandit state per arm (e.g. Beta alpha and beta counts). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]

## 6. Algorithms, parameters, defaults and thresholds

- **JITAI structure**: decision points, tailoring variables, intervention options, decision rules, proximal outcomes and distal outcomes are each defined explicitly in configuration. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Default option**: "send nothing" is available at every decision point and is chosen whenever no rule or model selects a prompt. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Decision points per day**: reference designs used 5 a day (walking-prompt micro-randomised trial, 37 participants, 6,061 decision points) and 6 a day (workplace wellbeing app, 1,255 users, 89 days); keep decision points within the user's budget (example 5). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Exploration reference**: the walking trial randomised 0.4 nothing, 0.3 walking suggestion, 0.3 anti-sedentary suggestion; a no-send probability near 0.4 is a reasonable starting exploration rate (proposal). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Tailoring variables**: phone unlocked, social or communication app in use, walking, Focus mode, calendar busy/free, time of day, weekday vs weekend; add the product's day type as a variable (proposal). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Receptivity priors** (70 students, 8,162 notifications, about 76% completion overall): 16:00 to 20:00 about 82% vs mornings about 70%; unlocked 85% vs locked about 78%; walking about 83%; social or communication app in use about 65% (lowest); weekends lower. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Weekend effect** (workplace app): prompts raised 24-hour engagement by 3.9% overall (RR 1.039), 8.7% on weekends vs 2.5% on weekdays, peaking at 11.8% at 12:30 on weekends; no significant decline over 89 days. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Bandit**: Thompson sampling over delivery windows plus a no-send arm; reward is the user acting on the prompt within a proximal window (the walking trial used 30 minutes, the workplace trial 24 hours; choice is an open question). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Engine benchmarks**: receptivity models improved receptivity by up to 36% vs random timing and kept improving over time; one prompt lifts next-30-minute activity by about 14% and 24-hour engagement by about 3.9%, so expect small per-prompt effects. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Habituation**: the day-1 prompt effect (66%) falls about 2% a day to zero by about day 28 → track exposure per template and vary content form, presentation and timing within every 2 to 4 week window. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q6 Reward and motivation; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Batching**: non-urgent items go into 2 to 3 digests a day; the tested 3-a-day schedule (09:00, 15:00, 21:00) improved inattention (effect size 0.65), productivity (0.58), stress (0.56), mood (0.49) and phone control (0.58) and cut unlocks (0.60); hourly batching made little difference; no notifications at all raised anxiety (0.56). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Real-time exceptions**: leave-now alerts and transition warnings at 10, 5 and 1 minutes before events bypass digests. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Suppression rules**: Focus on → only time-sensitive alerts the user allows; calendar event in progress → defer to the next digest; social or communication app in the foreground (if detectable) → defer. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Budget**: user-set daily cap, example 5; whether digests and time-critical cues count toward it is an open question. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Leave-by time**: leave-by = event start minus travel time minus buffer; shown as "leave in X min". [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Depleting timer**: fraction remaining = time left divided by block length, drawn as a shrinking ring. [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Estimate calibration**: store (estimated, actual) pairs and show the user's typical ratio; the method is not specified (open question). [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Decomposition limits**: first physical action under 10 minutes; every AI-suggested step under 15 minutes. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Start tiny**: a 5-minute timer on the first step. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Focus timer presets**: 12/3, 25/5 and custom (a 24/6 schedule was also tested). Fixed breaks reduced fatigue and distraction with no performance difference in one study; in a 2026 replication (n = 176) self-regulated breaks were preferred and rated better for energy (0.36), concentration (0.43), motivation (0.54) and distraction (0.55), with no learning difference → default hybrid: suggest a break at the preset time and let the user override. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Warm-up rule**: order the day as one warm-up micro-task, then the most important task (an inference in the source, untested). [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Easy-drift rule**: flag when for several consecutive days only low-priority (B/C) or easy tasks were completed; field data show easy-task choice rises with load (each extra patient under care raised the chance of picking an easier case by 8%), so heavy days need the check most (threshold is an open question). [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **If-then strength**: implementation intentions d = 0.65 (general population); with mental contrasting g = 0.336 (21 studies, 15,907 participants); interactive formation g = 0.465 vs document-based g = 0.277 → the wizard must be interactive. [Q3 Implementation intentions]
- **Reward timing**: rewards are immediate and real, with no gap after completion; example "15 min of game X" right after the block. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Bundling**: the tempting item unlocks only during the paired task; reference effects 51% more gym visits (full treatment) and 29% (intermediate); taught as a strategy it raised weekly workout likelihood 10% to 14% for up to 17 weeks; effects fell after a holiday disruption → re-offer bundles after holidays or day-type changes (proposal). [Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Gamification**: effects are small (a "trivial" 489 extra steps a day across 36 RCTs and 10,079 adults) → off by default; no token economy or variable-ratio rewards by default (no ADHD evidence found). [Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Desserts**: dessert items are limited (the cap value is an open question). [Q6 Reward and motivation]
- **Habit consistency**: rolling "X of the last 7 days" or a weekly target; several activity types can count toward one target; automatic grace days; misses count as paused; repair restores the record (engagement 85.20% with repair vs 68.66% unrepairable vs 93.14% intact). [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Habit expectations**: median 66 days to 95% of the automaticity plateau, range 18 to 254, exercise 91 days vs 59 to 65 for eating or drinking; more than half of participants had not plateaued within 84 days → no countdown to day 66. [Q7 Habits and streaks; Q0 Overall grading]
- **Re-plan**: auto-offer the next free slot; the optional reframe lasts about 20 seconds. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Plan sizing**: fewer tasks on low-energy days and after short sleep; the mapping from energy and sleep to task count is not specified (open question). [Q10 Emotional regulation; Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Friction parameters**: delay 5 to 10 seconds, default 6 seconds (90% of 1,039 users of a friction tool kept a 6-second breathing default); "open anyway" allowance 1 minute; active only inside scheduled focus blocks. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Friction expectations**: about 36% of opening attempts abandoned mid-pause; attempts decline exponentially and plateau after about 20 days; effects persist to about 13 weeks; the share of dismissed attempts falls over time as remaining opens become more intentional; users returning from breaks (26.9%, average 9.15 days) recover quickly. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Movement primer**: 10 to 20 minutes of moderate aerobic activity immediately before demanding cognitive work; internal expectation anchored to g about 0.55 on inhibition (not for copy); modality estimates varied widely (yoga about zero, cycling moderate), so default suggestions are aerobic; the benefit window after exercise is unquantified. [Q12 Acute exercise]
- **Wind-down**: the reminder fires at the same user-set time every day. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Minimum viable tracking**: (1) done or not done from check-off, (2) an optional single weekly rating of functioning and mood; everything else is passive or opt-in. [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Outcome metrics**: planned-vs-done = done divided by planned; on-time rate = on-time starts or arrivals divided by scheduled items (definition open); weekly self-rated functioning; all shown weekly. [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Engagement targets**: 1 to 3 minute interactions, design unit under 5 minutes; realistic benchmark about 3.4 opens a week and 3.4-minute sessions (median 27 opens over 7 weeks); onboarding under 2 minutes. [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Retention benchmarks**: typical mental-health apps 3.9% at 15 days and 3.3% at 30 days, median daily open rate 4.0% (trackers 6.3%, peer support 17.0%, breathing apps 0.0% retention at 30 days); an uncontrolled ADHD CBT app study retained 52.7% at week 7. [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Fixed counts**: welcome-back restart = 3 tasks; weekly commitments = 3; transition warnings = 10, 5 and 1 minutes; notification budget example = 5; digests = 2 to 3 a day. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Expected overall effect**: digital ADHD interventions pool to a small effect (g about 0.32; 23 RCTs, mostly children; guided and self-guided equal) → evaluate the product on function, not symptom scales, and do not promise more. [Q0 Overall grading; Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement]

## 7. Content and copy rules (tone, words to avoid, must-say / never-say)

- **Tone, plain language**: easy words, short sentences and short blocks of text; target Year 7 to 8 reading level (roughly US grade 6 to 7; approximate conversion). [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Tone, brevity**: every notification and prompt is "brief and clear". [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]

- **Tone, neutral misses**: misses are neutral data: "paused", never "broken". [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Tone, after a miss**: normalise it (common humanity), de-personalise it, then go straight to a next step. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Tone, shared responsibility**: attribute breaks to circumstances rather than the person (self-attributed breaks left 28.87% continuing vs 42.00% for external causes). [Q7 Habits and streaks]

- **Never-say, streaks**: no broken-streak notifications and no highlighting of broken streaks anywhere. [Q7 Habits and streaks]

## 8. Accessibility and neurodivergent-design requirements

- **Understand what things are (COGA objective 1)**: every control and screen has a plain text label; icons are never the only label. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Find what you need (objective 2)**: predictable, consistent layouts and navigation; lists use progressive disclosure. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Clear content (objective 3)**: easy words, short sentences and short blocks; Year 7 to 8 reading level. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Avoid and correct mistakes (objective 4)**: "Ask the user only for what you need!"; every action is undoable. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Help focus (objective 5)**: never distract the user from the task: one primary action per screen, Now/Next by default, brief optional celebrations, prompts suppressed during focus blocks and events. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Do not rely on memory (objective 6)**: the next step is always on screen, cues appear at the point of performance, captured distractions are kept, and if-then plans fire at the cue. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q2 Point of performance and time; Q3 Implementation intentions]
- **Provide help (objective 7)**: in-context help for each technique (e.g. the bundling explainer), re-openable at any time. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Adaptation and personalisation (objective 8)**: presets plus deep personalisation of notification style, colour and sound, reward types and gamification. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **No time pressure**: no timeouts, no auto-dismissing dialogs and no countdown-to-decide prompts; the friction pause exists only when the user enables and schedules it. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Fewer choices**: cut choices per screen; hide the backlog; the daily list stays short and gets shorter on low-energy days. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Co-design**: involve people with ADHD in design through a co-design/beta panel before copy and defaults are final; treat usability as equal in importance to efficacy. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Micro-interactions**: design unit of 1 to 3 minutes, never more than 5 minutes for any routine flow. [Q9 Digital therapeutics and engagement]
- **Low-effort start**: onboarding under 2 minutes, defaults that work unconfigured, and presets to avoid configuration overload. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Multi-sensory feedback**: every haptic cue has a visual equivalent; users can personalise haptics, sounds and colours. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **No shame visuals**: no red overdue counts or shame colours; neutral visual language for missed items. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Autonomy**: every nudge is overridable, friction always offers "open anyway", gamification is off by default, and social features are optional. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q11 Friction and distraction blocking; Q6 Reward and motivation; Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- **Strengths-based framing**: language and visuals avoid neuro-normative outcomes. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Low social pressure**: asynchronous, low-mutuality presence is offered for users with social anxiety. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- **Weekly views**: trends appear weekly, not daily. [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Lapse tolerance**: returning after any gap shows no penalty, no reset and no mention of missed days. [Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Readability check**: all copy passes a readability test for the Year 7 to 8 target before release (proposal). [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]

## 9. Privacy, security and safety requirements (build-relevant only)

- **Data minimisation**: collect only what a feature needs ("Ask the user only for what you need!"); all logging beyond check-offs is optional. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Consent to share**: data-sharing concerns are a documented reason people abandon tracking, so every share (partner, clinician, co-working, bring-your-own AI) is opt-in, per recipient, revocable, and previewed so the user sees exactly what leaves the device. [Q14 Self-monitoring; Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- **Process-only sharing**: partner and clinician check-ins carry the commitment and a process status only, never outcome scores; also excluding mood and medication data by default is a proposal. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- **Anonymous presence**: the live co-working room is anonymous by default and the presence counter shows counts only; no task content leaves the device for presence. [Q4 Body doubling and accountability]
- **HealthKit scope**: read-only access limited to sleep, steps and workouts. [Q12 Acute exercise; Q13 Sleep and wind-down; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **On-device AI**: break-it-down suggestions run on device by default. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Screen Time safety**: chosen apps are held as opaque Screen Time tokens; friction is off by default, runs only in scheduled focus blocks and can be switched off at any time. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Just-in-time permissions**: each permission is requested when its feature is first used, protecting the 2-minute onboarding and asking only for what is needed (proposal). [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Psychological safety**: given high emotion dysregulation, no shame colours, no red overdue counts, no broken-streak notifications and a neutral missed-plan flow. [Q10 Emotional regulation; Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Receptivity safety**: send nothing when the user is not receptive, because badly timed prompts may harm engagement. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Do not push silence**: never push users to switch off all notifications (silence raised anxiety); offer batching instead. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Not a treatment**: onboarding and store copy position the app as an adjunct to clinical care, with no medical sleep claims and no exercise treatment claims. [Q0 Overall grading; Q12 Acute exercise; Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **No clinical inference from sleep**: sleep data is never used to suggest medication or dose changes; NICE 1.8.17 frames sleep monitoring as a clinician task and the product never gives dosing advice. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Keep data through lapses**: data is never deleted because of inactivity. [Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Gap, crisis handling**: the source does not cover what to do if a mood check-in or reframe suggests distress (open question). [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Gap, minors**: the source does not cover data handling for 16 and 17 year olds (open question). [Title: population 16+]

## 10. Edge cases, failure modes and their mitigations

- **Missed block**: re-plan flow offers a new slot at once, with no red and no scolding. [Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Same task deferred again and again**: the reframe card appears after the threshold and never blocks the task. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q10 Emotional regulation]
- **Return after a lapse**: welcome back archives stale tasks (restorable), offers a 3-task restart, keeps all settings and never mentions missed days. [Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Missed habit day**: counted as paused, a grace day applies automatically, the 7-day window absorbs it and no notification is sent. [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Self-blame after a break (the most demotivating case)**: copy attributes the break to circumstances. [Q7 Habits and streaks]
- **Prompts go stale after 2 to 4 weeks**: rotate templates, refresh the content bank, and retire templates whose response rate falls. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Budget reached but a time-critical cue is due**: a rule is needed (open question); proposal: time-critical cues are exempt, and the user can switch individual offsets off. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Back-to-back events**: the next event's 10/5/1 warnings fall inside the current event, where prompts are suppressed; proposal: deliver one quiet Watch haptic "next up" alert instead of three. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Event in progress**: non-urgent prompts wait for the next digest. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Focus mode on**: only time-sensitive alerts the user allows break through. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Social or communication app in use (lowest completion, about 65%)**: defer where detectable; iOS does not expose the foreground app to third-party apps, so fall back to the other signals (platform note, verify). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Phone locked (about 78% completion vs 85% unlocked)**: favour windows when the user is usually active; live unlocked state is only observable while the app runs (platform note). [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Weekday and weekend differ**: the bandit conditions on weekday vs weekend and on day type. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **User turns all app notifications off**: the app still works fully; offer digest mode as the alternative, since total silence raised anxiety in the batching study. [Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Low energy or short sleep**: fewer tasks are planned, so the daily top-5 may show fewer than 5. [Q10 Emotional regulation; Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **HealthKit denied or no sleep data (no Watch, no sleep tracking)**: plan sizing uses the energy check-in or defaults; manual sleep logging is never forced. [Q13 Sleep and wind-down; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Primer done but no workout detected**: one-tap manual confirmation (proposal). [Q12 Acute exercise]
- **On-device model unavailable (ineligible device, Apple Intelligence off, model not ready)**: break-it-down falls back to manual steps and nothing is blocked. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **AI step out of range (15 minutes or more, or a first step of 10 or more)**: regenerate or ask the user to split it; out-of-range steps are never saved silently. [Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Frequent "open anyway"**: allowed without penalty, because the dismiss option is a key ingredient; the pause returns after the 1-minute allowance. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Friction built as an "are you sure?" dialog only**: fails the spec, since a message without a delay did nothing; a timed pause is required. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Friction effect plateaus after about 20 days**: expected, not a failure; never escalate friction automatically. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Falling share of dismissed attempts over time**: expected, as remaining opens are more intentional; not shown as failure. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Friction paused for days**: resuming restores the prior app selection and schedule; effects recover quickly on return. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Focus block ends while apps are shielded**: shields lift at block end because friction is scheduled to the plan. [Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
- **Easy-task drift on heavy days**: flag it and suggest a warm-up micro-task, then the most important task. [Q5 Chunking, Pomodoro, easy vs hard]
- **Wrong auto-derived stats (e.g. task done but not ticked)**: everything is editable and undoable, and stats recompute. [Q14 Self-monitoring; Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **User "learned enough"**: graduation retires reminders; stopping is supported with no guilt copy ("happy abandonment"). [Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Life changes (term ends, holidays)**: quick preset or day-type switch; bundles are re-offered after disruptions because their effect fell after a holiday. [Q6 Reward and motivation; Q14 Self-monitoring]
- **Reward novelty wears off**: reward options refresh periodically and desserts stay capped. [Q6 Reward and motivation; Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Large backlog**: only Now/Next is shown by default. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Live Activity time limit**: iOS ends a Live Activity after about 8 hours, so run one per block and start the next automatically (platform note, verify). [Q2 Point of performance and time]
- **Location cue limits**: iOS caps monitored regions per app, so keep only upcoming location cues active (platform note, verify). [Q3 Implementation intentions]
- **Routine cues that cannot be sensed (e.g. "after lunch")**: convert to a time trigger or a one-tap "I'm here" action (proposal). [Q3 Implementation intentions]
- **No Apple Watch**: Watch-first surfaces fall back to Live Activities, Lock Screen controls, widgets and iPhone haptics; a paired Watch receives iPhone notifications automatically while the iPhone is locked (platform note). [Q0 Overall grading; Q2 Point of performance and time]

## 11. Open questions / dependencies

- **Watch scope**: the source assumes an iPhone plus Apple Watch app (Watch parking lot, Watch haptics, complications, Watch wind-down reminder); confirm whether a watchOS companion is in scope or iPhone surfaces must cover all of these. [Q0 Overall grading; Q1 CBT/MCT/DBT/coaching; Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications; Q13 Sleep and wind-down]
- **Budget conflict**: 10/5/1 warnings for each event plus 2 to 3 digests can exceed a 5-a-day budget on a busy day; decide whether digests and time-critical cues count toward the budget. [Q2 Point of performance and time; Q8 JITAIs and notifications]
- **Top-5 vs Now/Next**: the product shows 5 daily priorities while the source says to show only Now/Next by default to limit choice overload; decide the default view. [Q15 Neurodivergent design guidelines]
- **Onboarding time**: under 2 minutes conflicts with accounts, medication setup, day types and permissions; decide what moves to later, just-in-time steps. [Q16 Abandonment and retention]
- **Friction grade conflict**: Q0 grades pause friction WEAK and Q11 grades it MODERATE; use WEAK in any copy. [Q0 Overall grading; Q11 Friction and distraction blocking]
