## 1. Features, screens and flows (with acceptance criteria)

### Core loop and product pillars
- Build order: the core loop ships first: log a dose, learn the window, fill it with chosen work, survive a bad day. AC: no feature outside this loop starts until testers show the loop works and keeps them coming back (Gate 2). [p74]
- The focus window drives the schedule: the app learns each person's own window from their check-ins and puts the work they chose inside it, instead of drawing a population curve. AC: in a test week, the solver places deep-work tasks inside the learned window. [p63, p66, p75]
- Personal duration multipliers with confidence: the app learns how long this person actually takes, and shows how confident it is. [p66]
- Lifestyles: a one-tap term/holiday switch, with routines learned separately for each lifestyle. [p63, p66]
- Log once: doses the user already logs in Apple Health are read in, so nobody enters the same dose twice. [p66, p77]
- Apple-native and Watch-first: built around Calendar, Reminders, Apple Watch and Live Activities, with iCloud calendars treated as first-class. [p66]
- Designed for lapses: amnesty, baseline days and shame-free re-entry are core flows, not settings. [p66]
- Privacy by architecture: health context stays on the device or in Apple Private Cloud Compute. [p66]

### Medication and dose logging (MVP)
- Medication setup asks for the user's own estimate of their focus window before any learning. That answer is stored as the stated estimate on the per-product FocusWindowModel. [p61, p72, p75]
- The user enters the Medication record (name, class stimulant or not, prescribed plan text, cut-off time, minimum re-log interval) and the app never edits it. AC: no automated code path writes to a Medication record. [p72]
- Specific opt-in consent for medication data is collected at first medication setup. It is stored as a ConsentRecord and can be withdrawn on that same screen. [p67, p70, p73]
- One-tap dose logging from Watch, widget, Control Center and Siri. AC: each surface creates a DoseEvent in one tap, tagged with the right source (app, Watch, Health import). [p72, p75]
- The double-dose guard ships in the MVP, is part of the free core and is never cut. The Medication record carries a user-entered minimum re-log interval and cut-off time for it to check against (the guard's behaviour is specified outside these pages). [p66, p72, p75, p76]
- Undo ships in the MVP and is never cut. PlanRevision (before, after, actor, rule, reason, undo token) powers undo, restore and "why". [p73, p75, p76]
- Each DoseEvent records product, time taken, time logged, source, a food flag and a booster flag. [p72]
- Switching brand creates a new Product (brand, formulation, strength, curve prior, stored label note), and the focus-window model is kept per product. [p72]
- Apple Health dose import is optional (decision 7: "Yes, optional"). AC: importing the same Health record twice produces exactly one DoseEvent, keyed by Health's ID. [p72, p77]
- "No meds today", late doses and partial days re-plan the day instantly. AC: after "No meds today" the plan updates at once and stays usable without a focus window. [p61]
- Shortage mode covers supply disruptions. It is deliberately left out of the MVP. [p61, p75]
- Supply tracker (v1): quantity on hand, repeats left, script expiry, eScript token and pharmacy notes, shown as a neutral supply view only. [p72, p74, p75]
- Cycle overlay arrives in v1.x (left out of the MVP). [p74, p75]

### Focus-window learning (MVP)
- Check-ins run 3 to 5 times a day and taper off. Each answer is yes, partly or no, with optional energy and optional mood. [p72, p75]
- The learned window is always shown with its uncertainty. Early estimates carry an honest "early estimate" label. [p61, p62]
- The user can manually override the window for each product. [p72]
- High-stakes blocks such as exams never rely on an early estimate. AC: while the model is still labelled "early estimate", the solver does not use the learned window to place a high-stakes block. [p61]
- When check-ins go unanswered, passive signals fill the gaps. [p62]
- Before the paywall, the app shows a "what I've learned about your week" summary. [p63]

### Today, Watch and Live Activity (MVP)
- The Today screen shows Now and Next. [p74, p75]
- A Live Activity mirrors Now and Next. In discreet mode it never shows medicine names. [p70, p75]
- The Watch app and Watch complication ship in the MVP. AC: the Watch logs doses offline with no login; logs are written locally and synced later. [p62, p74, p75]
- An app-owned wake alarm (AlarmKit) ships in the MVP. [p73, p74, p75]

### Tasks, planning and calendar (MVP)
- Tasks carry duration and deadline (MVP), focus demand (deep, medium, light, body), lifestyle scope and reason codes on lapses. The app infers most fields rather than asking. [p72, p75]
- The on-device solver places deep work inside the focus window and checks clashes, travel, hours and a 60% cap. [p71, p75]
- The app writes to its own app-owned calendar and keeps an identity map (ExternalObjectLink). The app UUID is also written into each event's URL field. [p62, p73, p75]
- Third-party (not app-owned) calendar events are never edited without an explicit confirm from the user. [p62]
- An in-app explanation of why full calendar access is needed appears before the system prompt. [p62, p68]
- If the user refuses full calendar access, a degraded mode lets them mark busy times by hand, and users can exclude sensitive calendars. AC: planning still works with calendar access denied. [p62]
- Manual planning is part of the free core. [p66]
- Changes proposed by the AI are previewed before anything is written. [p62]
- Nothing counts as done until the verifier has read the Calendar or Reminders write back. [p71]

### Bad days and re-entry (MVP)
- "Fix my day" works through fixed phrases (grammar tier). There is no free-form chat in the MVP. [p74, p75]
- The baseline day ships in the MVP and is never cut. [p75, p76]
- Amnesty ships in the MVP, is part of the free core and is never cut. [p66, p75, p76]
- A welcome-back flow gives shame-free re-entry after a lapse. [p66, p75]
- A notification budget caps how often the app interrupts. [p74, p75]

### Other MVP screens
- Calm mode. [p75]
- Setup presets: the author's own regime (for example "no gym on work days") is one template among several, never the default for everyone. [p75, p77]
- Support screen (must pass App Review at Gate 3). [p75, p76]
- Consent flows for each data category. [p75]

### Modes, learned patterns and explanations
- Modes are rule overlays with start and end dates and a stacking order. The defined modes are Exam, Sick day and Travel. [p72]
- The app proposes learned patterns as statements with an evidence count and interval, and the user approves or rejects each one. AC: a rejected pattern is not proposed again for 30 days. [p72]
- Every plan change can explain "why" from its PlanRevision (actor, rule, reason). [p73]

### Privacy and user-control screens
- A "What leaves this phone" page lists every destination, what goes there and why. [p70]
- Per-category data switches (original inventory section 57). Medication and Health data are locked to on-device and Private Cloud Compute. [p70]
- Export everything as JSON and CSV, with a warning that the file contains health data. [p63, p70]
- Delete everything from inside the app, including server copies and backups. [p70]
- Any consent can be withdrawn from the same screen where it was given. [p70, p73]
- An optional Face ID lock covers the whole app and, separately, the medication screens. [p70]
- Discreet mode removes medicine names from notification text, calendar titles and Live Activities. [p70]

### Subscription and paywall
- The free core is dose logging with the double-dose guard, the focus-window display, manual planning, amnesty and re-entry. [p66]
- Paid features are auto-scheduling, learning, Lifestyles, forecasting and roster import. [p67]
- Plans: an annual plan, a student price, a hardship scholarship, and an optional lifetime tier with a cap on third-party AI use. [p62, p67]
- A free trial of 21 to 30 days, a reminder 2 days before it ends, and one-tap cancel instructions. [p62, p67, p74]
- The paywall ships in v1 (App Store, Australia) and comes after the "what I've learned about your week" summary. [p63, p74]

### v1, v1.x and later features
- v1 (App Store, Australia): duration learning, Lifestyles toggle, roster import, supply tracker, encrypted sync, Mac console, onboarding under 3 minutes, paywall with a 21 to 30-day trial, privacy page, support screen. [p74]
- Lifestyles move to v1 because switching needs at least one learned lifestyle to switch from. AC (inferred): the switch stays unavailable until one lifestyle has learned statistics. [p75]
- Roster import reads roster screenshots (Vision OCR). Manual shift entry always stays as the fallback. [p68, p73, p76]
- v1.x: focus shields (Screen Time), cycle overlay, errands and opening hours, Siri schemas, Apple cloud parsing, email and LMS feeds, N-of-1 experiments, weekly review, shareable log export. [p74]
- The shareable log export is user-initiated and gives clinicians a reason to mention the app. [p67, p74]
- US and UK release (after Gate 4): spelling and formulation names, local crisis lines, term structures, localised pricing. [p74]
- Minimum age is 18+ at launch (recommended decision). [p77]

## 2. Data model (entities, fields, types, retention)

- Section 16.1 lists only objects that are added or changed; "the original list stays". The original canonical object list is outside pages 61 to 80 and must be merged in. [p71]
- Medication: name (String); class (enum: stimulant, nonStimulant); prescribedPlanText (String, as the user wrote it); cutOffTime (time of day); minimumReLogInterval (duration). User-entered; the app never edits it. [p72]
- Product: brand (String); formulation (String); strength (String, or quantity plus unit); curvePrior (prior parameters for the focus-window model); storedLabelNote (String). A brand switch creates a new Product. [p72]
- DoseEvent: product (ref Product); timeTaken (Date); timeLogged (Date); source (enum: app, watch, healthImport); foodFlag (Bool); boosterFlag (Bool). Health imports are keyed by Health's ID (unique) for de-duplication. [p72]
- CheckIn: time (Date); answer (enum: yes, partly, no); energy (optional); mood (optional). Feeds the FocusWindowModel. [p72]
- FocusWindowModel (one per Product): gridPosteriorSummary; evidenceCount (Int); statedEstimate (the user's own estimate); manualOverride (optional). Shown only as "your focus pattern". [p72]
- Lifestyle: name (String); isActive (Bool); statisticsSnapshot (learned statistics for this lifestyle); episodeDates (date ranges when it was active); scheduledDates (planned switch dates). Dormant snapshots never update. [p72]
- Mode: ruleOverlay (set of Rules); startDate; endDate; stackingOrder (Int). Instances: Exam, Sick day, Travel. [p72]
- Rule: level (enum: safety, hard, temporary, learned, heuristic); scope (all lifestyles or one Lifestyle); sourceText (String); confirmation (user confirmation). Safety rules are compiled into the app and are not user-editable. [p72]
- LearnedPattern: statement (String); evidenceCount (Int); interval (uncertainty interval); status (enum: proposed, approved, rejected, coolingOff). A rejection starts a 30-day cool-off, so a rejection timestamp is needed (inferred). [p72]
- Task (changed): adds focusDemand (enum: deep, medium, light, body), lifestyleScope and lapseReasonCodes. MVP tasks also carry duration and deadline. The app infers most fields. [p72, p75]
- SupplyRecord: quantityOnHand; repeatsLeft (Int); scriptExpiry (Date); eScriptToken (String, sensitive); pharmacyNotes (String). Neutral supply view only. [p72]
- ExternalObjectLink: appUUID; externalID; calendarID; occurrenceDate (for recurring instances); reminderID; syncState. The app UUID is also written into the event's URL field. [p73]
- PlanRevision: before; after; actor; rule; reason; undoToken. Powers undo, restore and "why". [p73]
- ConsentRecord: category; scope; provider; country; version; time. Every consent can be withdrawn where it was given. [p73]
- Behaviour ledger entries record outcomes, check-ins, reason codes and lifestyle tags. The nightly learning stage reads them. [p71]
- Personal context holds the lifestyle, mode, rules, learned models and focus window. [p71]
- Data class "Medication" (medicines, dose logs, boosters, supply, check-ins, the focus-window model) lives in an encrypted on-device store. It leaves the device only three ways: to Private Cloud Compute (stateless, nothing kept), as end-to-end encrypted sync between the user's own devices, or in an export the user starts. [p67]
- Data class "Health-sourced" (sleep, heart rate, workouts, cycle, State of Mind) is read on demand and only derived features are kept. It never leaves raw: at most a non-health summary. [p68]
- Data class "Calendar and reminders" (events, reminders, the app's ID map) lives in Apple's stores plus the local ID map. It syncs through the user's own iCloud, Google or Exchange and is never end-to-end encrypted. [p68]
- Data class "Planning data" (tasks, ledger, rules, preferences, lifestyles) is on-device plus encrypted sync, and leaves only as encrypted sync. [p68]
- Data class "Text and screenshots for AI" (for example "Lunch at 1", roster images) is processed on-device or in Private Cloud Compute. It goes to third-party AI only if the user opted in, de-identified and with no health content. [p68]
- Data class "Location" (saved places, geofences) is on-device only and never leaves. [p68]
- Data class "Product analytics" (screen counts, crashes) is aggregated. It never comes from medication or health screens, and crash logs are scrubbed of medicine names. [p68]
- Local store: SwiftData with a local configuration (no CloudKit for health data). Widgets and extensions read a shared App Group container. [p73]
- File protection: iOS data protection on the main store. Widget and extension data in the shared container is readable after first unlock (Data Protection class completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication). [p70]
- Retention by AI tier: grammar keeps nothing; on-device Foundation Models keep nothing; Private Cloud Compute keeps data only until the response returns; third-party keeps up to about 30 days by default, zero with a retention agreement, and never trains on API data. [p70]
- Server retention (v1): ciphertext only, which the server cannot read. Delete-everything removes server copies and backups. [p69, p70]
- Export format: everything, as JSON and CSV. [p70]

## 3. Backend services, APIs, jobs, integrations

- The MVP is local-only: the iPhone holds everything and the Watch talks to the phone directly. There is no Mac or iPad and no sync. [p69, p75, p77]
- v1 adds the app's own end-to-end encrypted sync. Devices encrypt with keys only they hold, and a minimal server in an Australian region stores ciphertext it cannot read. This enables Mac and iPad. [p69, p73, p77]
- The minimal server has two jobs only: the encrypted sync relay and a silent-push backstop. [p73]
- Plain CloudKit sync of dose logs is ruled out (App Store rule 5.1.3(ii): apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud"). CloudKit with encrypted fields is allowed only with Apple's written confirmation [confirm]. [p68, p69, p77]
- There is no background daemon. Upkeep is event-driven, because iOS gives about 30 s of refresh at its own discretion and throttles silent push. [p61, p71]
- Calendar state is reconciled on every app wake. [p62]
- Calendar imports are idempotent. [p62]
- Learning runs on-device at launch and nightly (durations, completion, focus window, changes) and feeds the personal context each night. [p71, p73]
- The executor writes to Calendar, notifications, AlarmKit and Live Activities. The verifier re-reads every write and repairs or rolls back. [p71]
- Calendar integration uses EventKit across iCloud, Google-via-iOS and Exchange. [p62, p68, p73]
- Reminders integration uses EventKit, with reminder IDs kept in ExternalObjectLink. [p71, p73]
- Apple Health (HealthKit) is read-only. At launch the app reads doses (Apple's Medications data has been readable by other apps since iOS 26), sleep and workouts; heart rate and cycle come later. State of Mind is also listed as a Health-sourced type. [p65, p68, p71, p78]
- AI runs on Apple FoundationModels (on-device and via Private Cloud Compute), with Vision for OCR, plus an optional third-party model behind the same LanguageModel interface. [p73]
- The named third-party AI options are Claude and OpenAI, on a paid API tier: retention up to about 30 days by default, zero-retention available by agreement, and no training on API data. Gemini is excluded because its terms ban medical advice and apps likely to be used by under-18s. [p70]
- Third-party AI is off at launch and opt-in later. Free-form chat and third-party AI are out of the MVP. [p75, p77]
- Third-party AI costs about US$0.35 to $1.05 per active user a month, which is why the lifetime tier caps third-party AI use. [p63, p67]
- App Store subscriptions: annual plan, student price, hardship scholarship, optional lifetime, 21 to 30-day trial. Enrol in Apple's Small Business Program (15% commission below US$1M a year). [p67]
- A trial-end reminder goes out 2 days before the trial ends. [p62, p67]
- Email and LMS feeds are v1.x integrations. [p74]
- Roster import (v1) parses roster screenshots through the AI data flow. [p68, p74]
- Travel, opening hours and errands (post-MVP) use CoreLocation and MapKit. A Phase 0 spike tests Sydney transit ETA. [p73, p74, p75]
- Crash reporting must scrub medicine names. No third-party SDKs on any screen that shows health data. [p68, p70]
- Product analytics are opt-in and aggregated (screen counts, crashes). [p68]
- Operations: a written breach-response plan, an independent security test before launch and every year, and a claims register reviewed before every release. [p61, p70]
- Company setup: a legal entity (Pty Ltd) from day one. [p63, p74]

## 4. Algorithms and parameters (every number)

- The pipeline has 9 stages. Surfaces (Watch, widgets, Live Activities, Siri, app, Mac) → Interpreter (grammar → on-device model → Apple cloud → opt-in AI) → Personal context (lifestyle, mode, rules, learned models, focus window; fed read-only by Apple Health: sleep, doses) → Policy engine (safety red lines → hard rules → modes → preferences) → Solver and validator (places tasks; checks clashes, travel, hours, 60% cap) → Executor (Calendar, notifications, AlarmKit, Live Activities) → Verifier (re-reads every write; repairs or rolls back) → Behaviour ledger (outcomes, check-ins, reason codes, lifestyle tags) → Learning (nightly, on-device: durations, completion, focus window, changes), which feeds back into Personal context each night. [p71]
- Design rule: rules and a solver decide, the model only interprets, and learning loops back. [p71]
- Four changes to the original chain: a safety layer at the top of the policy engine; lifestyles and modes inside the personal context; event-driven upkeep instead of a background daemon; and an AI interpreter that tries free, private tiers before any cloud model. [p71]
- Precedence, highest first: safety red lines (compiled in) → user hard rules → active mode → active lifestyle's rules → approved learned preferences → optimisation heuristics. [p74]
- A learned preference never overrides a hard rule, and nothing overrides a safety rule, including the user's own chat instructions. [p74]
- Interpreter tiers, in order: 0 grammar (on-device code), 1 Apple Foundation Models (on-device), 2 Apple Private Cloud Compute (stateless), 3 third-party model (opt-in). [p70, p71]
- AI output handling: fixed output schemas; the solver checks that anything the AI proposes is feasible; previews before writes; parsed content never touches medication data. [p62]
- Every AI feature has a grammar or manual path, because Apple Intelligence needs an iPhone 15 Pro or later. [p62]
- Focus-window learner: plain Swift, using conjugate Bayesian updates, grid posteriors and change detection. It runs at launch and nightly. [p73]
- Prior: the user's own estimate comes first, and each Product also stores a curve prior. Label durations are unreliable for individuals: 57% of 1,327 lisdexamfetamine users report 7 h or less against a 14 h label (secondary source). [p61, p72, p79]
- Sparse data: use defaults and shrinkage. About 20% of check-ins go unanswered, and passive signals fill the gaps. [p62]
- Check-in cadence: 3 to 5 a day, tapering. [p75]
- The model outputs an 80% range for the window. Target: for at least 70% of medicated testers, that range is within ±30 minutes by day 10. [p75]
- Model state per product: grid posterior summary, evidence count, stated estimate, manual override. [p72]
- Early-estimate rule: high-stakes blocks (exams) never lean on an early estimate. The evidence threshold is not given in these pages. [p61]
- Duration learning uses personal duration multipliers with confidence. Calibration targets: P50 predictions hit 40 to 60% and P80 predictions hit 70 to 90%. [p66, p76]
- Built-in comparison: for consenting testers, alternate pairs of days with the learned duration multiplier on or off, to measure whether learning improves adherence. [p76]
- Solver: a small on-device constraint solver. It must plan a real week in under 1 second on an older iPhone, and the choice is proven in a 2-week spike against real week-long plans. [p73, p75]
- Solver constraints: place tasks (deep work inside the focus window) and check clashes, travel, hours and a 60% cap. [p71, p75]
- Lifestyles: routines and statistics are learned separately for each lifestyle, and a dormant lifestyle's snapshot never updates. [p66, p72]
- Learned-pattern status machine: proposed → approved or rejected; rejected → cooling off for 30 days. [p72]
- Modes stack by stacking order and have start and end dates. [p72]
- Calendar identity: the app UUID goes in the event URL field, plus an ExternalObjectLink map (external ID, calendar ID, occurrence date, reminder ID, sync state). Identity must survive calendar moves, recurring edits and Exchange. [p62, p73, p75]
- Health dose de-duplication: imported doses are keyed by Health's ID. [p72]
- Background budget: about 30 s of refresh at the system's discretion; silent push is throttled. [p61]
- Business numbers: the trial is 21 to 30 days, with a reminder 2 days before it ends. Trials of 17 to 32 days convert 42.5% against 25.5% for trials under 4 days. AI apps retain 36% worse over 12 months. Apple's commission is 15% under US$1M a year. [p63, p67]
- Market numbers: about 600,000 Australians were dispensed ADHD medication in 2023 to 24 (22 per 1,000 people, up from 8 in 2018 to 19). Adults were 49.9% of new patients in 2022 to 23 (32.8% in 2019 to 20). The US has 15.5M adults with current ADHD, 33.4% of whom took stimulants in the past year; the UK has about 1.9M adults with ADHD. Australian app-store rating counts run 7 to 9% of US counts for comparable planner apps. A 1% paid conversion of Australia's medicated population is about 6,000 subscribers. [p63, p66]
- Evidence behind shortage handling: 71.5% of US adults on stimulants had trouble filling scripts. [p61]
- Cited method references: Murphy (conjugate Bayesian analysis of the Gaussian); Adams and MacKay 2007 (online change-point detection); Liao et al. 2019 (Personalized HeartSteps); Buehler, Griffin and Ross 1994 (planning fallacy); Bei et al. 2014 (sleep in school vs vacation); the AHRQ N-of-1 trials guide; Nahum-Shani et al. 2018 (JITAI framework); the HeartSteps micro-randomised trial; Silverman and Barasch (broken streaks); W3C cognitive accessibility guidance. [p79]

## 7. Privacy, security, safety, compliance (build-relevant)

- Everything medication-related, including the inferred focus window, is health information. [p67]
- Medication data is collected only with specific opt-in consent, stays on the device where possible, syncs end-to-end encrypted outside iCloud, and never reaches advertising, analytics or third-party AI. [p67]
- Build to the strictest rule in every launch market from day one: Washington's opt-in, GDPR explicit consent and Apple 5.1.2(i). [p67]
- No ad or analytics SDKs anywhere health data appears. The OAIC ruled against tracking pixels on health data in June 2026. [p61]
- No third-party SDKs on any screen that shows health data; crash reports are scrubbed of medicine names. [p68, p70]
- Health-sourced data never leaves the device raw: at most a non-health summary such as "a lighter day". [p68]
- Location never leaves the device. [p68]
- Third-party AI gets only opt-in, de-identified productivity text: never medication, check-ins or Health data. [p68, p70]
- Third-party AI consent is explicit and names the provider and the country. [p68, p73]
- Gemini is excluded as a provider. [p70]
- Never claim "your data stays in Australia" unless a contract guarantees where inference runs [confirm]. [p70]
- Safety rules are compiled in: the user cannot edit them and chat cannot override them. [p72, p74]
- Parsed AI content never touches medication; screenshots are a prompt-injection vector (OWASP LLM01). [p62]
- An optional Face ID lock covers the app and the medication screens. [p70]
- iOS file protection on the store; shared-container data readable after first unlock. [p70]
- Discreet mode: no medicine names in notification text, calendar titles or Live Activities. Calendars are never end-to-end encrypted. [p68, p70]
- An independent security test before launch, repeated yearly. Gate 3 requires no high-severity findings. [p70, p76]

## 12. Edge cases, failure modes, mitigations

- The window is wrong for this person: the user's own estimate comes first, check-ins refine it, uncertainty is shown, and no high-stakes block rests on an early estimate. [p61]
- Irregular dosing and shortages: "No meds today", late and partial days re-plan instantly; shortage mode; plans stay usable unmedicated. [p61]
- iOS won't run the app in the background: event-driven upkeep, reconcile on every wake, and a silent-push backstop. [p61, p62, p73]
- Calendar sync breaks, duplicates or double-books: an app-owned calendar, UUIDs in the URL field, idempotent imports, reconcile on every wake, no edits to third-party events without a confirm, and QA across iCloud, Google-via-iOS and Exchange. [p62]
- Recurring events and Exchange: ExternalObjectLink stores the occurrence date, so identity survives recurring edits and calendar moves. [p73, p75]
- The user refuses full calendar access: explain first, offer a degraded mode with manual busy times, and let sensitive calendars be excluded. Write-only access cannot read even the app's own events. [p62]
- The Watch app is flaky or logs out: the Watch works offline with no login dependency, and logs are written locally and synced later. [p62]
- Learning looks dumb on sparse data (about 20% of check-ins unanswered; early estimates noisy): defaults and shrinkage, "early estimate" labels, passive signals. [p62]
- The AI hallucinates, or a screenshot hijacks it: fixed output schemas, a solver feasibility check, previews before writes, and parsed content never touches medication. [p62]
- Older iPhones without Apple Intelligence: every AI feature has a grammar or manual path. [p62]
- The user asks for dose timing ("just tell me when to take my booster"): fixed referral; the safety rule wins over chat. [p74]
- Brand switch: it creates a new Product, and the focus-window model is kept per product. [p72]
- Repeated Health imports: keyed by Health's ID, so one Health record never creates two DoseEvents. [p72]
- Rejected learned pattern: a 30-day cool-off before it can be proposed again. [p72]
- Switching lifestyles: a dormant lifestyle's snapshot never updates while another is active. [p72]
- Overlapping modes (for example Exam plus Sick day): resolved by stacking order. [p72]
- Privacy breach, real or perceived: no ad or analytics SDKs near health data, on-device processing, end-to-end encrypted sync, a minimal server and a written breach plan. [p61]
- Association with stimulant misuse (a digital-health stimulant prescriber's founder was sentenced to 72 months in July 2026): no "study hack" framing, no sharing features, clinician-reviewed claims, and a disclosure policy for creators. [p61]
- Subscription resentment ("ADHD tax"): a free core, a trial-end reminder 2 days before, one-tap cancel instructions, student and hardship pricing, and an optional lifetime tier. [p62]
- The trial ends before learning shows value: a 21 to 30-day trial, and show what was learned before the paywall. [p63]
- AI features churn and cost: present it as a planner, not a chatbot; on-device and Private Cloud Compute by default; cap third-party AI on the lifetime tier. [p63, p67]
- A bigger player copies the idea: ship the learned window plus scheduling first, stay Apple-native, and offer full data export so users trust switching. [p63]
- A planning service shutting down with a week's notice destroys trust, so users need a complete export (JSON and CSV). [p65, p70]
- Australia alone is too small a market: US and UK localisation from v1, with Australia as the beta. [p63]
- Scope outruns one builder: the phased roadmap, a legal entity from day one, and the cut order. [p63, p76]
- Known category failure modes to design out: frequent layout changes, weak or missable alerts, rigid minute-level blocks, Watch logouts, over-packed days (the solver's 60% cap likely addresses this; inferred) and AI hallucination. [p64, p71]
