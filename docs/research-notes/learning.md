## 1. User needs and pain points → the feature or behaviour that answers each

- Chronic underestimation of task time: only 29.7% of thesis students finished by their best estimate (33.9 days predicted vs 55.5 actual, ratio 1.64), and 37.1% to 43.6% finished on time in other studies → learn a personal duration multiplier per category from logged actuals and book estimate × multiplier instead of the raw estimate. [§1 Cited Findings; §2 Inferences]
- Padding does not fix it: "worst case" predictions were met by only 48.7%, and only 13%, 19% and 45% finished by their 50%, 75% and 99% estimates → book P80 lengths for deadline-critical work and add a computed day buffer rather than trusting padded guesses. [§1 Cited Findings; §2 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- ADHD time estimates are noisier, not only biased (greater absolute error and variability across timing meta-analyses; medication's effect on timing could not be assessed) → learn a per-category spread σ as well as a mean bias, show intervals, and learn each person's medicated vs unmedicated difference instead of assuming medication fixes estimates. [§1 Takeaway, Cited Findings and Inferences; §2 Inferences]
- Memory of past durations is biased short, and people rarely consult past experience when planning (7% of think-aloud content vs 74% future scenarios) → show the person their own logged actuals for the category at the moment they type an estimate; linking to past experience raised on-time completion from 29.3% to 60.0% (Buehler Study 4). [§1 Cited Findings and Inferences; §9 Cited Findings]
- The learning loop starves without actual times, and manual time entry is effortful → near-effortless capture: one-tap start/stop on iPhone and Watch, and auto-stop when the next task starts. [§8 Inferences]
- Big tasks are hard to estimate → subtask splitting (unpacking), with a stored "was split" flag used as a duration covariate; segmentation/unpacking, reference-class forecasting and implementation intentions are the literature's listed remedies. [§1 Cited Findings; §2 Inferences]
- One high-uncertainty task can consume about half of a day's expected time (7-task example with σ = 2) → Monte Carlo day totals, a floating buffer block, and an explicit flag on high-σ categories. [§2 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Medication labels overstate real-world duration (lisdexamfetamine: 57.3% of 1,327 users report 7 h or less vs a 13 to 14 h claim; Ritalin LA/Medikinet retard 4.6 h vs claimed 6 to 8 h; IR methylphenidate typically 3 to 4 h, as short as 1 h) → learn each person's own focus window from check-ins, starting from wide formulation priors. [§4 Takeaway and Cited Findings]
- Check-ins feel like a chore, but burden comes from questionnaire length, not prompt frequency (3, 6 and 9 prompts per day tested over 14 days) → single-item, one-tap check-ins placed only where the curve is uncertain, tapering once learned. [§4 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- About 1 in 5 check-ins will be missed (79% average compliance across 477 EMA articles) → models tolerate about 20% missing check-ins and never treat silence as an answer. [§4 Inferences; §10 Takeaway]
- Notification fatigue and habituation are named risks of just-in-time support → about 3 to 6 decision points per day, an availability gate, and a learned burden term so the app sends less when prompts stop working. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Some moments are unsafe or useless for prompts (driving, in class, asleep, Apple Watch workout, Focus/Do Not Disturb, and optionally the medication off-window) → a hard availability gate on every prompt. [§3 Inferences]
- Experimentation is felt as "weird suggestions" → exploration is scaled down (posterior variance × 0.5), capped at about 1 to 2 exploratory moves per day, and limited to low-stakes items. [§3 Inferences]
- Experimentation near deadlines is harmful → exploit-only for exams, assessments due within 48 h, medication reminders, P1/fixed items and anything marked "protected". [§3 Inferences]
- Routines change across the year (term, holidays, exam period, placement), so one model is wrong for part of the year → user-labelled lifestyles (day-type regimes) with their own routine parameters and instant switching. [§6 Takeaway]
- Relearning after every break wastes weeks → dormant lifestyles are frozen, switching back is instant, and next year's holidays reactivate from last year's stored snapshot. [§6 Inferences C and E]
- People forget to flip the toggle → a detector suggests (never forces) a switch, with its evidence and one-tap accept/undo. [§6 Inferences G]
- People flip the toggle late → "since Monday" back-dates the switch and re-attributes those days by replaying raw events. [§6 Inferences G]
- Holiday sleep drifts later for 1 to 2 weeks and does not snap back at term start (irregular sleepers' melatonin onset was 2 h 36 min later) → faster forgetting for the first 14 holiday days and a 3 to 5 day re-adaptation period on return to term. [§6 Inferences D; §7 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Opaque learning erodes trust → every learned number shows its evidence count, interval and maturity label; learned rules need explicit approval; a "what the app learned" screen allows reset per item. [§9 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Stated preferences and actual behaviour can disagree → stated preferences are strong priors and constraints; the app only asks to change them on strong evidence, and a "no" silences that suggestion for 30 days. [§9 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Not every missed item is a failure (clash, class ran over, exam) → one-tap lapse reason codes that keep rule-driven skips out of the learning data. [§10 Takeaway and Inferences]
- People log good days more than bad days → per-day logging coverage, low-coverage days excluded from completion learning, coverage shown to the user. [§10 Inferences]
- People lapse from logging for days → forgetting freezes during the gap, and return uses a low-burden "welcome back" mode without reading the gap as a lifestyle change. [§10 Inferences]
- Medication and ADHD data are health data → all learning on-device in plain Swift, raw logs kept on the phone, nothing exported by default. [§11 Takeaway and Inferences]
- People need a planning aid, not medical advice → the window is phrased "you usually report it working X to Y after dose", never as dosing advice, and dose reminders live in the user-set hard-rules layer. [§4 Inferences]

## 2. Features and behaviours (with acceptance criteria)

- F1 Personal duration booking: every task has a category and an optional estimate E; the planner books E × M_0.5 by default and E × M_0.8 when the task is deadline-critical, with M_q from the category posterior (see section 3). AC: in a new account with cold-start prior m₀ = log 1.4 to log 1.6, a 60 min estimate books 84 to 96 min at P50; with κ₀ = 4 the person's own mean carries weight 0.50 after 4 logged tasks, 0.71 after 10 and 0.86 after 25. [§2 Inferences; §1 Inferences]
- F2 Past actuals at estimate time: while the person types an estimate, show their logged range for that category, e.g., "your last 6 'essay drafting' sessions: 1.4× to 2.3× your estimate". AC: the range uses logged actuals only (never recalled or planned values); with n < 3 the prior is shown instead ("typical for people like you: ×1.4"). [§1 Inferences; §9 Inferences]
- F3 Timer capture: one-tap start/stop on iPhone and Watch; starting another task auto-stops the running one. AC: starting task B while A runs sets A's stop time to B's start time; no learning record ever defaults actual = planned (a display may, learning may not). [§8 Inferences; §10 Inferences]
- F4 Subtask splitting: any task can be split into subtasks. AC: a per-task "split" flag is stored and available as a duration covariate. [§1 Cited Findings; §2 Inferences]
- F5 Partial completion: the person can report "fraction f done after t minutes". AC: stores pseudo-actual A = t/f marked noisy (σ inflated ×1.5); e.g., 40 min at f = 0.5 records 80 min. [§2 Inferences]
- F6 Implausible-timer guard: AC: if actual A > 6E and A > 4 h, the app asks for confirmation (or winsorises) before learning from it; a 5 h actual on a 45 min estimate triggers the check, a 5 h actual on a 60 min estimate does not (6E = 6 h). [§2 Inferences]
- F7 Floating day buffer: AC: the day total is computed from 1,000 on-device Monte Carlo draws of each task's log-normal; a floating buffer block equal to P80(total) minus the sum of per-task medians is placed in the day; per-task P80s are never summed. [§2 Inferences]
- F8 High-uncertainty flag: categories with high σ are visibly flagged wherever they are planned. AC: flag shows on the category and on its tasks (the σ threshold is an open question). [§2 Inferences]
- F9 Lapse reason codes: when a planned item lapses, a one-tap sheet offers "Blocked by rule/event", "Chose not to / couldn't start", "Started, stopped", "Did it but didn't log"; ignoring it counts as "No response". AC: the sheet appears only on lapse; each code routes data as in section 3 (rule skip excluded, chosen skip = failure, started-stopped = censored, didn't-log asks for the actual, no response = missing). [§10 Inferences]
- F10 Medication list: the person adds each medication with formulation (source priors exist for IR methylphenidate, OROS methylphenidate/Concerta and lisdexamfetamine) and role (long-acting morning dose vs IR booster). AC: each formulation has its own global curve parameters; an IR booster never shares a parameter set with an ER morning dose. [§4 Inferences]
- F11 Dose logging: log time, medication and amount for each dose; several doses in a day superpose. AC: today's effect curve updates from logged doses as E_total = min(1, Σ E_d); dose reminders are user-set hard rules that the planner never moves or randomises; no screen gives dosing advice. [§4 Inferences; §3 Inferences; §9 Inferences]
- F12 Check-in learning phase: during the first 5 to 7 medicated days, 3 to 5 one-tap prompts per day, "Working now? Yes / Partly / No", at the times of maximum predictive entropy of P(yes). AC: each prompt is a single item; prompt times cluster around the uncertain onset and wear-off edges; unmedicated days do not count toward the 5 to 7. [§4 Inferences]
- F13 Check-in taper: AC: when the 80% credible interval of both t_on and t_off is narrower than ±30 min, prompting drops to about 1 prompt every 2 to 3 days; it does not taper while either interval is wider. [§4 Inferences]
- F14 Relearning triggers: AC: a logged dose or formulation change, or a change-point flag on the medication-window series, re-opens the learning phase (3 to 5 prompts per day again) with variance inflated (κ ← min(κ, 3)). [§4 Inferences; §5 Inferences]
- F15 Learned window card: per formulation, "works about 0:45 to 6:30 after dose; 80% CI ±40 min", plus evidence count and maturity label. AC: always expressed relative to dose time and framed as a planning aid; no wording recommends a dose, a dose time or an amount. [§4 Inferences; §9 Takeaway]
- F16 Today's focus curve: the planner reads E_total(t) from today's logged or scheduled doses in the active lifestyle. AC: moving one booster time changes only that dose's contribution. Derived: the Medication page's "productive windows" and "unproductive gaps" are the ranges where E_total(t) is above or below a threshold that the source does not set. [§4 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- F17 Watch heart-rate support: AC: the HR residual (HR minus the unmedicated baseline for the same activity and time of day) enters only as a low-weight likelihood term; a window is never produced from HR alone; HR that fails to rise after a logged dose flags a possibly missed or mis-timed dose log (Derived: flag as a question to the person, never auto-edit the log). [§4 Inferences; §10 Inferences]
- F18 Adaptive slot choice: flexible items are placed by Thompson sampling over eligible slots with posterior variance scaled by 0.5. AC: each placement logs its propensity; at most about 1 to 2 exploratory moves per day; none on exams, assessments due within 48 h, medication reminders, P1/fixed or protected items; nothing ever lands outside the item's user-approved window. [§3 Inferences]
- F19 Adaptive notifications: AC: about 3 to 6 decision points per day; zero sends while driving, in class, asleep, in an Apple Watch workout, in Focus/Do Not Disturb, or (if the person opts in) in the medication off-window; send probability clipped to [0.1, 0.8]; success = acted on within 30 min; every decision, sent or not, is logged with its probability. [§3 Inferences]
- F20 Lifestyles (day-type regimes): the person creates and names up to about 4 (source examples: term, holidays, exam period, placement) and toggles the active one. AC: switching is instant (snapshot pointer swap) with no relearning; a dormant lifestyle's learned values are unchanged when reactivated. [§6 Inferences C and F]
- F21 First-activation warm start: AC: a new lifestyle starts from the previous lifestyle's posterior with κ capped at 3 pseudo-days and predictive SD 1.5× the old one; wake and mid-sleep prior means come from the old lifestyle's free-day (weekend) values; the person's own data reaches 50% weight after 3 active days, 70% after 7 and 82% after 14. [§6 Inferences D]
- F22 Recurring activation: AC: reactivating a stored lifestyle sets κ ← κ/(1 + months since its last episode/6), capped at 7 pseudo-days; e.g., 6 months halves κ and 12 months divides it by 3. [§6 Inferences E]
- F23 Switch suggestion: AC: with both lifestyles learned, a prompt appears only when P(other lifestyle) > 0.9 on 2 consecutive days (sticky HMM, P(stay) = 0.97) or the log-likelihood-ratio CUSUM exceeds 3; it states the evidence and offers one-tap accept and undo; the app never switches on its own. [§6 Inferences G]
- F24 Back-dating: AC: confirming a switch "since Monday" re-attributes every logged event since Monday to the new lifestyle by replay, producing the same model state as if the toggle had been set on Monday. [§6 Inferences G]
- F25 Merge suggestion: AC: when two lifestyles' anchor predictive distributions are within Hellinger distance 0.2, the app suggests merging them. [§6 Inferences F]
- F26 Academic calendar: the person enters or imports, once, the university session and recess dates. AC: change-point hazard is raised around those dates, and they seed lifestyle detection when the holiday lifestyle has never been learned. [§6 Inferences G]
- F27 Routine anchors: wake, sleep onset, sleep midpoint and meal times are learned per lifestyle and per weekday type. AC: wake = end of the last asleep* sample of the main sleep episode; sleep onset = start of the first asleep* sample; inBed bounds used when stage edges are missing; first phone unlock or first Watch activity used as a cross-check when there is no Watch sleep data. [§6 Inferences A; §7 Inferences]
- F28 Routine change notice: AC: "Since Tuesday your wake time averages 9:10 vs 7:20; update routine?" appears only when all four hold: P(change within the last 3 days) > 0.8; shift > max(30 min, 1 SD of day-to-day variability); at least 2 independent signals agree; the shift has persisted 3 or more days. [§5 Inferences]
- F29 Stated preferences (Personalise page): preferred times per activity category are stored as priors worth κ = 10 to 15 observations. AC: the app asks to update one only when behaviour disagrees with P > 0.9 after n ≥ 10 comparable events; options are accept, reject, snooze; a rejection hides that suggestion for 30 days. [§9 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
- F30 Learned rule proposals (e.g., "move reading to mornings"): AC: proposed only with n ≥ 10 comparable events in the current lifestyle, P(effect > minimum worthwhile difference) ≥ 0.9, and a stable result over the last 2 weeks; nothing changes until the person approves. [§9 Inferences]
- F31 Maturity labels: AC: every learned number shows n, an interval and a label: n < 3 prior only; 3 ≤ n < 8 "early estimate (n = 5)" with a wide interval; n ≥ 8 with the 80% interval narrower than ×1.5 "learned" (§1 calls this last state "confident"; see section 12). [§9 Inferences; §1 Inferences]
- F32 "What the app learned" screen: AC: lists each learned parameter with n, interval and last change, and offers reset-to-prior per item that restores only that item's prior statistics. [§9 Inferences]
- F33 Personal experiments (N-of-1), e.g., 90 vs 60 min study blocks: AC: 6 to 8 blocks of 2 study days (one day per arm, order randomised within the block; 12 to 16 study days); outcomes = completed pomodoro-equivalents, self-rated focus 1 to 5, overrun minutes; the first block after any lifestyle switch is dropped; adopted only if P(θ > 0) > 0.8, the effect is at least the smallest worthwhile difference, and the person approves. [§3 Inferences]
- F34 Logging coverage: AC: each day stores coverage = share of planned items with an outcome, plus whether any passive data exist; coverage is visible in the interface; days under 30% coverage are excluded from completion learning. [§10 Inferences]
- F35 Welcome-back mode: AC: after 3 or more days with no logging, forgetting pauses (statistics identical before and after the gap), the return experience is low-burden, and the gap is not treated as a lifestyle change unless passive anchors say so. [§10 Inferences]
- F36 End-of-day realism rating: AC: the person can rate whether the day's plan was realistic, and the share of "realistic" days is tracked. [§12 Inferences]
- F37 On-device calibration tracking: AC: rolling 30-task coverage of P50 (target 0.5) and P80 (target 0.8), pinball loss at q = 0.5 and 0.8, Brier score and a 5-bin reliability table for completion, plan adherence (blocks done within ±30 min of plan), reschedules per day, end-of-day overrun minutes, suggestion acceptance rate, acted-within-30-min rate. [§12 Takeaway and Inferences]
- F38 Safe model upgrades: AC: a new model version is replayed over the person's stored history and compared with the current model on prequential log score before it is used. [§12 Inferences]
- F39 Explanations: AC: every number in an explanation is computed deterministically; the on-device language model may only rephrase, and any displayed number equals the computed value. [§9 Inferences]
- F40 Hard-rules layer: exams, fixed classes and events, medication reminders and a sleep-window floor, tagged per lifestyle. AC: no learned model can move, drop or reprioritise a hard-rule item; learning only changes soft costs. [§9 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- F41 Protection and priority tiers: AC: any item can be marked "protected"; items carry a priority tier, with P1 treated like a fixed item for exploration (tiered priorities follow the borrowed P1 to P4 pattern); fixed events are never overbooked. [§3 Inferences; §8 Inferences]
- F42 Approved windows: AC: a flexible item can carry a user-approved time window, and no automatic placement or move ever puts it outside. [§3 Inferences]
- F43 Passive imputation (permission-gated): AC: HealthKit sleep supplies wake/sleep; the HR residual supplies a weak "medication on" signal; Screen Time, Focus mode and calendar attendance supply "task probably done"; all are used as priors for a missing value, never stored as ground truth. [§10 Inferences]

## 3. Learning model: priors, likelihoods, updates, check-ins, confidence, cold start (every number)

- **Architecture:** conjugate Bayesian models with population ("outside view") priors and personal posteriors (partial pooling), all closed-form or small-grid arithmetic in plain Swift; state = sufficient statistics, a few kilobytes in total. [§1 Takeaway; §11 Takeaway; §6 Inferences B]
- **Why partial pooling:** on held-out data it beat no pooling (log predictive density -74.3 beta and -71.6 logit vs -97.2 no pooling) and shrinks thin estimates toward the population, so the app does not over-read 2 examples. [§1 Cited Findings; §9 Cited Findings]
- **Shrinkage weight:** posterior mean m_n = (κ₀m₀ + n·x̄)/(κ₀ + n); personal weight w = n/(n + κ₀); personal data dominates once n > κ₀; with κ₀ = 4, w = 0.50 at n = 4, 0.71 at n = 10, 0.86 at n = 25. [§1 Inferences]
- **Prior strengths κ₀ (pseudo-observations):** duration multiplier per category 3 to 5 (3 to 4 in the duration cold-start recipe); completion probability per slot 4 to 6; wake/sleep anchor mean 3 days; medication onset/offset about 2 to 3 check-in days (weak, from label data); stated time preference 10 to 15 (strong). [§1 Inferences; §2 Inferences; §9 Inferences]
- **Setting κ₀ from population data:** κ₀ = σ²/τ², τ² = between-person variance of the parameter. HeartSteps recipe for regression priors: significant features get prior mean = population point estimate and prior SD = SD of person-specific estimates (37 participants in their case); non-significant features get prior mean 0 and SD = half that SD. [§1 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **Fixed noise option:** HeartSteps fixed its noise variance σ² from earlier residuals "for algorithm stability" instead of updating it; a fallback for any regression that proves unstable. [§1 Cited Findings]
- **Empirical Bayes path:** at launch set m₀ and τ² from literature or pilot data; later from population data collected with privacy protection (local DP or federated; not v1). [§1 Inferences; §11 Inferences]
- **NIG conjugate update (exact):** prior NIG(m₀, V₀, a₀, b₀); V_n⁻¹ = V₀⁻¹ + n; m_n = V_n(V₀⁻¹m₀ + n·x̄); a_n = a₀ + n/2; b_n = b₀ + ½[m₀²V₀⁻¹ + Σx_i² - m_n²V_n⁻¹]; posterior predictive = Student-t with 2a_n degrees of freedom, location m_n, scale² = b_n(1 + V_n)/a_n. [§1 Cited Findings]
- **Update cadence:** posteriors updated nightly in batch (HeartSteps updated nightly; Thompson sampling copes with delayed or batched feedback better than UCB). [§1 Cited Findings; §3 Cited Findings; §11 Inferences]
- **Duration, observation:** y = log(actual A / estimate E) per (category, context); tasks with no estimate go into a separate NIG on y = log(A). [§2 Takeaway and Inferences]
- **Duration, likelihood and prior:** y ~ N(µ_c, σ_c²), (µ_c, σ_c²) ~ NIG(m₀, 1/κ₀, a₀, b₀); heavy tails are expected (estimate-vs-actual data: median blowup 1×, mean 1.81×, 99th percentile 32×), which the Student-t predictive handles at small n. [§2 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **Duration, cold start:** m₀ = log 1.4 to log 1.6 (thesis ratio 55.5/33.9 = 1.64; software mean blowup 1.81, median 1.0); a₀ = 2 to 3 (weak but proper); b₀ set so the prior-mean σ ≈ 0.5 to 0.6; κ₀ = 3 to 4; an ADHD-specific default multiplier must come from pilot data. [§2 Inferences; §1 Gaps]
- **Duration, quantiles:** M_q = exp(m_n + t⁻¹_{2a_n}(q)·s_n), s_n = √(b_n(1 + V_n)/a_n); block length = E × M_0.5 (median) or E × M_0.8 (deadline-critical); expected minutes for adding up a day = E × exp(m_n + s_n²/2). [§2 Inferences]
- **Duration, hierarchy:** µ_c = µ_user + δ_c, δ_c ~ N(0, τ_c²); a user-level NIG updated with all tasks provides m₀ for any new category, so a new category (e.g., "lab report") starts at the person's overall multiplier (population → user → category). [§2 Inferences]
- **Duration, lifestyle offset:** category multipliers are global with a small per-lifestyle offset δ_L ~ N(0, 0.1²) on the log scale. [§6 Inferences A]
- **Duration, covariates:** conjugate NIG Bayesian linear regression on y with time of day, medicated vs not, lifestyle, day of week, split into subtasks (unpacking effect) and task size bucket; at most 5 features until n > 50. [§2 Inferences]
- **Duration, censoring:** abandoned after c min → right-censored y > log(c/E), entering the likelihood via the survival function 1 - CDF; update by grid, Laplace, or a moment-matched pseudo-observation E[y | y > ℓ] = µ + σ·φ(α)/(1 - Φ(α)), α = (ℓ - µ)/σ, with extra variance. [§2 Takeaway and Inferences; §10 Cited Findings]
- **Duration, other rules:** partly done → pseudo-actual t/f with σ inflated ×1.5; skipped by a hard rule (clash, exam, cancelled) → excluded; implausible (A > 6E and A > 4 h) → confirm or winsorise; never learn from actual = planned defaults. [§2 Inferences; §10 Inferences]
- **Duration, precision needed:** with log-ratio SD ≈ 0.5, SE of the mean = 0.5/√n; ±20% (±0.18 on the log scale, 95%) needs n ≈ 30; ±40% needs n ≈ 7. [§1 Inferences]
- **Duration, forgetting:** λ = 0.99 per active day or none (slow-changing trait); after a detected change, durations are left alone unless the task content changed. [§5 Inferences]
- **Completion model:** logit P(complete) = βᵀx with about 20 features: sin(2πh/24), cos(2πh/24), sin(4πh/24), cos(4πh/24); weekday/free-day; lifestyle ID; predicted medication effect E(t); hours since wake; sleep-debt proxy; task category; calendar density; reminder sent (yes/no). [§3 Inferences]
- **Completion prior:** mean from population values or the person's stated preferred times; SD = between-person SD. [§3 Takeaway and Inferences]
- **Completion posterior:** diagonal-Gaussian Laplace approximation, mean = posterior mode, precision update q_i ← q_i + Σ_j x_ij² p_j(1 - p_j), run nightly in batch (Chapelle and Li Algorithm 3); negligible on-device cost. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **Completion, lifestyle structure:** β_L = β_global + Δ_L; hour harmonics and intercept are lifestyle-specific, category effects are global; a Beta(α, β) per slot bucket is also stored per lifestyle (Derived: its prior strength α₀ + β₀ = the slot κ₀ of 4 to 6). [§6 Inferences A and B; §1 Inferences]
- **Thompson sampling:** draw β̃ from the posterior with variance multiplied by α ≈ 0.5 (less exploration, lower regret); randomisation probabilities clipped to about [0.1, 0.8]. [§1 Cited Findings; §3 Takeaway and Inferences]
- **Beta forgetting:** α ← λα + (1 - λ)α₀, β ← λβ + (1 - λ)β₀ per active day. [§5 Inferences]
- **Receptivity model:** per-lifestyle logistic model; outcome = acted on within 30 min; burden covariate X_{t+1} = 0.95·X_t + 1{sent}; clipping φ(π) = min(1 - ε₀, max(π, ε₁)) with ε₀ = 0.2 and ε₁ = 0.1 so π ∈ [0.1, 0.8]; forgetting λ = 0.95 (about 20 days of memory); unavailable decision points are excluded from randomisation (HeartSteps enforced "no treatment" when the person might be driving). [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences; §5 Inferences; §6 Inferences A; §10 Cited Findings]
- **Medication, latent curve (Model A, default):** effect E(τ), τ = t - t_d (time since dose); 0 before t_on, linear rise to 1 at t_on + r, plateau until t_off - f, linear fall to 0 at t_off; r and f fixed per formulation (e.g., 30 to 60 min), leaving t_on and t_off as the 2 free personal parameters. [§4 Takeaway and Inferences]
- **Medication, likelihood:** P(yes at t_j) = ε₀ + (1 - ε₀ - ε₁)·E(t_j), lapse rates ε₀ ≈ 0.1 (says yes when off) and ε₁ ≈ 0.1 (says no when on); check-ins are a noisy binary or ordinal observation of the latent curve. [§4 Takeaway and Inferences]
- **Medication, posterior:** exact grid over t_on = 0 to 180 min and t_off = 2 to 16 h after dose, both in 15 min steps (13 × 57 = 741 cells); output such as "works about 0:45 to 6:30 after dose; 80% CI ±40 min". [§4 Inferences]
- **Medication, boosters:** E_total(t) = min(1, Σ_d E_d(t - t_d)); an IR booster and an ER morning dose use separate parameter sets; the curve relative to dose is global per formulation across lifestyles, while dose clock times are lifestyle-specific. [§4 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- **Medication, formulation priors (wide):** IR methylphenidate t_on 20 to 60 min, t_off mode about 3.5 h (range 1.5 to 5 h); Concerta t_on 45 to 90 min, t_off mode about 10 h (range 7 to 12 h); lisdexamfetamine t_on 60 to 120 min, t_off mode about 9 to 10 h (range 6 to 14 h). [§4 Inferences]
- **Medication, evidence behind priors:** Concerta label: first maximum at about 1 h, rising over the next 5 to 9 h, mean peak 6 to 10 h, half-life about 3.5 h; lisdexamfetamine beat placebo at every point from 2 to 14 h post dose (n = 103, 30/50/70 mg); real-world self-reports: IR methylphenidate mean 3.06 h, Concerta 10.2 h (IQR 7.5 to 11.5), Ritalin LA/Medikinet retard 4.6 h (IQR 3.4 to 6.0), dextroamphetamine peak at 4.4 h. [§4 Cited Findings]
- **Medication, food:** the Concerta label reports no PK/PD difference after a high-fat breakfast, so the model needs no food-effect term for Concerta (Derived implication). [§4 Cited Findings]
- **Medication, Model B (prior shape only):** C(t) = Σ_d D_d·A·(e^(-k_e(t - t_d - t_lag)) - e^(-k_a(t - t_d - t_lag)))·1[t > t_d + t_lag], effect E = C^h/(EC₅₀^h + C^h); k_e from half-life (Concerta 3.5 h → k_e ≈ 0.2 per h); OROS needs a two-input release profile; do not fit Model B per person (mean absorption time is individually biased even with concentration data). [§4 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **Medication, not needed:** Gaussian-process regression (n³/6 Cholesky cost) is unnecessary given the grid posterior. [§4 Cited Findings; §11 Cited Findings]
- **Medication, check-in schedule:** learning phase = first 5 to 7 medicated days, 3 to 5 prompts per day at maximum predictive entropy of P(yes); taper to about 1 prompt per 2 to 3 days once both 80% CIs are under ±30 min; single item only; expect about 20% missing. [§4 Inferences]
- **Medication, heart rate:** residual = HR minus the unmedicated baseline for the same activity and time of day; Gaussian likelihood term with effect about 5 bpm and a large SD (pooled stimulant effect +5.7 bpm, 95% CI 3.6 to 7.8, 10 RCTs, n = 2,665); used to fill gaps between check-ins and flag missed or late dose logs; never sets the window alone. [§4 Takeaway, Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **Medication, relearning:** a dose or formulation change or a change-point flag re-opens learning: inflate variance, κ ← min(κ, 3). [§4 Inferences; §5 Inferences]
- **Routine anchors:** NIG (m, κ, a, b) per anchor (wake, sleep onset, sleep midpoint, meals) per weekday type per lifestyle; κ₀ = 3 days; forgetting λ = 0.97 to 0.98 per active day (about 33 to 50 days of memory). [§1 Inferences; §5 Inferences; §6 Inferences A and B]
- **General forgetting on NIG:** κ ← λκ + (1 - λ)κ_floor, a ← λa + (1 - λ)a_floor, with the sums scaled to match; effective memory ≈ 1/(1 - λ) days; applied only on active days and only to the active lifestyle's lifestyle-specific statistics. [§5 Inferences; §6 Inferences C]
- **Change detection, BOCPD:** run on wake time, sleep midpoint, first-activity time, medication-window reports and duration log-ratios; constant hazard H = 1/60 per day for routine anchors; Student-t predictive from NIG; run lengths with probability < 10⁻⁴ pruned so cost per day stays roughly constant. [§5 Takeaway, Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **BOCPD recursion:** growth P(r_t = r_{t-1} + 1, x_{1:t}) = P(r_{t-1}, x_{1:t-1})·π_t·(1 - H); change P(r_t = 0, x_{1:t}) = Σ P(r_{t-1}, x_{1:t-1})·π_t·H; normalise by P(x_{1:t}); conjugate updates ν ← ν + 1, χ ← χ + u(x_t). [§5 Cited Findings]
- **Known triggers:** a logged dose or formulation change, or a new calendar pattern (new job or timetable), raises the hazard to about 1/7 for the next 14 days. [§5 Inferences]
- **CUSUM (cheap alarm):** S_hi = max(0, S_hi + x - µ̂₀ - k), S_lo = max(0, S_lo + µ̂₀ - k - x); k = δ/2 (half the shift to detect), alarm at h ≈ 4 to 5 σ; efficient for shifts of 2σ or less. [§5 Cited Findings]
- **Genuine-shift rule (all required):** P(r_t ≤ 3 days | data) > 0.8; post-change mean differs by more than max(30 min, 1 SD of day-to-day variability); at least 2 independent signals agree (e.g., wake time and first phone use or first Watch activity); persists 3 or more days. [§5 Takeaway and Inferences]
- **Drift types covered:** sudden (BOCPD), gradual and incremental (forgetting), recurring (stored lifestyle snapshots reused rather than retrained). [§5 Cited Findings; §6 Cited Findings]
- **Lifestyle freeze:** lifestyle-specific statistics update and decay only while their lifestyle is active; global parameters keep updating under every lifestyle. [§6 Inferences C]
- **Lifestyle warm start (tempered "power" prior):** m_new = m_old + Δ_pop; κ_new = min(κ_old, 3); a, b scaled so predictive SD ≈ 1.5× old; personal weight n/(n + 3) = 50% at 3 days, 70% at 7, 82% at 14 (adapts in about 3 to 7 days); λ = 0.90 per day for the first 14 days of a holiday episode, then 0.97. [§6 Takeaway and Inferences D]
- **Holiday anchor priors:** wake mean = term free-day wake, or term weekday wake + 50 to 90 min if free-day data are sparse (§6 says +50 to 60 min); prior SD 1.5× term SD; sleep midpoint = term free-day midpoint (social jetlag assumed to collapse); expect higher day-to-day variability. [§7 Inferences; §6 Inferences D]
- **Recurring lifestyle:** κ ← κ/(1 + Δmonths/6), capped at κ ≤ 7 pseudo-days, so last year's pattern informs day 1 and this year's data dominates within about a week. [§6 Inferences E]
- **Return to term:** variance inflation for the first 3 to 5 days back. [§7 Inferences]
- **Switch detector:** 2-state HMM forward filter over daily vectors (wake time, sleep midpoint, first-activity time, calendar class hours, campus visit yes/no if permitted, step pattern); emissions = each lifestyle's Student-t predictive; P(stay) = 0.97; prompt when P(other) > 0.9 for 2 consecutive days. Cheap equivalent: S_t = max(0, S_{t-1} + log p(x_t|L2) - log p(x_t|L1)), prompt at S_t > 3 (odds about 20:1). Holiday lifestyle never learned: BOCPD on anchors plus academic calendar dates. [§6 Inferences G]
- **No MAML:** gradient meta-learning is unnecessary; the hierarchical prior gives the same few-sample adaptation in closed form on-device. [§6 Takeaway and Inferences H]
- **Maturity ladder (effective n after forgetting):** n < 3 prior only; 3 ≤ n < 8 "early estimate"; n ≥ 8 and 80% interval narrower than ×1.5 "learned" (§1 variant: "learned" at n ≥ 8, "confident" once the 80% predictive interval of the multiplier is narrower than ×1.5). [§9 Takeaway and Inferences; §1 Inferences]
- **Decision thresholds:** learned rule n ≥ 10, P(effect > MWD) ≥ 0.9, stable 2 weeks, approval; stated-preference challenge P > 0.9 after n ≥ 10, 30-day cool-off on rejection; N-of-1 adoption P(θ > 0) > 0.8 and effect ≥ MWD; medication taper both 80% CIs < ±30 min; switch prompt P > 0.9 for 2 days or CUSUM > 3; change notice P(r_t ≤ 3) > 0.8. [§9 Inferences; §3 Inferences; §4 Inferences; §5 Inferences; §6 Inferences G]
- **Missing data, routing:** blocked by rule/event → unavailable, excluded from completion and duration models; chose not to / couldn't start → completion failure for that slot and context; started, stopped → duration censored at elapsed time; did it but didn't log → ask for the actual, else duration missing and completion = success; no response → missing. [§10 Inferences]
- **Missing data, coverage:** weight days by inverse coverage probability, or exclude days under 30% coverage from completion models; days where passive data look normal but the app was not opened are missing-not-at-random (neither success nor failure). [§10 Inferences]
- **Missing data, gaps:** 3 or more days without logging freeze forgetting (no decay toward the prior on empty days). [§10 Inferences]
- **N-of-1 analysis:** y = µ + θ·arm + block effect; report P(θ > 0) and posterior median; blocks of 2 periods with randomised or counterbalanced order; discard or down-weight early periods for carryover; analyse each outcome separately; suits fast onset/offset interventions. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- **Offline tuning:** κ₀, forgetting λ, BOCPD hazard and the lifestyle-detector threshold are tuned by replay to maximise prequential log score and minimise false switch prompts; unbiased replay keeps only events where the logged action matches the candidate policy's action and needs logged propensities (π clipped to [0.1, 0.8]). [§12 Cited Findings and Inferences]

## 4. Scheduler and planning rules and parameters

- Two layers: a hard-constraint rules layer that is never learned, and a soft-cost objective; learned models may only change soft costs. [§9 Inferences]
- Hard rules: exams, fixed classes, fixed events, medication reminders and a sleep-window floor, each tagged to the lifestyle(s) it applies to. [§9 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- Fixed events are never overbooked; items carry priority tiers (P1 highest; P1 to P4 is the borrowed pattern), and P1/fixed items are exploit-only. [§8 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
- Stated preferences act as strong priors and as constraints; behaviour can only suggest changes to them. [§9 Takeaway]
- Candidate slots are generated from the hard constraints and each item's user-approved window; only eligible slots are scored. [§3 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Slot scoring: sample β̃ from the completion posterior (variance × 0.5), choose the argmax of P(complete) × value, and log the implied propensity for later evaluation. [§3 Inferences]
- Exploit-only (no randomisation) for exams, assessments due within 48 h, medication reminders, P1/fixed items and anything marked "protected"; this is an availability gate like HeartSteps'. [§3 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Exploration is allowed only for flexible, low-stakes items (habits, errands, optional study blocks) and is capped at about 1 to 2 exploratory moves per day. [§3 Inferences]
- No automatic placement or move ever puts an item outside its user-approved window. [§3 Inferences]
- Exploration principle: stay above a fixed performance baseline uniformly over time (conservative bandit); the rules above are its operational form. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Block length: E × M_0.5 by default; E × M_0.8 for deadline-critical work (reference-class uplifts depend on accepted risk, e.g., roads +15% at P50 vs +32% at P80). [§2 Takeaway and Inferences; §1 Cited Findings]
- Booking example: 90 min planned × 1.6 → "booking 2 h 25 min" (Derived: the example implies rounding booked lengths to 5 min; rounding rule not stated). [§9 Inferences]
- Day totals: add per-task means E × exp(m_n + s_n²/2), or run 1,000 Monte Carlo draws on-device; never add per-task P80s (too conservative); means add, medians and quantiles do not. [§2 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Floating buffer block = P80 of the day total minus the sum of per-task medians. [§2 Inferences]
- High-σ categories are flagged, since one such task can dominate the day total. [§2 Inferences]
- Medication-aware placement: today's E_total(t) (logged and scheduled doses, boosters superposed) is a feature of P(complete), so slots with high learned effect score higher for categories where the data show it; the source defines no hard "focus work only inside the window" rule. [§3 Inferences; §4 Inferences]
- Hours since wake (from the active lifestyle's anchors for today's weekday type) and weekday/free-day are completion features; calendar density is also a feature. [§3 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- Switching lifestyle changes which snapshot the planner reads, in O(1), with no relearning. [§6 Inferences C]
- Items skipped because of a rule or clash are "unavailable": they do not lower that slot's learned completion probability. [§10 Inferences]
- Assessments due within 48 h are exploit-only, so every assessment needs a due date the planner can read. [§3 Inferences]
- Notification plan: about 3 to 6 decision points per day (HeartSteps used 5); availability gate (Focus/Do Not Disturb, in class, driving, asleep, Watch workout, medication off-window if chosen); send/no-send or timing randomised with π ∈ [0.1, 0.8]; proximal outcome = acted on within 30 min; burden X_{t+1} = 0.95X_t + 1{sent} as a covariate. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Design each prompt type with the JITAI components: distal outcome, proximal outcome, decision points, tailoring variables, intervention options and decision rules. [§3 Cited Findings]
- Medication check-in prompts go at the maximum-entropy times (3 to 5 per day in the learning phase, then about 1 per 2 to 3 days). Derived: they pass the same availability gate as other prompts. [§4 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
- Dose reminders sit in the hard-rules layer: never moved, never randomised, never explored. [§4 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
- Learned rules (e.g., "move reading to mornings") change the plan only after explicit approval. [§9 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Plan-quality measures: adherence = share of blocks done within ±30 min of plan; reschedules per day; end-of-day overrun minutes. [§12 Inferences]
- The 90 vs 60 min block question is answered by a randomised N-of-1 (2-day blocks, 6 to 8 blocks) and adopted only with approval, not by silent exploration. [§3 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Derived: social plans found in the calendar are fixed events (hard constraints) unless the person marks them movable; a suggestion to move one is a proposal that needs explicit approval, never an automatic move. [Not in source; derived from §9 Inferences and §3 Inferences]
- Derived: the daily top-5 priorities list is not covered; if the on-device model drafts it, any durations, times or counts in it must come from the deterministic models. [Not in source; derived from §9 Inferences]

## 5. Lifestyles and life stages: what each needs, default day types to ship

- Model: lifestyles are a small set of user-labelled recurring regimes (source: Lifestyle 1 = term, Lifestyle 2 = holidays), each with a frozen-when-dormant snapshot, inside a hierarchical model with shared global parameters. Derived: the product's "day types" map onto lifestyles, with weekday types (workday vs free day) inside each lifestyle. [§6 Takeaway and Inferences A]
- Cap: about 4 user-named lifestyles (source examples: term, holidays, exam period, placement); suggest merging two whose anchor predictive distributions are within Hellinger distance 0.2. [§6 Inferences F]
- Global per person (shared by all lifestyles): duration multiplier per category (plus offset δ_L ~ N(0, 0.1²)), medication curve relative to dose per formulation, category effects on completion. [§6 Inferences A]
- Lifestyle-specific: dose clock times and booster habits; wake, sleep and meal anchors (mean and SD per weekday type); hour-of-day harmonics and intercept of the completion model; notification receptivity (class vs no class changes availability). [§6 Inferences A]
- Hard rules (exams, fixed events, medication reminders) are tagged per lifestyle and never learned. [§6 Inferences A]
- University student, term (Lifestyle 1): classes are hard rules; calendar class hours and campus visits (if permitted) feed the lifestyle detector; assessments due within 48 h are protected; an exam period can be its own lifestyle. [§6 Inferences F and G; §3 Inferences; §9 Inferences]
- University student, holidays (Lifestyle 2) evidence: when constraints were removed (n = 139) weekday bedtime moved about 50 min later and weekday sleep +30 min, weekend bedtime about 25 min later and weekend sleep +24 min, social jetlag fell, sleep became more regular, and 92% got at least 7 h. [§7 Cited Findings]
- University student, holidays prior: wake mean = term free-day wake (else term weekday wake + 50 to 90 min), prior SD 1.5× term SD, sleep midpoint = term free-day midpoint, higher variability, drift over the first 1 to 2 weeks (λ = 0.90 for 14 days, then 0.97). [§7 Inferences; §6 Inferences D]
- School students and school holidays (adolescents, n = 146, age 16.2 ± 1.0): an abrupt delay in sleep timing at the start, further linear delays through the break, stabilising in the second week; later, longer, lower-quality and more variable sleep; eveningness and older age go with later timing. [§7 Cited Findings; §6 Cited Findings]
- Return to term or school: do not assume wake times snap back; re-adaptation with variance inflation for the first 3 to 5 days back; irregular sleepers (Sleep Regularity Index 52 ± 10 vs 84 ± 2) had melatonin onset 2 h 36 min later (00:08 vs 21:32), and regularity correlated with GPA (r = 0.37). [§7 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Social jetlag (mid-sleep on workdays minus free days; reported in over 70% of people by at least 1 h, secondary source) is why free-day anchors are the best personal holiday prior. [§7 Cited Findings and Takeaway]
- Semester boundaries and job changes: expect an intrinsic routine change every couple of months (hazard 1/60 per day); a new timetable or job raises the hazard to 1/7 for 14 days. [§5 Inferences]
- Medicated people across lifestyles: dose clock time follows wake time (so it shifts in holidays), while the effect curve relative to dose does not change. [§4 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- "Uni day with a long-acting dose and boosters" (product example): ER morning dose and IR boosters are separate formulations with separate parameter sets, superposed with E_total = min(1, Σ E_d); their clock times are stored per lifestyle. [§4 Inferences; §6 Inferences A]
- Derived, people who skip medication on some days (e.g., weekends): log an explicit "no dose today" so the day is an unmedicated day (E(t) = 0), not missing; the "medicated vs not" duration covariate and the E(t) completion feature learn the difference; unmedicated days supply the HR baseline; the 5 to 7 day learning phase counts only medicated days. [Not in source; derived from §2 Inferences, §3 Inferences, §4 Inferences and §10 Takeaway]
- Derived, athletes and frequent exercisers: Watch workouts block prompts; the HR residual must compare like-for-like activity, and activity-driven HR swings make HR evidence weak, so its weight stays low. [Not in source; derived from §3 Inferences and §4 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Not in source, shift workers: Derived notes only: anchors come from the "main sleep episode", which may be in daytime; "hours since wake" is clock-independent while the hour-of-day harmonics assume a stable 24 h pattern; a shift pattern could be a lifestyle (counting toward the cap of about 4) or a weekday type. [Not in source; derived from §3 Inferences, §6 Inferences A and F, §7 Inferences]
- Not in source: parents and carers, and travellers; see sections 11 and 12. [Not in source]

- Estimate-time evidence: "your last 6 'essay drafting' sessions: 1.4× to 2.3× your estimate"; drawn from logged actuals only. [§1 Inferences]
- Evidence shown to people always comes from logged data, never recalled or planned values. [§9 Cited Findings; §10 Inferences]
- Check-in wording: "Working now? Yes / Partly / No"; one item, nothing more. [§4 Inferences]
- Medication window wording: "works about 0:45 to 6:30 after dose; 80% CI ±40 min" or "you usually report it working 0:45 to 6:30 after dose"; times are relative to the dose (h:mm after dose). [§4 Inferences]
- Medication copy is a planning aid only: never dosing advice (no suggested dose, time or amount). [§4 Inferences]
- Routine change: "Since Tuesday your wake time averages 9:10 vs 7:20; update routine?"; every detected change is explained. [§5 Inferences]
- Preference challenge: "You've finished 11 of 12 study blocks after 2 pm but 3 of 9 before 11 am; update your preferred time?" with Accept, Reject and Snooze. [§9 Inferences]
- Stated-preference entry example: "I study best 9 to 11". [§9 Inferences]
- Lifestyle switch: "Last 3 days: up at 9:40 (term avg 7:15), no classes. Switch to Holidays? Your holiday settings from July are ready." with one-tap accept and undo; phrased as a suggestion, never an automatic switch. [§6 Inferences G]
- Late switch: offer back-dating ("since Monday"). [§6 Inferences G]
- Lapse reason labels: "Blocked by rule/event", "Chose not to / couldn't start", "Started, stopped", "Did it but didn't log"; shown only when an item lapses. [§10 Inferences]
- Return after 3 or more days away: a "welcome back" low-burden mode. [§10 Inferences]
- Learned rules are always proposals needing approval (e.g., "move reading to mornings"). [§9 Takeaway and Inferences]
- "What the app learned" rows: parameter, n, interval, last change, and "reset to prior". [§9 Inferences]
- Experiment results report P(θ > 0) and the posterior median before asking for approval. [§3 Inferences]
- Logging coverage is shown in the interface; high-σ categories are flagged explicitly (wording not specified). [§10 Inferences; §2 Inferences]
- Numbers are generated deterministically; the on-device language model may only phrase them and must never compute them. [§9 Inferences]

## 9. Accessibility and neurodivergent-design requirements

- One-tap, single-item prompts only: burden comes from questionnaire length, not prompt frequency. [§4 Cited Findings and Inferences; §10 Takeaway]
- Prompt budget: medication check-ins 3 to 5 per day only during the 5 to 7 day learning phase, then about 1 per 2 to 3 days; general decision points about 3 to 6 per day. [§4 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
- Learn habituation (burden variable, λ = 0.95) so the app backs off when prompts are ignored. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Respect availability: no prompts in Focus/Do Not Disturb, class, driving, sleep or workouts, and an opt-in mute for the medication off-window. [§3 Inferences]
- Reason-code sheet only when an item lapses, one tap to answer. [§10 Inferences]
- Externalise time: show the person's own logged durations when they estimate, and show intervals rather than single numbers (ADHD estimates are more variable). [§1 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Near-effortless time capture: Watch start/stop and auto-stop on the next task. [§8 Inferences]
- Support unpacking tasks into subtasks, and implementation intentions (listed remedies for the planning fallacy). [§1 Cited Findings]
- Predictability: limited exploration (variance × 0.5, 1 to 2 moves per day, low-stakes items only, never outside approved windows), so the plan does not feel random. [§3 Inferences]
- Control: never auto-switch lifestyles; one-tap accept and undo; approval for learned rules; accept, reject or snooze on preference challenges; 30-day cool-off; reset per learned item. [§6 Inferences G; §9 Inferences]
- Every change is explained with before and after numbers. [§5 Inferences; §9 Inferences]
- Lapses do not erase progress: forgetting freezes after 3 or more days away, the return is a low-burden "welcome back" mode, and the gap is not treated as a lifestyle change. [§10 Inferences]
- Rule-driven skips (clash, class ran over, exam) are never counted as failures. [§10 Inferences]
- Evening tilt: do not plan as if holiday wake times will snap back at term start; allow 3 to 5 days of re-adaptation. [§7 Inferences]
- The best visual format for intervals on a phone or Watch (e.g., quantile dotplots) is unresolved and needs testing. [§9 Gaps]
- Not covered by this source: Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, contrast and Reduce Motion requirements (need a separate spec). [Not in source]

## 10. Privacy, security and safety requirements (build-relevant only)

- All learning on-device; raw logs stay on the phone; nothing exported by default (medication and ADHD data are health data). [§11 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Model state is sufficient statistics (a few kilobytes) in the app container. [§6 Inferences B; §11 Takeaway]
- Cross-device sync only through CloudKit private database / end-to-end encrypted containers; the phone owns model state and syncs it to the Watch. [§11 Inferences]
- Passive signals are permission-gated: location (campus visit) only "if permitted"; Screen Time, Focus and calendar attendance only "where permitted". [§6 Inferences G; §10 Inferences]
- Population-prior learning, if ever added, uses local differential privacy (Apple used ε = 4 with daily per-use-case submission limits) or federated aggregation with DP noise, requires a server and consent, and is out of scope for v1. [§8 Cited Findings; §11 Inferences]
- Replay on anything beyond the person's own device uses a consenting pilot cohort only. [§12 Inferences]
- Medication safety: the learned window is a planning aid, never dosing advice; dose reminders are user-set hard rules that are never randomised, explored or moved. [§4 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
- Heart rate never sets the medication window on its own; passive signals are priors for missing values, never ground truth. [§4 Inferences; §10 Inferences]
- Notification safety: nothing is sent while the person may be driving (HeartSteps enforced "no treatment" when driving was indicated) or in the other unavailable states. [§3 Cited Findings and Inferences; §10 Cited Findings]
- The language model never computes numbers; displayed numbers are deterministic. [§9 Inferences]
- Foundation Models has a Private Cloud Compute option; whether any health-related text may leave the device is a decision to make (see section 12). [§11 Cited Findings]
- User control over learned data: per-item reset to prior, approval before learned rules act, back-dating and undo on lifestyle switches. [§9 Inferences; §6 Inferences G]
- A legal/compliance review is outside the source's scope. [§11 Inferences]

## 11. Edge cases, failure modes and mitigations (time zones, travel, DST, missed logs, shift work)

- Missed check-ins (expect about 20%): treat as missing, never as "no"; keep prompts single-item. [§4 Inferences; §10 Takeaway]
- Missing sleep-stage edges: take wake = end of the last asleep* sample and onset = start of the first asleep* sample, falling back to inBed bounds. [§7 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- No Watch sleep data: cross-check anchors with first phone unlock or first Watch activity. [§7 Inferences]
- HR confounded by activity, caffeine and stress (pooled drug effect only +5.7 bpm): compare to a same-activity, same-time baseline, keep weight low, never use HR alone. [§4 Inferences]
- Missed or late dose log: a logged dose with no HR rise flags a possible mis-timed or missed log. [§4 Inferences]
- Dose or formulation change: re-open the learning phase and raise the change hazard to 1/7 for 14 days. [§4 Inferences; §5 Inferences]
- IR booster and ER dose on the same day: separate parameter sets, superposed with a cap of 1 (min(1, Σ E_d)). [§4 Inferences]
- Label vs real-world duration mismatch: wide priors, learned from check-ins. [§4 Takeaway and Cited Findings]
- Grid floor: t_off starts at 2 h, but the IR methylphenidate prior range starts at 1.5 h and reports go as short as 1 h, so very short responders would be truncated (see section 12). [§4 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Abandoned task: right-censored at elapsed time, never logged as a completed duration. [§2 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Partly done task: pseudo-actual t/f with σ × 1.5. [§2 Inferences]
- Clash, exam or cancellation: excluded from duration and completion learning. [§2 Inferences; §10 Inferences]
- Timer left running or implausible value (A > 6E and A > 4 h): confirm or winsorise. [§2 Inferences]
- Task with no estimate: learned in a separate NIG on log(actual). [§2 Inferences]
- New category: starts at the person's overall multiplier, not the population one. [§2 Inferences]
- One high-σ task dominating the day: Monte Carlo total, floating buffer, explicit flag. [§2 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- Logging only good days: coverage tracking; days under 30% coverage excluded or inverse-coverage weighted. [§10 Inferences]
- Active day with the app unopened (passive data normal): missing-not-at-random, counted as neither success nor failure. [§10 Inferences]
- Logged "did it" without an actual: ask for the actual; else duration missing, completion = success. [§10 Inferences]
- Planned time shown as actual in the UI: never feed it to learning (it would pull multipliers toward 1). [§10 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- 3 or more days without logging: freeze forgetting, welcome-back mode, no lifestyle change unless passive anchors show one. [§10 Inferences]
- Forgotten lifestyle toggle: HMM or CUSUM suggestion after 2 consecutive days above P = 0.9 (or S_t > 3). [§6 Inferences G]
- Late or mistaken toggle: back-date "since Monday" and replay raw events. [§6 Inferences G]
- One late night: must not trigger a switch or change notice (sticky P(stay) = 0.97, persistence of 3 or more days, 2 or more agreeing signals). [§6 Cited Findings and Inferences G; §5 Inferences]
- Holiday lifestyle never learned: BOCPD on anchors plus academic calendar dates, with raised hazard around them. [§6 Inferences G]
- Continued holiday drift in weeks 1 to 2: λ = 0.90 per day for the first 14 days, then 0.97. [§6 Inferences D; §7 Inferences]
- Return to term: no snap-back assumption; variance inflation for 3 to 5 days. [§7 Inferences]
- Recurring lifestyle after many months: κ divided by (1 + months/6), capped at 7. [§6 Inferences E]
- Near-duplicate lifestyles: suggest merge at Hellinger < 0.2; more than about 4 lifestyles: cap. [§6 Inferences F]
- Carryover after a lifestyle switch during an experiment: drop the first block. [§3 Inferences]
- Exploration near deadlines: blocked for exams, assessments within 48 h, medication reminders, P1/fixed and protected items. [§3 Inferences]
- Stated preference contradicted by behaviour: ask only after n ≥ 10 and P > 0.9; a rejection starts a 30-day cool-off. [§9 Inferences]
- Change detected in routines: re-open learning for medication and anchors (κ ← min(κ, 3)); leave durations alone unless task content changed. [§5 Inferences]
- The language model alters a number: not allowed; numbers come only from deterministic code. [§9 Inferences]
- Device without Apple Intelligence: Foundation Models is unavailable, so explanations need a deterministic template path (Derived fallback). [§9 Cited Findings; §11 Cited Findings]
- Background processing limits on iOS 26 unverified: the nightly update may not run every night. [§11 Gaps]
- Derived, time zones and travel: store every event as an absolute instant plus the local time zone in effect; compute dose-relative quantities (τ = t - t_d, E(t)) from elapsed time so the learned curve is unaffected by travel; replay and back-dating need the stored zone to rebuild local-time features. [Not in source; derived from §4 Inferences and §6 Inferences B and G]
- Derived, time zones and travel: a zone change makes local-clock anchors jump, which BOCPD, the HMM and the CUSUM would read as a regime change; treat it as a known event, suppress switch prompts and change notices, and hold anchor updates until the new zone has persisted 3 or more days or the person confirms. [Not in source; derived from §5 Inferences and §6 Inferences G]
- Derived, travel and doses: dose reminders are user-set hard rules, so the app must not re-time them or advise on dose timing in a new zone; it asks the person to confirm reminder times. [Not in source; derived from §4 Inferences and §9 Inferences]
- Derived, DST: compute anchors and hour-of-day features in local wall-clock time so a DST change does not look like a 1 h wake shift; on 23 h and 25 h days, dose-relative curves use elapsed time. [Not in source; derived from §3 Inferences and §4 Inferences]
- Derived, shift work: the main sleep episode may be daytime and a dose may fall after midnight, so daily series should key off sleep-wake days rather than calendar dates; hours since wake stays valid, hour-of-day harmonics may not; rotating patterns need a lifestyle or weekday type each within the cap of about 4. [Not in source; derived from §3 Inferences, §6 Inferences A and F, §7 Inferences]
- Derived, unmedicated days: without an explicit "no dose today" log, an unmedicated day is indistinguishable from a missing dose log. [Not in source; derived from §10 Takeaway and §4 Inferences]

## 12. Open questions / dependencies

- Day types vs lifestyles: the source models multi-day, user-toggled regimes (capped at about 4) with weekday types inside; the product's examples mix both ("uni day" is per-day, "school holidays" is a regime, "shift work" either); decide the mapping and whether weekday types may be auto-assigned while lifestyles never auto-switch. [§6 Takeaway, Inferences A, F and G]
- Lifestyle cap of about 4 vs the number of day types the product wants to offer. [§6 Inferences F]
- Maturity ladder conflict: §1 has early (n ≥ 3), learned (n ≥ 8), confident (80% interval < ×1.5); §9 has prior (n < 3), early (3 to 7), learned (n ≥ 8 and interval < ×1.5); pick one label set. [§1 Inferences; §9 Inferences]
- Holiday wake shift conflict when free-day data are sparse: +50 to 60 min (§6 D) vs +50 to 90 min (§7); 60 min satisfies both. [§6 Inferences D; §7 Inferences]
- λ = 0.90 for 14 days is written under first activation but says "an L2 episode"; confirm whether it also applies on recurring activation. [§6 Inferences D and E; §7 Inferences]
- Grid bounds: t_off floor of 2 h vs IR methylphenidate prior range from 1.5 h and reports as short as 1 h; lower the floor or accept truncation. [§4 Cited Findings and Inferences]
- How the formulation priors become grid weights (shape of "t_on 20 to 60 min", "mode 3.5 h, range 1.5 to 5 h") and the exact fixed r and f per formulation ("e.g., 30 to 60 min"). [§4 Inferences]
- Whether grid cells with t_off < t_on + r + f are excluded (not stated). [§4 Inferences]
- How the "Partly" answer enters a binary likelihood (half-weight, ordinal model, or other). [§4 Takeaway and Inferences]
- Whether lapse rates ε₀ and ε₁ stay fixed at 0.1 or are learned. [§4 Inferences]
- What happens if both 80% CIs are still wider than ±30 min after 7 medicated days (extend the learning phase?). [§4 Inferences]
- How many binary check-ins recover t_on and t_off within ±30 min is unknown; needs simulation. [§4 Gaps]
- Check-in spacing rules and whether check-ins pass the same availability gate are unspecified. [§4 Inferences; §3 Inferences]
